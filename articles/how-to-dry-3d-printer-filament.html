How to Dry 3D Printer Filament (4 Methods That Actually Work)

📅 May 7, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🌡️

Why Wet Filament Ruins Your Prints

Most filaments absorb moisture from the air — especially PLA, PETG, and nylon. Even a few days in a humid room can make your spool print poorly. You'll hear popping sounds from the nozzle, see stringing that wasn't there before, and notice weak layer adhesion on parts that used to be solid. The problem isn't your printer settings. It's the water inside the plastic.

When filament contains moisture, that water turns to steam in the hot end. It expands violently, causing bubbles, under-extrusion, and that characteristic crackling noise. The fix is simple: dry your filament before printing. This guide covers four methods that work, from free DIY approaches to dedicated dryers worth the investment.

How to Tell If Your Filament Is Wet

Before you dry anything, confirm moisture is actually the problem. Here are the signs:

  • Popping or crackling from the nozzle during extrusion (most obvious sign)
  • Stringing that gets worse over time, even with good retraction settings
  • Bubbles or rough surface on what should be smooth walls
  • Poor layer adhesion — parts snap apart easier than they should
  • Steam or hissing visible at the nozzle tip when extruding

If you're seeing these issues and your settings haven't changed, moisture is the prime suspect. Time to dry that spool.

Method 1: Use Your Kitchen Oven

The oven method is free and works well for PLA and PETG. But you need to be careful — too hot and you'll deform the filament or melt the spool.

Temperature and time:

  • PLA: 45–50°C (113–122°F) for 4–6 hours
  • PETG: 60–65°C (140–149°F) for 4–6 hours
  • ABS: 70–75°C (158–167°F) for 4 hours
  • Nylon: 70–80°C (158–176°F) for 6–8 hours

Steps:

  1. Preheat your oven to the target temperature. Use an oven thermometer if you don't trust the dial — accuracy matters here.
  2. Place the spool on the center rack, laying flat. If the spool is plastic, check the temperature rating first. Many spools deform above 60°C.
  3. Prop the oven door open slightly (a wooden spoon works) to let moisture escape.
  4. Check after 2 hours. If the spool looks warped or the filament is sticking together, stop — your oven runs too hot.
  5. After drying, let the spool cool completely in a dry place before printing.

Warning: Never dry filament in an oven you use for food. The plastics can leave residues. If a food-safe oven is your only option, run a cleaning cycle afterward.

Method 2: Food Dehydrator (Best Budget Option)

A food dehydrator is better than an oven for filament drying. The temperature is more stable, airflow is built-in, and you won't risk melting spools. You can find used ones cheap at thrift stores or buy new for $30–50.

Setup:

  1. Remove the spool from its packaging and place it on a dehydrator tray. If the center hole is too small for the tray, rest the spool on its edge between trays.
  2. Set the temperature: 45°C for PLA, 60°C for PETG, 70°C for ABS/nylon.
  3. Run for 4–6 hours. No need to prop anything open — dehydrators are designed to move moist air out.
  4. Store the dried spool in a sealed bag with desiccant packets immediately after.

This method works great for most users. The only downside is capacity — most dehydrators fit one spool at a time.

Method 3: DIY Heated Box

If you have a spare cardboard box, a light bulb, and a small fan, you can build a filament dryer in 30 minutes. This approach scales well — you can dry multiple spools at once.

What you need:

  • Cardboard box or plastic storage container
  • 40–60W incandescent light bulb (old-school, not LED)
  • Small computer fan (12V, powered by USB or adapter)
  • Thermometer or temperature probe
  • Desiccant packets (optional, for extra drying power)

Build steps:

  1. Cut holes in opposite sides of the box — one for the fan (intake), one for exhaust.
  2. Mount the light bulb in the center, away from the cardboard walls. Use a ceramic socket if you have one.
  3. Place the fan to blow air across the bulb and over the spools.
  4. Add a thermometer probe near where the filament sits.
  5. Test run: turn on the bulb and fan, wait 15 minutes, check the temperature. Adjust bulb wattage or vent size until you hit 45–60°C.
  6. Load your spools and run for 4–6 hours.

The light bulb provides heat; the fan circulates air and carries moisture away. It's not pretty, but it works — and costs almost nothing if you have the parts lying around.

Method 4: Dedicated Filament Dryer (Most Convenient)

If you print regularly with hygroscopic materials (PETG, nylon, TPU), a dedicated filament dryer is worth the investment. These devices maintain precise temperature, include built-in fans, and some even let you print directly from the dryer.

Popular options:

  • SUNLU S2 Filament Dryer — ~$35, fits one spool, adjustable temp (35–70°C), 4-hour timer
  • PrintDry Filament Dryer — ~$60, better temperature control, runs up to 24 hours
  • Polymaker PolyDryer — ~$80, premium build, can print while drying, includes desiccant

The advantage of these units is consistency. Set it and forget it — no checking temperatures, no worrying about melting spools. For under $50, the SUNLU S2 is hard to beat for most home users.

How Long Does Dried Filament Stay Dry?

Not long, if you leave it out. PLA can absorb enough moisture to cause problems in 12–24 hours of humid air. PETG is even faster — a few hours in a damp room and you're back to popping and stringing.

Storage rules:

  • Keep dried filament in a sealed bag or airtight container with desiccant packets
  • Change desiccant when it changes color (most turn from blue to pink when saturated)
  • For frequent printing, consider a dry box that feeds directly to your printer
  • Store spools in a cool, dry place — not a garage or basement

If you're pulling filament from storage and it's been more than a week, do a quick test print before committing to a long job. Better to catch moisture early than waste hours on a failed print.

Quick Reference: Drying by Material

Material Temp Time Moisture Sensitivity
PLA 45–50°C 4–6 hours Medium
PETG 60–65°C 4–6 hours High
ABS 70–75°C 4 hours Low–Medium
Nylon 70–80°C 6–8 hours Very High
TPU 50–55°C 4–6 hours Medium–High

Conclusion

Wet filament is one of the most common causes of print failures that beginners blame on "bad settings" or "cheap filament." Before you tweak retraction or lower your temperature, check if your spool has been sitting out. A quick dry — whether in an oven, dehydrator, DIY box, or dedicated dryer — can restore print quality in a few hours. And once you've dried a spool, store it right. Prevention beats rescue every time.

For most users, a food dehydrator is the best balance of cost, safety, and effectiveness. If you print a lot with PETG or nylon, a dedicated filament dryer pays for itself in saved prints within a few months.
