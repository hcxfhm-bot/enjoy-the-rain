PLA+ vs Regular PLA: Which Filament Should You Choose?

📅 May 25, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
⚖️

What's the Difference Between PLA+ and Regular PLA?

If you've shopped for 3D printer filament, you've seen PLA+ (sometimes called PLA Plus, PLA Pro, or Tough PLA) alongside regular PLA. Both are beginner-friendly materials, but PLA+ claims improved strength and toughness. Is it worth the extra cost, or is it just marketing? Let's break down the real differences.

PLA (Polylactic Acid) is the most popular 3D printing filament for good reason: it's easy to print, affordable, and produces good surface finish. However, it's brittle compared to other materials — drop a PLA print and it might crack or shatter.

PLA+ is a modified version of PLA with additives that improve its mechanical properties. Different manufacturers use different formulations, but the goal is always the same: keep PLA's printability while adding toughness. The result is a filament that's still easy to print but produces stronger, more durable parts.

Strength Comparison

The main selling point of PLA+ is increased strength and toughness. But what does that actually mean in practice?

Tensile Strength

Regular PLA has a tensile strength around 50-60 MPa. PLA+ typically tests at 55-70 MPa — a modest improvement, but not dramatic. Where PLA+ really shines is in impact resistance and layer adhesion.

Impact Resistance

This is where PLA+ makes the biggest difference. Regular PLA is brittle; it tends to crack or shatter under impact. PLA+ formulations are significantly more impact-resistant, meaning prints can survive drops and handling better. If you're printing functional parts like phone cases, drone frames, or mechanical components, PLA+ is worth considering.

Layer Adhesion

PLA+ generally has better layer bonding than regular PLA. This means prints are less likely to delaminate under stress. For parts that will be under load, this improved layer adhesion translates to real-world strength gains.

Temperature Resistance

Both PLA and PLA+ have similar temperature limitations. They start to soften around 50-55°C, making them unsuitable for parts that will be in hot cars, near electronics that run warm, or in any environment above room temperature.

Some "High Temperature PLA" or "HTPLA" variants exist that can withstand higher temperatures after annealing, but standard PLA+ doesn't significantly improve heat resistance. If temperature resistance is your priority, consider PETG or ABS instead.

Printability Comparison

One of PLA's biggest advantages is how easy it is to print. Does PLA+ sacrifice that convenience for strength?

Nozzle Temperature

Regular PLA prints well at 190-220°C. PLA+ typically needs slightly higher temperatures, around 200-230°C. The difference is small, and most printers handle both without issue.

Recommended temperatures:

  • Regular PLA: 195-210°C
  • PLA+: 205-225°C

Bed Temperature

Both materials work well with unheated beds, but a heated bed at 50-60°C improves adhesion. For more on bed temperatures, see our PLA temperature guide.

Bed Adhesion

PLA+ tends to have slightly better bed adhesion than regular PLA, likely due to its modified formulation. Both stick well to PEI, glass with glue stick, and blue painter's tape.

Warping

Neither material warps significantly. Both PLA and PLA+ are low-warp materials that work well on open-frame printers without enclosures. For more on warping issues, see our warping solutions guide.

Stringing

PLA+ can be slightly more prone to stringing than regular PLA due to its modified formula. However, this is easily controlled with proper retraction settings. If you're experiencing stringing, try increasing retraction distance slightly.

Surface Finish and Appearance

Regular PLA typically produces prints with a slightly glossy finish and good detail. PLA+ finishes vary by brand but generally have a more matte appearance. Some users find PLA+ hides layer lines better, giving a smoother overall look.

For parts that will be painted, both materials work well. Check our guide on how to paint 3D prints for best results.

Price Comparison

PLA+ costs more than regular PLA — typically 20-40% more per kilogram. Is the price premium worth it?

When to Choose Regular PLA

Regular PLA is the better choice when:

  • You're printing display models: Figurines, art pieces, and decorative items don't need extra strength.
  • Budget is a priority: If you print a lot and cost matters, regular PLA gives more prints per dollar.
  • You're learning: For beginners practicing and making mistakes, cheaper filament means lower cost to learn.
  • Surface finish matters: Regular PLA often has a slightly better glossy finish for show pieces.
  • Printing small details: Some PLA+ formulations can be slightly stringier; regular PLA handles fine details well.

When to Choose PLA+

PLA+ is worth the extra cost when:

  • Parts will be handled: Phone cases, drone frames, and handheld objects benefit from impact resistance.
  • Mechanical function is required: Gears, brackets, and structural parts need the improved layer adhesion.
  • Parts might be dropped: Kids' toys, outdoor items, and anything portable survives better in PLA+.
  • Layer strength matters: If your print has thin sections that might delaminate, PLA+ bonds better.
  • You want peace of mind: For important prints where failure isn't an option, the extra strength is insurance.

PLA+ Brand Variations

Not all PLA+ is created equal. Different brands use different formulations, and quality varies significantly. Some popular options include:

  • eSun PLA+: Very popular, good balance of price and performance, consistent quality
  • PolyTerra PLA: Excellent matte finish, good strength, eco-friendly packaging
  • Hatchbox PLA: Budget-friendly, decent strength improvement over basic PLA
  • Prusament PLA: Premium option, excellent consistency, very good mechanical properties
  • Polymaker PolyLite PLA Pro: High strength, excellent layer adhesion

Conclusion

The choice between PLA+ and regular PLA comes down to what you're printing. For display models and practice prints, regular PLA's lower price and slightly better finish make it the smart choice. For functional parts, mechanical components, and anything that needs to survive real-world use, PLA+'s improved strength and impact resistance are worth the extra cost.

Most 3D printing enthusiasts end up keeping both on hand: regular PLA for everyday printing and PLA+ for parts that matter. If you're just starting out, begin with quality regular PLA to learn the ropes, then add PLA+ when you start printing functional parts.
