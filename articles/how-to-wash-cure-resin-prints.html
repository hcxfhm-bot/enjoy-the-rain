How to Wash and Cure Resin Prints: Complete Guide

📅 May 26, 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
✨

You've just finished printing a stunning resin miniature, and it looks beautiful—but it's not finished yet. Post-processing is where your print transforms from a sticky, half-cured prototype into a durable, professional-looking finished product.

Washing and curing are the two critical steps that separate good resin prints from great ones. Get these steps right, and your prints will look like they came from a professional studio. Rush through them, and you'll end up with sticky, brittle, or discolored parts.

Why Washing and Curing Matter

After printing, your resin print is only partially cured. Uncured resin remains on the surface, which is:

  • Toxic: Raw resin causes skin irritation and is harmful if ingested or inhaled
  • Sticky: Uncured surfaces attract dust and never fully harden
  • Weaker: Parts that aren't fully cured lack structural integrity

Proper washing removes excess resin, while UV curing completes the polymerization process, making your prints safe, durable, and ready for handling or painting.

What You'll Need

Before you start, gather these essentials:

  • Isopropyl alcohol (IPA): 91% or higher concentration works best
  • Wash basin or wash-and-cure station: Dedicated stations like Elegoo Mercury X or Anycubic Wash and Cure make the job much easier
  • UV light source: Either a curing station or natural sunlight
  • Protective gloves: Nitrile gloves are essential
  • Paper towels: For drying prints
  • Tongs or silicone grippers: To handle prints without touching uncured resin

Step 1: Removing Your Print from the Build Plate

First, carefully remove your print from the build plate. For FEP or Foil tanks, let the print soak in the tank for a few minutes first—this loosens the print and makes removal easier.

Use a plastic scraper or the included tools that came with your printer. Never use metal tools, as they can puncture your FEP film.

Pro Tip:

Print a removal jig or use silicone gloves for better grip. This prevents accidentally bending or breaking delicate parts.

Step 2: Washing Your Resin Print

Method 1: Manual Washing

For manual washing, submerge your print in a container of IPA:

  1. Fill a container with 91%+ isopropyl alcohol
  2. Submerge the print completely for 1-2 minutes
  3. Gently agitation the print to loosen uncured resin
  4. Remove and inspect for any remaining residue
  5. Repeat if necessary, especially for hollow prints or complex geometries
  6. Rinse briefly in fresh IPA for the final wash

Method 2: Wash and Cure Station

Dedicated wash stations like the Elegoo Mercury Plus offer several advantages:

  • Motorized agitation for thorough cleaning
  • Larger capacity for multiple prints
  • Integrated curing chamber
  • Less IPA evaporation between uses

Simply place your prints in the washing basket, set the timer (typically 2-4 minutes), and let the machine do the work.

How Long to Wash?

The washing time depends on your print complexity:

  • Simple prints: 1-2 minutes
  • Standard prints: 2-3 minutes
  • Hollow prints or complex geometries: 3-5 minutes total (change IPA once)

Step 3: Drying Your Print

Before curing, your print must be completely dry. Water or IPA trapped in crevices will cause cloudiness or prevent proper curing.

Air drying: Place prints on a paper towel in a well-ventilated area for 15-30 minutes.

Compressed air: For faster drying, use canned air or a compressor to blow IPA out of cavities and holes.

Hair dryer: A cool setting can speed up drying, but never use heat as it can deform certain resins.

Signs Your Print is Dry:

  • No visible liquid or pooling
  • Surface no longer feels slick or oily
  • No IPA smell (though some odor may linger)

Step 4: UV Curing Your Print

Curing completes the polymerization process. Without it, your prints remain brittle and potentially toxic.

Using a Curing Station

  1. Place your dried print on the rotating platform
  2. Set the timer based on resin type and print size
  3. Most standard resins need 2-5 minutes per side
  4. Rotate prints halfway through for even exposure
  5. Check the cure—prints should feel hard and not tacky

Curing Times by Resin Type

  • Standard resin: 4-6 minutes total (2-3 minutes per side)
  • Flexible resin: 3-4 minutes total (shorter to prevent melting)
  • Dental/castable resin: 2-4 minutes (can be more brittle if over-cured)
  • Tough/engineering resin: 6-8 minutes total

Sun Curing (Natural UV)

You can cure prints in direct sunlight, but be aware:

  • Time required: 15-30 minutes in strong sunlight
  • Risks: Inconsistent curing, dust accumulation, overheating
  • Best practice: Use for rough cure only, then finish in a UV station

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Under-Washing

Leftover uncured resin remains sticky and toxic. When in doubt, wash longer—IPA won't damage your print, but uncured resin will ruin it.

2. Over-Curing

Too much UV exposure can cause:

  • Yellowing or discoloration
  • Brittleness and cracking
  • Warping of thin sections

3. Rushing the Dry Time

Moisture trapped under the surface will cause cloudiness and prevent proper curing. Patience here saves you from reprints.

4. Touching Uncured Resin

Always wear gloves when handling uncured prints. Skin contact can cause irritation, and oils from your fingers can interfere with curing.

Water-Washable Resins

A newer category of resin uses water instead of IPA for washing. These offer:

  • No IPA purchase or storage required
  • More environmentally friendly disposal
  • Less odor during washing

However, water-washable resins still require UV curing, and prints must be completely dry before curing—water trapped in the print can cause issues.

Troubleshooting Post-Processing Issues

Print is Still Tacky After Curing

Increase cure time by 1-2 minutes. If still tacky, your UV light may be too weak or the resin may be expired.

Cloudy or Frosted Appearance

This usually means moisture got trapped during curing. Re-wash, dry thoroughly, and cure again.

Yellowing

Caused by over-curing or exposure to UV light over time. Use a UV-resistant topcoat for displayed prints.

Conclusion

Washing and curing might seem like extra steps, but they're essential for producing safe, durable, and beautiful resin prints. Invest in a quality wash-and-cure station, follow these guidelines, and you'll consistently achieve professional results.
