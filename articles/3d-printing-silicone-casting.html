3D Printing for Silicone Casting: Molds, Masters & Techniques

📅 June 1, 2026⏱️ 7 min read✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🫧

Silicone casting with 3D printed molds combines the design freedom of 3D printing with the unique properties of silicone — flexibility, food safety, skin safety, and temperature resistance. Here's how to do it.

Why Cast Silicone?

  • Flexible parts — Gaskets, grips, custom phone cases
  • Food-safe items — Ice cube trays, chocolate molds, baking tools
  • Skin-contact safe — Prosthetics, cosplay contacts, wearables
  • Heat resistant — Up to 230°C for platinum-cure silicone

Two Approaches

ApproachProcessBest For
3D printed moldPrint a negative mold, pour silicone inSimple shapes, single-use molds
3D printed master + silicone moldPrint a positive, pour silicone around it, remove masterComplex shapes, reusable molds

Silicone Types

  • Platinum-cure (addition) — Food safe, skin safe, no shrinkage, most expensive
  • Tin-cure (condensation) — Cheaper, slight shrinkage, NOT food/skin safe

Critical: Mold Release

Silicone sticks to everything — except itself. Without mold release, your silicone casting will fuse with the printed mold permanently. Apply 2-3 coats of mold release spray to the printed mold before pouring silicone.
