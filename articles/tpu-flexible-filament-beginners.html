TPU Flexible Filament: A Beginner's Guide to Printing Soft Materials

📅 May 5, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🧽

What Makes TPU Different from PLA?

TPU (thermoplastic polyurethane) is soft and bendy. PLA is rigid. That difference changes everything about how you print it — you can't push it through a hot end the same way. TPU is abrasive (the hardness damages brass nozzles over time) and it squishes against the feed gear. But the results — flexible parts, phone cases, shoe soles — are worth the learning curve.

The Real Challenge: TPU Doesn't Like to Be Rushed

The number one mistake beginners make is printing TPU too fast. TPU needs time to soften and flow. Print at 15–30mm/s maximum for the first layer, then 20–40mm/s for the rest. Yes, that's slow. Yes, it's worth it. Going faster turns your filament into a jammed mess and ruins your prints.

Temperature Settings for TPU

220–250°C for most TPU brands. Start at 230°C and go up in 5°C steps if you see under-extrusion. The bed should be 40–60°C — you don't need high bed temps for TPU because it doesn't warp. A PEI sheet or BuildTak surface works great.

Your Nozzle Matters More Than You Think

Brass nozzles wear out quickly with TPU due to its abrasiveness. Consider upgrading to hardened steel or ruby-tipped nozzles if you print TPU regularly. Also: use a nozzle with a larger diameter (0.6mm or 0.8mm) to reduce clog risk. Standard 0.4mm works but clogs more often.

Direct Drive vs Bowden: Which Works Better for TPU?

Direct drive extruders (like those on Bambu Lab, Prusa, or Voron printers) are much better for TPU. The short distance between the gear and hot end means less flex and better control. If you're on a Bowden setup (like stock Ender 3), consider upgrading to a direct drive conversion kit or printing at even slower speeds (15–20mm/s) to compensate.

Slicer Settings That Actually Help

Reduce print speed to 20–30mm/s. Disable or minimize retraction (TPU strings less but retracting can cause clogs). Lower layer height to 0.12–0.16mm for better adhesion. Turn cooling fan on but keep it below 50% — too much cooling causes layer adhesion problems in TPU. Disable "avoid crossing walls" in your slicer to reduce stringing.

Common TPU Problems and Fixes

Jammed extruder → slow down, raise temperature by 5°C, check nozzle for debris. Stringing → reduce temperature, enable coasting in slicer. Layer delamination → increase temperature slightly, reduce fan speed, slow down. Feeding issues / clicking → loosen extruder tension, switch to direct drive.

What Can You Actually Print with TPU?

Phone cases, watch bands, flexible hinges, shoe insoles, gaskets, cable covers, RC car tires, soft toys. TPU fills a gap that rigid filaments can't. It's not for everything — don't try printing structural parts with it. But for functional flexible parts, TPU is your go-to. Brands like eSUN make solid eSUN PLA+ for rigid prints when you need them.

Bottom Line

TPU is slower and trickier than PLA, but nothing else gives you the same results for flexible prints. Slow down, print at 230–245°C, and if you're on Bowden, seriously consider a direct drive upgrade. The $20 investment in a BMG extruder kit pays for itself the first time you avoid a 4-hour failed print.
