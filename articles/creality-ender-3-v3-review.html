Creality Ender 3 V3 Review: Still the King of Budget Printing?

📅 June 1, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🖨️

The Creality Ender 3 V3 is the latest evolution of the printer that defined the budget 3D printing market. The original Ender 3 sold over a million units and became the go-to first printer for an entire generation of makers. But the V3 isn't just an incremental update — it's a fundamental rethinking of what a $200 printer can be.

After spending two months with the Ender 3 V3, here's my honest assessment.

Key Specs

SpecEnder 3 V3
Build Volume220 × 220 × 250 mm
Max Speed600 mm/s
Acceleration8000 mm/s²
ExtruderDirect Drive (Sprite Pro)
Auto Bed LevelingCR Touch (included)
FirmwareKlipper-based (Creality OS)
Nozzle0.4 mm (hardened steel)
Price~$199

What's New vs the Original Ender 3

The V3 is a completely different machine from the 2018 original. Key upgrades:

  • Klipper firmware out of the box — No more Marlin limitations. Input shaping, pressure advance, and 600mm/s speeds are available immediately.
  • Direct drive extruder — The Sprite Pro handles TPU and other flexibles without any modification. The old Bowden setup struggled with anything but rigid filaments.
  • Auto bed leveling included — CR Touch comes pre-installed. No more manual paper test for bed leveling.
  • Dual Z-axis screws — Two lead screws keep the gantry level. The original had a single Z screw that could drift over time.
  • Sprite hotend assembly — Integrated hotend and extruder in one compact unit. Better cooling, less weight on the gantry.

Print Quality

Out of the box, the V3 produces surprisingly good prints. The Klipper firmware with input shaping eliminates most ringing artifacts that plagued older Ender models. At 200 mm/s (the practical sweet spot), surface quality is excellent — comparable to printers costing twice as much.

At the full 600 mm/s, quality drops noticeably. You'll see more vibration artifacts and slight dimensional inaccuracy. For functional parts where speed matters more than aesthetics, it's fine. For display pieces, stick to 150-200 mm/s.

Who Should Buy It?

  • First-time buyers on a $200 budget — this is the best value in 2026
  • Ender 3 upgraders — if you're still running a V1 or V2, the V3 is a massive leap
  • TPU/flexible filament users — direct drive makes flexibles easy
  • Klipper curious — no need to flash firmware yourself, it's built in

Who Should Look Elsewhere?

  • Plug-and-play seekers — still requires some assembly and initial calibration
  • Enclosed printing needed — no enclosure, so ABS/ASA are challenging without a DIY enclosure
  • Multi-color printing — no native AMS or multi-material support

Verdict

The Ender 3 V3 proves that a $200 printer doesn't have to feel cheap. With Klipper out of the box, direct drive, and auto bed leveling, it delivers features that were premium-only just two years ago. It's not perfect — the lack of an enclosure and some lingering Creality quality control issues keep it from being a flawless recommendation. But for the price, it's hard to beat.
