How to Sand 3D Prints: The Complete Guide to Smooth Finishes

📅 June 1, 2026⏱️ 7 min read✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🧹

Sanding is the most reliable way to get smooth, professional-looking 3D prints. Whether you're removing layer lines from an FDM print or cleaning up a resin model, the right sanding technique can transform a rough print into something that looks factory-made.

Why Sand Your Prints?

  • Remove layer lines — Eliminate the "3D printed" look
  • Prepare for painting — Smooth surfaces take paint better
  • Fit parts together — Tighten tolerances for mechanical assemblies
  • Mirror finish — With enough sanding, PLA can look like polished acrylic

Sanding Grit Progression

The key is starting coarse and working your way up. Never skip more than 2 grit levels at a time:

StageGritPurpose
Rough120-220Remove major layer lines and imperfections
Medium320-400Smooth out sanding marks from rough stage
Fine600-800Pre-paint smoothness
Very Fine1000-2000Glossy / mirror finish

Sanding by Material

PLA

PLA sands easily but melts under friction heat. Use wet sanding (dip sandpaper in water) to prevent melting. Work in light, quick strokes rather than heavy pressure.

PETG

PETG is more flexible than PLA, making it harder to sand flat. It tends to gum up rather than dust. Use sharp sandpaper and replace it frequently. Wet sanding helps.

Resin

Cured resin sands beautifully — almost like soft wood. Start at 220 and work up. Always wear a mask; resin dust is harmful to inhale.

Pro Tips

  • Use sanding sponges for curved surfaces — they conform to shapes better than flat paper
  • Spray primer between stages — it reveals low spots you'd otherwise miss
  • Automated sanding — Dremel with sanding drums for large flat areas (but be careful not to over-sand)
  • Fill gaps first — Use automotive spot putty or epoxy to fill layer gaps before sanding
