How to Fix Ghosting/Ringing in 3D Prints: Speed & Acceleration

📅 June 1, 2026⏱️ 7 min read✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
👻

Ghosting (also called ringing or echoing) shows up as faint duplicate shadows of features like holes and corners on your print surface. It's caused by vibrations that aren't fully damped — the printer's frame resonates after a direction change, and those vibrations get recorded in the print.

What Ghosting Looks Like

After every sharp feature (hole, corner, text), you'll see a faint "echo" repeated at regular intervals. The spacing of the echoes corresponds to the vibration frequency of your printer's frame.

Fixes Ranked by Effectiveness

1. Enable Input Shaping (Klipper) ⭐ Best Fix

Input shaping is by far the most effective fix. It pre-compensates for the printer's resonant frequencies in the G-code, canceling out vibrations before they happen.

  • Install an ADXL345 accelerometer on your print head
  • Run SHAPER_CALIBRATE in Klipper
  • Save the recommended shaper type and frequency

This alone can eliminate 80-90% of ringing.

2. Reduce Acceleration

Lower acceleration means less violent direction changes, which means less vibration. Try halving your current acceleration and see if ghosting improves.

3. Reduce Print Speed

Particularly the outer wall speed. 30-40 mm/s outer walls produce much less ghosting than 80-100 mm/s.

4. Mechanical Fixes

  • Tighten all frame bolts
  • Add frame braces or stiffening plates
  • Ensure the printer is on a solid, heavy surface (not a wobbly table)
  • Install vibration dampening feet
