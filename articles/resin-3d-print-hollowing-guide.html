How to Hollow Resin Prints: Save Resin & Avoid Suction Cups

📅 June 1, 2026⏱️ 7 min read✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🫙

Hollowing resin prints can save 50-70% on resin costs and reduce print time significantly. But if you don't add drain holes, the hollow cavity creates a suction cup effect that can rip the print off the supports or cause it to implode during curing. Here's how to do it right.

Hollowing Workflow

  1. Import model into slicer (Lychee, Chitubox, PrusaSlicer)
  2. Enable hollowing — Set wall thickness to 2-3mm
  3. Add drain holes — Minimum 3mm diameter, at lowest point of each cavity
  4. Add a second hole at the top for air flow
  5. Check supports — Hollow parts are lighter and may need fewer supports

Critical: Avoid Suction Cups

A suction cup forms when a hollow cavity has a flat surface facing the build plate with no drain hole. As the print peels from the FEP film, the vacuum pulls the print apart. Rule of thumb: every enclosed cavity must have at least 2 holes — one at the bottom (drain) and one at the top (vent).

Post-Processing Hollow Prints

  • Flush IPA through drain holes to clean the interior
  • Cure the exterior first, then use a UV flashlight inside the holes
  • Fill drain holes with putty or UV resin if you want a seamless surface
