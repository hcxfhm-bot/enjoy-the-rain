Best Shelly Smart Home Devices in 2026: Complete Buyer's Guide

📅 July 12, 2026 ⏱️ 11 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🔌

If you've been building out a smart home and keep running into the same wall — devices that require a cloud subscription, hubs that only work with their own ecosystem, or switches that don't fit in a standard US or EU gang box — you haven't discovered Shelly yet.

Shelly is a Bulgarian smart home manufacturer that has quietly become one of the most beloved names in the DIY smart home community. Their devices are compact, affordable, run local by default, and work beautifully with Home Assistant, ESPHome, and even Tasmota firmware. Whether you want to automate your lights, monitor energy usage, or retrofit an old water heater, Shelly has a module for that.

In this guide, we'll cover the best Shelly smart home devices in 2026, compare the key product lines (Shelly Plus vs Shelly Pro vs Shelly Plus Addon), and show you exactly how to integrate them with your existing smart home setup.

Why Shelly Devices Are Different

Most consumer smart switches come with a significant catch: they only work through a proprietary cloud. Lose your internet connection, and your "smart" switch becomes a dumb one. Shelly flips this model on its head.

Every Shelly device ships with a built-in web interface. You connect to it directly over Wi-Fi (or Ethernet, on Pro models), configure it, and it works — locally, with or without internet access. That's a huge advantage for:

  • Privacy-conscious users — your data never touches a cloud server you don't control
  • Reliability — automations run on the device itself, not in the cloud
  • Flexibility — MQTT, CoIoT, REST API, and even custom firmware options give you total control
  • Retrofitting — Shelly's tiny form factor means most modules fit inside standard wall boxes behind your existing switch

The Sonoff devices we covered previously are excellent, but Shelly fills a complementary niche: devices that are smaller, more power-efficient, and often come with extra features like energy monitoring built in.

Shelly Product Lines Explained

Before diving into specific devices, let's clear up the naming confusion. Shelly sells three main lines:

Shelly Plus (Wi-Fi)

The Plus line is Shelly's mid-range. These are Wi-Fi devices with a faster processor than the original Shelly 1, support for scripting, and native integration with Home Assistant via CoIoT. They're the sweet spot for most DIY smart home enthusiasts. If you're just starting out, the Plus line is where you should focus.

Shelly Pro (Wi-Fi + Ethernet)

The Pro line adds Ethernet connectivity, making them ideal for permanent installations where Wi-Fi might be unreliable. Pro models also support power measurement, dual-switch inputs, and come in a DIN-rail mountable form factor — perfect for electrical panels. If you're wiring a new build or want the most stable setup, go Pro.

Shelly Plus Addon

The Plus Addon is a companion board that connects to a Shelly Plus device to add extra inputs and outputs — temperature sensors, door/window sensors, analog inputs, and more. Think of it as an expansion header for advanced projects.

Best Shelly Devices in 2026

1. Shelly Plus 1 — The Best All-Around Smart Switch

If you only buy one Shelly device, make it the Shelly Plus 1. This tiny Wi-Fi switch module can control a single circuit (lights, fans, garage doors, water heaters — anything under 16A). It fits inside a standard wall switch box, requires a neutral wire, and offers:

  • Wi-Fi connectivity with local control
  • Power monitoring (current, voltage, power)
  • Scripting support for custom automations
  • Integration with Home Assistant, Google Home, Alexa, and MQTT
  • Over-the-air firmware updates

Pair it with a 3D-printed enclosure if you need to mount it outside a wall box. The compact design makes it incredibly versatile for DIY projects, from automating a garden fountain to retrofitting an old ceiling fan.

2. Shelly Plus 1PM — Power Monitoring in the Same Size

Identical in form factor to the Plus 1, but with real-time power monitoring built in. This is the device you want for:

  • Tracking energy consumption of specific appliances
  • Setting up automations based on power draw (e.g., "turn off the space heater when it draws less than 50W")
  • Monitoring a device's health — a fridge or freezer that starts drawing unusual power can signal a problem

Cost is only slightly higher than the Plus 1, making the 1PM the better default choice unless you're certain you don't need power monitoring. If you're building an energy monitoring system with Tasmota, these devices slot right in alongside your Sonoff POW units.

3. Shelly Plus 2PM — Two Channels, One Device

When you need to control two circuits from a single module — say, a double light switch or a ceiling fan with a light — the Shelly Plus 2PM is your answer. It supports two independent channels, each with power monitoring, and can be configured as:

  • Switch mode — control two separate circuits
  • Light mode — control two lights with one physical switch (in dual-switch setups)
  • Decoupled mode — the physical switch and smart control operate independently

It's also an excellent choice for automating plant care systems where you want to control a pump and a grow light from one module.

4. Shelly Pro 4PM — Four Channels for Heavy Duty

The Shelly Pro 4PM is a DIN-rail mountable, four-channel relay with Ethernet connectivity. This is the device for serious installations:

  • Electrical panel installations (HVAC control, water heater, EV charger)
  • Commercial or rental property automation where reliability is critical
  • Anyone who wants zero Wi-Fi dependency

Each channel handles up to 16A, and you get power monitoring on all four channels. The Ethernet port means you can place it far from your Wi-Fi router without signal issues. If you're building a whole-home security system with ESP32 and Home Assistant, this is the backbone you want.

5. Shelly Plus RGBW — Smart LED Control

For lighting enthusiasts, the Shelly Plus RGBW controls up to four LED channels (R, G, B, W) or four white-only strips. It supports:

  • RGB and RGBW LED strips
  • White-only LED strips (4 channels of white at different color temperatures)
  • Direct integration with Home Assistant for color and brightness control
  • Scheduling and scene automation

Pair it with WS2812B LED strips controlled by ESP32 for a separate project, or use the Shelly RGBW for simpler single-color zoning. Either way, automating your lighting is one of the highest-impact smart home upgrades you can make.

6. Shelly Plus H&T — Temperature & Humidity Sensor

The Shelly Plus H&T is Shelly's first battery-powered device in the Plus line. It measures temperature and humidity with a reported battery life of up to 16 months. Data is pushed over Wi-Fi to Home Assistant via MQTT or CoIoT, making it ideal for:

  • Monitoring bathroom humidity (trigger an exhaust fan when it exceeds 70%)
  • Tracking basement moisture levels
  • Climate monitoring for a wine cellar or greenhouse
  • General indoor air quality awareness

For a more hands-on approach, you can also build your own sensor with ESP32 and BME280 using ESPHome — it won't have a battery, but it costs less and gives you more flexibility.

Shelly Plus vs Shelly Pro: Which Should You Buy?

The decision between Plus and Pro comes down to your installation context:

Feature Shelly Plus Shelly Pro
Connectivity Wi-Fi only Wi-Fi + Ethernet
Power monitoring PM models only All models
Mounting Inside wall box DIN rail or wall box
Price $15–$25 $30–$55
Best for Retrofitting existing switches Permanent installs, panel mounting

For most home users retrofitting existing wall switches, the Plus line is the clear winner — it fits where a standard switch fits, costs less, and has all the smarts you need. If you're wiring a new construction or want rock-solid reliability without Wi-Fi dependencies, the Pro line is worth the extra investment.

How to Connect Shelly to Home Assistant

Setting up Shelly devices with Home Assistant is straightforward. There are two methods:

Method 1: Shelly Integration (Recommended for Beginners)

Home Assistant has a native Shelly integration. Just add the Shelly integration, and it will automatically discover your devices on the same network via CoIoT. Each device appears as entities for switches, sensors, and power readings. No configuration files needed.

Method 2: MQTT (Recommended for Power Users)

For more control, enable MQTT on each Shelly device and connect them to your Home Assistant MQTT broker. This gives you:

  • Custom entity naming and grouping
  • Advanced automations triggered by MQTT messages
  • Integration with other MQTT devices in one system
  • Offline resilience — MQTT can work on a local network without internet

If you're already running Home Assistant, check out our Home Assistant on Raspberry Pi setup guide for step-by-step instructions on getting your MQTT broker configured.

Can You Flash Tasmota or ESPHome on Shelly Devices?

Unlike Sonoff devices, Shelly devices run their own firmware which is already excellent — and open enough that most users never need an alternative. However, flashing is possible:

ESPHome on Shelly: Some community members have ported ESPHome to Shelly Plus devices, giving you the full power of ESPHome's configuration language. This is useful if you want to integrate non-Shelly sensors or use advanced ESPHome features not available in stock firmware.

Tasmota on Shelly: Tasmota is not officially supported on Shelly hardware due to architectural differences. The stock firmware is genuinely superior in this case — Shelly's scripting engine covers most use cases that would otherwise require Tasmota. See our Sonoff Tasmota vs ESPHome comparison for more on when each firmware shines.

3D Printing Custom Shelly Enclosures

One of the great things about Shelly's small form factor is that you can easily mount them in custom locations. If the device needs to live outside a wall box, design a simple 3D-printed enclosure:

  • Waterproof IP65-rated boxes for outdoor installations
  • Magnetic mount plates for temporary placement
  • Custom DIN rail adapters for electrical panel mounting

We've covered designing wall mounts for smart home devices in detail — the same principles apply to Shelly. Design for ventilation (these devices can run warm under load) and ensure any outdoor enclosure has adequate IP ratings.

Common Shelly Smart Home Setups

Setup 1: Smart Lighting Without Replacing Switches

Use a Shelly Plus 1 behind your existing dumb switch. The switch becomes a physical trigger for the smart module. You get voice control, app control, and automation — without changing your wall aesthetics.

Setup 2: Energy Monitoring Panel

Install a Shelly Pro 4PM in your electrical panel to monitor your whole home's energy consumption. Each channel tracks a different circuit group (HVAC, kitchen, garage). Combined with Home Assistant's energy dashboard, you get real-time and historical energy tracking — no cloud subscription required.

Setup 3: Climate-Controlled Grow Room

Combine a Shelly Plus 2PM (one channel for exhaust fans, one for grow lights) with a Shelly Plus H&T for humidity monitoring. Set up automations in Home Assistant: fans turn on above 60% RH, lights follow a schedule, and everything is monitored from one dashboard.

Where to Buy Shelly Devices

Shelly devices are widely available online. Key considerations:

  • Check regional variants — some Shelly devices come in US (110V), EU (230V), and UK versions. Buy the correct voltage for your region.
  • Verify neutral wire compatibility — most Shelly switches require a neutral wire in the switch box. Older US homes may not have one; in that case, look at the Shelly Dimmer or battery-powered options.
  • Buy from authorized sellers — Shelly fakes exist. Buy from Amazon, Shelly's official store, or recognized electronics distributors.

Final Verdict: Are Shelly Devices Worth It in 2026?

Absolutely. Shelly remains one of the best choices for DIY smart home enthusiasts in 2026. The combination of local-first operation, compact size, power monitoring on most models, and excellent Home Assistant integration makes them hard to beat at any price point.

The Shelly Plus 1PM is our top recommendation for most users — it's affordable, versatile, and has enough features to serve as the backbone of a complete smart home retrofit. For more demanding applications, the Pro line delivers rock-solid Ethernet connectivity and DIN-rail mounting that commercial installations require.

Whether you're replacing your light switches, monitoring your energy bill, or building a complex automation system, Shelly devices give you the reliability and control that cloud-only alternatives simply can't match.
