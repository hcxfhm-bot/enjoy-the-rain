3D Printed Wall Mounts for Smart Home Devices: Design Tips and 10 Models

📅 July 1, 2026 ⏱️ 9 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🏠

One of the most practical applications of a 3D printer in a smart home setup is creating custom wall mounts for your devices. Whether you need to mount an ESP32 temperature sensor, a smart speaker, or a touchscreen dashboard, 3D printing lets you create perfectly fitting brackets that commercial options simply can't match.

In this guide, we'll cover the essential design principles for creating strong, functional wall mounts, and share 10 ready-to-download models you can print today.

Why 3D Print Your Own Wall Mounts?

Before diving into designs, let's address why 3D printing your own mounts makes sense:

  • Perfect fit: Commercial mounts are designed for popular products. If you're using custom ESP32 builds or less common sensors, you won't find off-the-shelf solutions.
  • Customization: Need a specific angle, cable routing hole, or ventilation? You can design exactly what you need.
  • Cost savings: A single printed mount costs pennies in filament versus $10-30 for commercial options.
  • Rapid iteration: If the first design isn't quite right, you can tweak and reprint in an hour.

Essential Design Principles

When designing wall mounts for smart home devices, follow these key principles to ensure your prints are functional and durable:

1. Wall Attachment Method

Choose the right attachment method based on your wall type and how permanent you want the mount to be:

  • Screws and anchors: Best for drywall. Use #6 or #8 screws with appropriate anchors. Design 5mm diameter holes to accommodate standard screws.
  • Command strips: Great for renters or temporary setups. Design a flat back surface at least 50x50mm for good adhesive contact.
  • Magnetic mounts: Perfect for metal surfaces (refrigerators, electrical panels). Embed 10mm x 2mm neodymium magnets in your print.

2. Structural Strength

Smart home devices are typically light, but mounts still need to be strong enough to withstand accidental bumps and temperature changes:

  • Wall thickness: Use at least 2-3 perimeters (0.8-1.2mm) for structural parts.
  • Infill: 20-30% infill provides good strength without wasting filament.
  • Layer orientation: Print wall mounts vertically (standing up) so layer lines run perpendicular to the wall, maximizing strength.

For more on optimizing print strength, see our infill patterns guide.

3. Device Retention

How will your device stay in the mount? Consider these options:

  • Snap-fit: Flexible plastic clips that snap over the device. Great for devices you won't remove often. Design clips with 1-2mm thickness for PLA flexibility.
  • Friction fit: Slightly undersized holes that hold devices through tight tolerance. Works well for circuit boards.
  • Screw retention: Use small M2 or M3 screws to secure devices. Best for permanent installations.
  • Gravity + angle: Simple angled shelves where the device sits. Easiest to design but less secure.

4. Cable Management

Don't forget about power and data cables:

  • Include cable routing channels in your design (5-8mm wide for USB cables).
  • Add zip tie holes for securing cables.
  • Consider where the power cable exits relative to the wall.

5. Ventilation

Many smart home devices generate heat. If you're mounting something like an ESP32-CAM or a Raspberry Pi:

  • Include ventilation slots or holes in the back and sides.
  • Don't fully enclose devices that need airflow.
  • Consider convection: hot air rises, so place intake vents low and exhaust vents high.

Design Software Recommendations

For creating custom wall mounts, these free tools work well:

  • Tinkercad: Best for beginners. Great for simple brackets and combining basic shapes. Our Tinkercad tutorial gets you started in 30 minutes.
  • FreeCAD: Better for parametric designs where you want to easily adjust dimensions. See our FreeCAD guide.
  • Fusion 360: Free for personal use. Best for complex designs with moving parts. Full tutorial here.

10 Ready-to-Print Wall Mount Models

Here are 10 practical wall mounts you can download and print for common smart home devices:

1. ESP32/ESP8266 Dev Board Wall Mount

A simple snap-fit mount for standard ESP32 and ESP8266 dev boards (30mm x 50mm). Includes cable routing channel and Command strip back. Perfect for temperature/humidity sensors running ESPHome or Tasmota.

Print settings: PLA, 0.2mm layer height, 20% infill, no supports needed.

2. ESP32-CAM Security Camera Mount

Adjustable angle mount for ESP32-CAM modules. Features a ball joint for 360-degree positioning and screw holes for permanent mounting. Pair this with our ESP32-CAM security camera guide.

Print settings: PETG recommended for outdoor use, 0.2mm layers, 25% infill.

3. Sonoff Smart Switch Enclosure with Mount

Weatherproof enclosure for Sonoff Basic/R3 smart switches with integrated wall mount tabs. Great for outdoor smart home projects.

Print settings: ASA or PETG, 0.2mm layers, 30% infill, 3 perimeters.

4. Amazon Echo Dot (4th/5th Gen) Wall Mount

Minimalist ring mount that lets you hang your Echo Dot on the wall. Keeps your counter clear and points the speaker toward you. Fits both 4th and 5th generation Echo Dots.

Print settings: PLA works fine, 0.2mm layers, 15% infill.

5. Raspberry Pi 4 Wall Mount with Cable Management

Full enclosure for Raspberry Pi 4 with built-in cable management channels and ventilation. Perfect for Home Assistant or OctoPrint servers. Check out our Raspberry Pi NAS build guide for similar projects.

Print settings: PLA or PETG, 0.2mm layers, 20% infill, supports required for overhangs.

6. Smart Thermostat Recessed Mount

Frame mount for popular smart thermostats (Nest, Ecobee, Honeywell) that covers the rectangular hole left by old thermostat bases. Includes level bubble holder for perfect installation.

Print settings: PLA, 0.15mm layers for smooth finish, 25% infill.

7. Zigbee Sensor Multi-Mount

Versatile mount for common Zigbee sensors (Aqara, Sonoff, Hue). Features a 55mm diameter recess with optional snap-in bracket for ceiling or wall mounting.

Print settings: PLA, 0.2mm layers, 15% infill.

8. Tablet Dashboard Wall Mount

Frame-style mount for tablets used as Home Assistant dashboards. Fits 7-10 inch tablets with adjustable viewing angle. Combine with our smart mirror guide for inspiration.

Print settings: PETG for strength, 0.2mm layers, 30% infill, print in parts.

9. WS2812B LED Strip Channel Mount

Clip-on mounts for WS2812B LED strips that attach to walls. Diffuses light and hides the strip itself. See our WS2812B LED control guide for the electronics side.

Print settings: PLA, 0.2mm layers, 20% infill. Use white filament for better light diffusion.

10. Cable Management Raceway with Sensor Mounts

Combines cable routing with integrated mounting points for ESP32 sensors. Run cables along walls while keeping your sensors positioned optimally. Great for complex setups like our DIY weather station.

Print settings: PLA, 0.2mm layers, 20% infill, print in 100mm sections.

Material Recommendations

Choose your filament based on where the mount will be used:

  • Indoor (living room, bedroom): PLA is fine. It's easy to print, rigid, and comes in many colors.
  • Indoor (warm areas, near electronics): Consider PETG for better heat resistance. See our PETG vs PLA comparison.
  • Outdoor or garage: Use ASA or PETG. Both resist UV and temperature extremes. Our ASA filament guide covers outdoor printing in detail.
  • Visible installations: Print in a color that matches your walls, or paint after printing using techniques from our painting guide.

Printing Tips for Wall Mounts

Get the best results with these tips:

  • Orient for strength: Print tall, thin mounts vertically so layer lines don't create weak points parallel to the wall.
  • Use brims for adhesion: Tall, thin prints can tip over. A 5mm brim helps them stay attached to the bed.
  • Slow down for overhangs: If your mount has shelves or protrusions, slow down to 30-40mm/s for better overhang quality.
  • Test fit before finalizing: Print a test piece at 0.3mm layer height to verify fit before committing to a longer 0.15mm print.

Where to Find More Models

  • Printables: Search for your specific device name + "mount" or "enclosure"
  • Thingiverse: Large collection but quality varies. Check comments for issues.
  • Thangs: Good for finding models across multiple sites at once.

Conclusion

3D printing your own wall mounts is one of the most practical ways to integrate your smart home devices cleanly and professionally. With basic design skills and a few hours of printing, you can create custom solutions that commercial products simply can't match.

Start with one of the ready-to-print models above, then customize it to fit your exact needs. Once you've designed a few mounts, you'll have templates you can quickly modify for any new device you add to your smart home.
