Best Raspberry Pi Alternatives in 2026: Orange Pi, Banana Pi & More Compared

📅 August 3, 2026 ⏱️ 11 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🍓

The Raspberry Pi has been the darling of the maker world since 2012. But in 2026, it's no longer the only game in town. Whether you're priced out by Pi shortages, need more performance for a smart mirror project, or want native SATA for a home NAS build, there are compelling alternatives worth considering.

In this comprehensive guide, we'll compare the best Raspberry Pi alternatives available in 2026 — from budget-friendly Orange Pi boards to powerhouse Odroid machines. By the end, you'll know exactly which single-board computer (SBC) fits your next DIY project.

Why Consider a Raspberry Pi Alternative?

Before diving into specific boards, let's address the elephant in the room: why not just buy a Raspberry Pi?

Supply and Price Issues

The Raspberry Pi shortage of 2020-2023 left a lasting impact. While supply has stabilized, prices haven't fully returned to pre-shortage levels. A Raspberry Pi 4 4GB that once cost $55 now often sells for $70-80. For budget-conscious makers, alternatives offer similar specs at lower prices.

Performance Needs

The Raspberry Pi 5 is impressive, but some projects need more. If you're building a Home Assistant server with dozens of Zigbee devices, running machine learning models, or transcoding 4K video for a media server, boards like Odroid or Rock Pi offer significantly more processing power.

Feature Gaps

Raspberry Pi boards prioritize GPIO and educational use. But what if you need:

  • Native SATA for direct SSD/HDD connection (no USB adapters)
  • PCIe slots for expansion cards
  • More RAM (some alternatives offer 16GB+)
  • Better storage options like NVMe SSDs
  • Multiple Ethernet ports for router/firewall projects

Alternative SBCs fill these gaps while Raspberry Pi remains focused on its core mission.

Learning and Experimentation

Exploring alternative platforms expands your skills. Different SoCs (Rockchip, Allwinner, Amlogic) teach you about the ARM ecosystem beyond Broadcom. If you're building a smart home with ESP32 and need a central hub, understanding multiple SBC platforms makes you a more versatile maker.

Top Raspberry Pi Alternatives in 2026

Let's break down the best alternatives by category:

Best Budget Alternative: Orange Pi 5

Price: $60-85 (4GB model)

SoC: Rockchip RK3588 (8-core ARM Cortex-A76/A55)

RAM: 4GB, 8GB, 16GB, or 32GB options

The Orange Pi 5 is arguably the most popular Raspberry Pi alternative in 2026. Built on Rockchip's powerful RK3588 SoC, it outperforms the Raspberry Pi 5 in raw CPU benchmarks while costing less.

Key advantages:

  • 2-3x faster CPU than Raspberry Pi 5
  • NVMe SSD slot (M.2 2280) for fast storage
  • Dual HDMI 2.1 output (8K capable)
  • Good community support and Armbian compatibility

Best for: Media centers, Home Assistant servers, general-purpose desktop replacement

Trade-offs: Software support isn't as polished as Raspberry Pi OS. Expect to use Armbian or third-party distributions. Power management is less refined, and the board runs warm under load.

Best for Storage Projects: Banana Pi BPI-M5

Price: $75-95

SoC: Amlogic S905X3 (4-core ARM Cortex-A55)

RAM: 4GB

For makers building NAS servers or storage-heavy projects, the Banana Pi BPI-M5's native SATA support is a game-changer. No USB-to-SATA adapters, no performance bottlenecks — just direct, reliable disk connections.

Key advantages:

  • Native SATA III port for direct HDD/SSD connection
  • Low power consumption (~3W idle)
  • Gigabit Ethernet + Wi-Fi onboard
  • Good for always-on storage servers

Best for: NAS builds, file servers, media storage, backup appliances

Trade-offs: Slower CPU than Orange Pi 5 or Raspberry Pi 5. Not ideal for compute-heavy tasks. Community is smaller than Orange Pi's.

Best Performance: Odroid M1

Price: $100-150

SoC: Rockchip RK3568 (4-core ARM Cortex-A55)

RAM: 4GB or 8GB

Hardkernel's Odroid line has been a staple in the SBC world for years. The Odroid M1 targets serious makers who need reliability, expandability, and long-term support.

Key advantages:

  • PCIe slots for expansion cards (NVMe, SATA, network cards)
  • Excellent build quality and documentation
  • Long-term support from Hardkernel (5+ years)
  • Dedicated GPU for hardware video decoding

Best for: Complex projects requiring expansion cards, professional applications, long-term deployments

Trade-offs: More expensive. Large form factor won't fit in standard Pi cases. Overkill for simple projects.

Best for IoT and Smart Home: Rock Pi 4C+

Price: $65-90

SoC: Rockchip RK3399 (6-core ARM Cortex-A72/A53)

RAM: 4GB

The Rock Pi 4C+ strikes a balance between performance and IoT-friendly features. It's an excellent choice for Home Assistant hubs, smart door controllers, and IoT gateways.

Key advantages:

  • Better Wi-Fi than Raspberry Pi (dual-band, external antenna support)
  • NVMe SSD support via M.2 slot
  • 40-pin GPIO compatible with Raspberry Pi HATs
  • USB-C power delivery support

Best for: Smart home hubs, IoT projects, makers who need reliable Wi-Fi

Trade-offs: Older SoC (2018) means less raw performance than RK3588-based boards. Software support requires Radxa's custom images or Armbian.

Best Value: Orange Pi Zero 3

Price: $15-25 (1GB model)

SoC: Allwinner H618 (4-core ARM Cortex-A53)

RAM: 512MB, 1GB, 2GB options

For ultra-budget projects, the Orange Pi Zero 3 is unbeatable. At $15, it's cheaper than a Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W while offering better performance and full-size ports.

Key advantages:

  • Incredibly low price
  • Full-size HDMI, USB, and Ethernet ports
  • Small form factor (56mm x 46mm)
  • Good for headless servers and lightweight tasks

Best for: Lightweight IoT projects, DNS servers, AdBlock devices, sensor hubs

Trade-offs: Limited RAM (512MB-2GB). Not suitable for desktop use. Allwinner SoC has weaker community support than Rockchip.

Software and Ecosystem Considerations

Hardware specs only tell half the story. The Raspberry Pi's greatest strength is its software ecosystem. Here's how alternatives compare:

Operating System Support

Raspberry Pi: Raspberry Pi OS (official), Ubuntu, Fedora, countless others. First-class support from major distributions.

Orange Pi / Rock Pi: Armbian (community-maintained), vendor-specific images, sometimes Ubuntu. Not as polished, but functional.

Odroid: Hardkernel provides custom Ubuntu images. Good quality, but fewer choices than Raspberry Pi.

Banana Pi: Armbian, community images. Hit-or-miss quality depending on the board.

Community and Documentation

Raspberry Pi's documentation is extensive, beginner-friendly, and officially maintained. Alternatives rely more on community wikis, forum posts, and GitHub repositories. If you're new to SBCs, the Raspberry Pi's ecosystem is significantly easier to navigate.

However, if you're comfortable with Linux troubleshooting and reading source code, alternative boards offer more freedom to customize and optimize. The PCB design skills you develop with alternative platforms translate well to other electronics projects.

GPIO and HAT Compatibility

Most Raspberry Pi alternatives include a 40-pin GPIO header, but compatibility isn't guaranteed:

  • Pins match, libraries don't: Physical GPIO pins may align, but software libraries like RPi.GPIO won't work
  • Voltage differences: Some boards use 1.8V GPIO instead of 3.3V, requiring level shifters
  • HATs may not fit: Different board layouts mean Raspberry Pi HATs physically won't attach

For GPIO-heavy projects (like controlling relay modules or reading sensors), verify that your chosen alternative has working GPIO libraries for your programming language.

Project-Specific Recommendations

Home Assistant Hub

Best pick: Orange Pi 5 (8GB) or Rock Pi 4C+

Running Home Assistant with Zigbee2MQTT, ESPHome, and multiple integrations benefits from extra RAM and CPU. The Orange Pi 5's RK3588 chip handles database operations and automation scripts smoothly. Rock Pi 4C+ offers better Wi-Fi if you're placing the hub centrally.

Consider pairing with a Zigbee coordinator for your smart devices. A Mosquitto MQTT broker runs efficiently on either board.

Media Center / Kodi Box

Best pick: Orange Pi 5

The RK3588's video decoding capabilities (8K decode, 4K encode) make it a powerhouse media center. Dual HDMI 2.1 outputs support high-refresh displays, and NVMe storage ensures smooth library navigation. Install CoreELEC or LibreELEC for a dedicated media OS.

Network Storage (NAS)

Best pick: Banana Pi BPI-M5 with native SATA

For a home NAS, direct SATA connection eliminates the USB bottlenecks that plague Raspberry Pi-based storage servers. Install OpenMediaVault and connect multiple drives for reliable network storage.

Desktop Replacement

Best pick: Orange Pi 5 (16GB or 32GB model) or Odroid M1

Using an SBC as a daily driver requires 8GB+ RAM and fast storage. The Orange Pi 5's NVMe slot and high-RAM options make it viable for web browsing, coding, and light productivity. For better expansion options (PCIe cards for better GPU or network), choose Odroid M1.

Lightweight IoT Projects

Best pick: Orange Pi Zero 3

For headless projects like DNS servers (Pi-hole), AdBlock (AdGuard Home), or simple sensor hubs, the Zero 3's $15 price point is perfect. It runs Armbian well and sips power at idle.

Pair with ESPHome devices for distributed sensors throughout your home.

Buying Tips and Gotchas

Before ordering an alternative SBC, keep these in mind:

Power Supply Requirements

Alternative boards often have different power requirements than Raspberry Pi:

  • Orange Pi 5: Requires 5V/4A USB-C (not standard phone chargers)
  • Odroid M1: 12V/2A barrel jack (entirely different standard)
  • Rock Pi 4C+: USB-C PD preferred for stable operation

Check power specs carefully. Using an underpowered supply causes random crashes and data corruption.

Cooling Requirements

High-performance SoCs like RK3588 run hot. The Orange Pi 5 needs active cooling (fan + heatsink) for sustained loads. Budget for a case with ventilation and quality thermal pads.

Shipping and Availability

Most alternative SBCs ship from China. Delivery times of 2-4 weeks are common. AliExpress and official vendor sites offer the best prices, but Amazon may provide faster shipping at a markup.

Warranty and Support

Raspberry Pi has established warranty processes and distributors worldwide. Alternative board support varies widely. Some vendors (Hardkernel/Odroid) offer good support; others (no-name brands) provide none. Read reviews before buying.

When to Stick with Raspberry Pi

Despite the compelling alternatives, there are still reasons to choose Raspberry Pi:

  • Beginners: If you're new to SBCs, the documentation and community support are invaluable
  • Educational use: Schools and workshops benefit from standardization
  • HAT ecosystem: If you're using specific Raspberry Pi HATs (camera modules, PoE HATs, etc.), staying with Pi ensures compatibility
  • Reliability: For critical systems that need to "just work," the mature software stack reduces risk

The Raspberry Pi 5 is still an excellent board. Alternatives aren't "better" universally — they're better for specific use cases.

Final Recommendations

Choose your SBC based on project needs:

  • Best overall alternative: Orange Pi 5 — great performance, good community, reasonable price
  • Best for NAS/storage: Banana Pi BPI-M5 — native SATA is a killer feature
  • Best for expansion: Odroid M1 — PCIe slots unlock professional applications
  • Best for smart home: Rock Pi 4C+ — good Wi-Fi, HAT-compatible GPIO
  • Best budget option: Orange Pi Zero 3 — $15 for a capable headless server

For most makers, the Orange Pi 5 hits the sweet spot: significantly faster than Raspberry Pi 5, affordable, and well-supported by the community. If you're building a NAS, smart mirror, or Home Assistant hub, it's worth serious consideration.

Whichever board you choose, remember: the best SBC is the one that fits your project. Specs on paper matter less than having working drivers, good documentation, and a supportive community. Before committing, search for your specific project (e.g., "Orange Pi 5 Home Assistant") to see if others have successfully walked that path.
