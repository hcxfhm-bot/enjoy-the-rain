3D Printing Tolerances: How to Get Parts That Fit Together

📅 May 23, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
⚙️

You designed two parts that should snap together — but they don't. The hole is too tight, or the peg is too loose. This is a tolerance problem, and it's the #1 frustration for anyone moving from decorative prints to functional parts.

FDM 3D printers are not perfectly accurate. Every printer has a slight dimensional error, and understanding how much — and compensating for it — is the difference between parts that fit and parts that go in the trash.

What Are 3D Printing Tolerances?

Tolerance is the allowed deviation from a designed dimension. In 3D printing, it means how close your printed part is to the size you specified in your CAD model.

A well-tuned FDM printer can hold ±0.20mm tolerance on most dimensions. A poorly calibrated one might be off by ±0.50mm or more. That difference is the gap between "slides in perfectly" and "doesn't fit at all."

Why FDM Prints Aren't Dimensionally Perfect

Several factors cause dimensional error in FDM printing:

  • Extrusion width: Your nozzle deposits a line slightly wider than the nozzle diameter. A 0.4mm nozzle typically creates a 0.42-0.45mm line, making external dimensions slightly larger and holes slightly smaller.
  • Shrinkage: Filament shrinks as it cools. PLA shrinks ~0.3%, ABS ~0.8%. A 100mm part in ABS shrinks to 99.2mm.
  • Over-extrusion: Too much flow makes everything bigger. Even 5% over-extrusion adds 0.10mm to each side of a part.
  • Belt slack and backlash: Loose belts cause positional errors, especially on reversals (direction changes). This makes circles slightly oval.

The Hole vs. Peg Problem

Here's the core issue: holes always print smaller than designed, and pegs always print larger. This double-whammy means you need more clearance than you'd expect.

A 10mm hole typically prints at 9.6-9.8mm. A 10mm peg prints at 10.1-10.3mm. So your "10mm peg in a 10mm hole" has an actual interference of 0.3-0.7mm — that's why it doesn't fit.

Clearance Values That Actually Work

These values assume a reasonably well-tuned printer (±0.20mm). Adjust up or down based on your printer's accuracy.

Fit TypeClearance (per side)Total ClearanceExample Use
Press fit0.05-0.10mm0.10-0.20mmSnap-fit connectors, permanent joints
Snug sliding0.10-0.15mm0.20-0.30mmDrawers, lids, caps
Smooth sliding0.15-0.25mm0.30-0.50mmMoving parts, rails, sliders
Loose / clearance0.25-0.40mm0.50-0.80mmPivot points, rotating joints

Example: For a 10mm peg that needs to slide smoothly through a hole, make the hole 10.4-10.5mm in your CAD model.

How to Calibrate Your Printer's Tolerance

Before you design parts with tight tolerances, you need to know your printer's actual accuracy. Here's a quick calibration process:

Step 1: Print a Tolerance Test

Download a tolerance test model (search Thingiverse or Printables for "tolerance test"). These typically have a series of pegs and holes with decreasing clearance. The smallest peg that still fits tells you your printer's practical tolerance.

Step 2: Measure with Calipers

Use digital calipers (a $10 pair is fine) to measure both the pegs and holes on your test print. Write down:

  • Designed peg size vs. actual peg size
  • Designed hole size vs. actual hole size
  • The difference = your printer's dimensional offset

Step 3: Adjust Flow Rate

If your pegs are consistently 0.1-0.2mm oversized, you're over-extruding. Reduce flow rate by 2-3% in your slicer and re-test. For a full calibration walkthrough, see our 3D printer calibration guide.

Step 4: Apply Horizontal Expansion

Most slicers have a "horizontal expansion" or "XY compensation" setting. If your external dimensions are consistently 0.15mm oversized, set horizontal expansion to -0.15mm. This scales all external dimensions down automatically.

Tolerance by Filament Type

Different filaments need different clearance because of shrinkage:

  • PLA: Minimal shrinkage (0.3%). Use the standard clearance values above.
  • PETG: Slight shrinkage (0.3-0.5%). Add 0.05mm extra clearance. PETG is also slightly more elastic, so press fits grip harder than expected.
  • ABS/ASA: Significant shrinkage (0.7-1.0%). Add 0.15-0.20mm extra clearance. Parts designed on screen will be noticeably smaller when printed. Enclosed printers with stable temps help. Check our ABS filament guide for more.
  • TPU: Very forgiving — flexible material compresses. You can use tighter tolerances (0.05-0.10mm less clearance) because TPU deforms to fit.

Design Tips for Better-Fitting Parts

  • Add chamfers, not sharp edges: A 0.5mm chamfer on peg edges helps them slide into holes without catching.
  • Use alignment pins: Instead of one large peg, use two small ones. They're easier to print accurately and guide the parts together.
  • Print parts in the same orientation: Tolerances vary depending on layer direction. If you print a peg flat and a hole standing up, they'll have different dimensional errors.
  • Account for elephant foot: The first few layers are slightly wider due to squish. Either add a small chamfer at the base or use ironing settings to reduce it.
  • Use threaded inserts for precision joints: When parts need to be disassembled, threaded inserts give you metal-thread precision that printed threads can't match.

When to Blame the Printer vs. the Design

Before tweaking your printer, check your design first:

  • Hole comes out oval: Loose belt. Tighten the belt for that axis.
  • Everything is uniformly oversized: Over-extrusion. Reduce flow rate.
  • Parts don't fit but dimensions measure correct: Design issue — your clearance values are too tight. Add 0.1mm more per side.
  • One axis is consistently off: Your steps/mm calibration is wrong on that axis. Follow our calibration guide to fix e-steps and XYZ steps.

Conclusion

Getting parts that fit together comes down to three things: knowing your printer's tolerance, designing with the right clearance, and compensating for filament shrinkage. Print a tolerance test, measure with calipers, and apply those offsets to every design. For PLA, start with 0.20mm clearance per side for sliding fits. For ABS, bump that to 0.35mm.

Once your printer is dialed in, functional prints become just as reliable as decorative ones. For more on getting the best out of your printer, see our complete settings guide and speed vs. quality tips.
