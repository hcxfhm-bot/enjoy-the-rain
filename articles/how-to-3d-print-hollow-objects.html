How to 3D Print Hollow Objects: Settings, Tips & Wall Thickness

📅 May 24, 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🎨

Printing hollow objects saves filament, reduces print time, and creates lightweight parts. But there's a catch — hollow prints collapse, leak, or crack if your wall thickness or settings are wrong. This guide covers two ways to print hollow (infill-based and vase mode), the minimum wall thickness for each material, and how to avoid the most common failures.

Two Ways to Print Hollow

There are fundamentally different approaches to hollow 3D printing, and they suit different use cases:

Method 1: Low or Zero Infill

Set infill to 0% (or 5–10% for stability). The printer builds the outer walls normally but leaves the interior empty. This works for boxes, enclosures, and containers where you need defined top and bottom surfaces.

  • Best for: Enclosures, containers, hollow figurines, lamp shades
  • Pros: Full design freedom, closed top/bottom surfaces
  • Cons: Very thin walls can sag or collapse during printing

Method 2: Vase Mode (Spiralize)

Vase mode (called "Spiralize Outer Contour" in Cura) prints a single continuous wall that spirals upward. No Z-seam, no layer transitions — just one smooth, unbroken line. It produces watertight, seamless vessels.

  • Best for: Vases, cups, hollow cylinders, decorative containers
  • Pros: Seamless, fast, beautiful surface finish
  • Cons: Only works on models with a single continuous outer wall. No internal features, no closed top surface.

Minimum Wall Thickness by Material

Going too thin is the #1 reason hollow prints fail. Here are the practical minimums for a standard 0.4mm nozzle:

MaterialMin Wall (mm)Wall Line CountNotes
PLA0.82 linesMost forgiving. 0.8mm prints reliably.
PETG1.02–3 linesSlightly more sag; 1.0mm is safer.
ABS1.23 linesWarp risk at thin walls. Needs enclosure.
TPU1.02–3 linesFlexible = more wall sag. Thicker is better.
Nylon1.23 linesWarps if too thin. See nylon settings.

Rule of thumb: Always use at least 2 wall lines (0.8mm with a 0.4mm nozzle). For functional parts that take stress, use 3 lines (1.2mm).

Settings for Low-Infill Hollow Prints

When you want a hollow object with top/bottom surfaces (like a box):

  • Infill: 0% (completely hollow) or 5% (light grid support)
  • Wall count: 3–4 lines for structural parts, 2 for display-only
  • Top/bottom layers: 4–6 layers (prevents sagging on the top surface)
  • Print speed: Reduce to 80% of normal — thin walls need more time to cool
  • Part cooling: 80–100% for PLA, 40–60% for PETG
  • Infill pattern: If using 5% infill, gyroid distributes force better than grid

Common problem: The top surface sags into the hollow interior. Fix this by increasing top layer count to 6–8, or add 5% infill as a support scaffold.

Settings for Vase Mode

Vase mode requires specific settings to work correctly:

  • Enable Spiralize: Yes (Cura: Special Modes → Spiralize Outer Contour)
  • Wall line count: 1 (that's the whole point — single wall)
  • Bottom layers: 4–6 (seals the base)
  • Top layers: 0 (vase mode can't close the top)
  • Infill: 0% (ignored by vase mode anyway)
  • Z-hop: Disable — vase mode should never lift
  • Flow rate: 95–100% (over-extrusion creates rough surfaces)

Wall thickness in vase mode: Controlled by line width, not wall count. Set line width to 0.4–0.6mm for a standard nozzle. A wider line (0.6mm) gives a sturdier wall at the cost of a slightly rougher exterior.

When Vase Mode Doesn't Work

Vase mode only works on models with one continuous outer contour per layer. It fails if your model has:

  • Internal walls or partitions
  • Multiple separate bodies on the same layer
  • Holes that go all the way through (like a donut shape with changing inner diameter)
  • Overhangs that need supports

If your model has any of these, use low-infill hollow instead.

Common Hollow Print Problems & Fixes

  • Walls collapse inward: Increase wall count to 3. Reduce speed. Increase cooling.
  • Top surface sags: Add more top layers (6–8). Or use 5% infill as internal support.
  • Print is not watertight: In vase mode, increase line width. In normal mode, increase flow to 102%. Check for under-extrusion.
  • Stringing inside hollow space: Enable retraction. Increase retraction distance by 0.5mm. See our stringing fix guide.
  • Warping at base: Use a brim. Increase bed temp by 5°C. See warping solutions.

Best Hollow Print Ideas

  • Vases & planters: Classic vase mode project. Try the "Twisted Vase" on Thingiverse.
  • Lamp shades: Low-infill with thin walls lets light through beautifully. Use white PLA.
  • Storage boxes: 0% infill with 3 walls and a lid. Lightweight but functional.
  • Hollow figurines: Save filament on large display models. 0% infill, 2 walls.
  • Floats & buoys: Vase mode creates watertight objects. Perfect for pool toys.

Conclusion

Hollow 3D printing is a skill that pays off immediately — you'll save filament and time on every large print. Start with vase mode for simple vessels (it's almost foolproof), then experiment with low-infill hollow for boxes and enclosures. The key rules: never go below 2 wall lines, add extra top layers to prevent sagging, and slow down when walls are thin. With these basics, your hollow prints will come out clean, strong, and filament-efficient.
