Bambu Lab P1S Review: The Best Mid-Range 3D Printer in 2026?

📅 June 1, 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🎯

The Bambu Lab P1S sits in an interesting position in Bambu's lineup — more capable than the A1 series but significantly cheaper than the X1-Carbon. It's marketed as the "sweet spot" between price and performance. But does it live up to that promise?

After three months of daily printing on the P1S, here's what I've found.

What Makes the P1S Special

1. Enclosed at $699

The P1S is the cheapest enclosed CoreXY printer from a major brand. The enclosure makes it viable for ABS, ASA, and other high-temp materials without any DIY modifications. This alone sets it apart from the A1 series.

2. CoreXY Speed

The CoreXY kinematics deliver genuine 500 mm/s printing. Unlike bed-slinger printers where high speeds cause quality issues, the P1S maintains excellent quality even at 300+ mm/s. The 20,000 mm/s² acceleration means it reaches top speed almost instantly.

3. AMS Multi-Material

With the AMS (sold separately at ~$300), the P1S becomes a capable multi-color printer. Bambu Studio's filament painting feature makes color assignment intuitive, and the automatic flushing system handles material changes reliably.

What You Miss vs the X1-Carbon

The $500 price difference comes down to:

  • No Lidar first-layer scanning — The X1-Carbon uses a Lidar sensor to verify the first layer in real time. The P1S relies on traditional mesh bed leveling. In practice, this rarely matters if your bed is clean and properly leveled.
  • No AI spaghetti detection — The X1-Carbon can detect print failures and auto-pause. The P1S camera is manual-monitoring only.
  • Different hotend — The P1S uses a standard hotend vs the X1-Carbon's all-metal hotend. Both handle 300°C, but the X1's is more durable for continuous high-temp printing.

Real-World Print Quality

Print quality is virtually identical to the X1-Carbon for 95% of prints. The same Bambu Studio profiles produce the same results. Input shaping, pressure advance, and flow calibration all work the same way.

The only scenario where the X1-Carbon pulls ahead is unattended printing with unknown filaments — the Lidar and AI monitoring can catch issues the P1S can't.

Verdict

The P1S is the best mid-range 3D printer in 2026. It delivers 95% of the X1-Carbon's capability at 60% of the price. Unless you need AI monitoring or print constantly with exotic materials, the P1S is the smarter buy.
