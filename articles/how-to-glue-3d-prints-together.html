How to Glue 3D Prints Together: Best Adhesives & Techniques

📅 June 1, 2026⏱️ 7 min read✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🧴

When your print is too big for the build plate or you need to combine multiple colors, gluing is the answer. But not all adhesives work with all filaments, and the wrong glue can create weak joints that fail under stress.

Best Glues by Material

MaterialBest GlueBond Strength
PLACyanoacrylate (CA) + acceleratorStrong
PETG2-part epoxyVery Strong
ABSABS slurry (ABS + acetone)Strongest (welds)
ResinUV-cure resin or CA glueStrong
TPUFlexible CA or contact cementModerate

Technique for Invisible Seams

  1. Sand both surfaces flat with 220 grit — flat surfaces bond better
  2. Apply thin, even layer of adhesive to one surface only
  3. Press firmly for 30 seconds — ensure full contact
  4. Use CA accelerator for instant bond (but be careful — it creates white residue)
  5. Fill any gaps with gap-filling CA or epoxy

ABS Slurry — The Welding Method

Dissolve ABS scraps in acetone to create a paste. Apply to both surfaces and clamp. The acetone melts the ABS, creating a molecular weld — the joint becomes as strong as the original print. This is the gold standard for ABS assembly.

Structural Reinforcement

For parts under load, glue alone isn't enough:

  • Pins — Insert metal rods or wooden dowels into aligned holes
  • Magnets — Embed neodymium magnets for snap-fit connections
  • Threaded inserts — Use heat-set inserts for bolt-together assemblies

See our threaded inserts guide and tolerances guide for more assembly techniques.
