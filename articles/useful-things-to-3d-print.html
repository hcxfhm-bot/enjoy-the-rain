15 Actually Useful Things to 3D Print at Home

📅 May 10, 2026 ⏱️ 9 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🏠

The Problem with "Cool" 3D Prints

Let's be honest: most 3D printing content online is about figurines, dragons, and decorative nonsense that ends up in a landfill within a year. They look impressive on social media, but they don't solve real problems. If you want to justify owning a 3D printer to your spouse, roommates, or yourself, you need to print useful things.

This list focuses on prints that solve actual problems: organizing your space, fixing broken items, customizing your workspace, and replacing expensive store-bought tools. Each one has been tested by real users and proven to earn its place in a home.

All models are free and available on Printables or Thingiverse. No paid files, no subscriptions.

1. Drawer Organizers

Problem solved: Junk drawers where batteries, screws, and cables mix into an unusable mess.

Why it works: Unlike generic organizers, 3D printed ones can be custom-sized to your exact drawer dimensions. Print multiple small bins that fit together perfectly.

Filament: PLA (cheap, rigid, looks clean).

Print time: 30 minutes to 2 hours per bin.

2. Cable Management Clips

Problem solved: Charging cables that fall behind the desk every time you unplug your phone.

Why it works: Stick these to your desk edge with double-sided tape. Cables clip in and stay put. Print a few in different colors to match your setup.

Filament: PLA or PETG (PETG is more flexible for snap-fit).

Print time: 15–30 minutes per clip.

3. Custom Phone Stand

Problem solved: Propping your phone against a coffee mug to watch videos.

Why it works: You can print a stand that matches your phone's exact dimensions, includes cable routing, and has the right angle for your desk setup. Many designs also work as a ring holder.

Filament: PLA (easy to print, looks good).

Print time: 1.5–3 hours.

See our beginner projects guide for more details on printing phone stands.

4. Replacement Dishwasher Rack Clips

Problem solved: Dishwasher racks where the plastic coating chipped, exposing sharp metal that rusts and tears dishcloths.

Why it works: Replacement clips from the manufacturer cost $10–20 each. You can print a full set for under $1. Measure the rack's diameter and search for "dishwasher rack tip" on Printables.

Filament: PETG (dishwasher-safe temperatures; PLA will soften).

Print time: 10–20 minutes per clip.

5. Wall-Mounted Tool Rack

Problem solved: Screwdrivers and wrenches scattered in a drawer, impossible to find when you need them.

Why it works: Print custom holders that mount to a French cleat system or direct to the wall. Each tool gets a labeled spot. No more digging.

Filament: PLA (indoor use) or PETG (garage/humid areas).

Print time: 2–6 hours depending on complexity.

6. Customizable Planters with Drainage

Problem solved: Store-bought planters that are either ugly, expensive, or don't fit your space.

Why it works: 3D printed planters can be sized exactly for your windowsill, hanging location, or desk. Just make sure to include drainage holes — or print a separate saucer.

Filament: PLA (indoor plants) or PETG (outdoor/humid).

Print time: 2–5 hours.

Note: Standard PLA absorbs moisture over time and may degrade outdoors. For outdoor planters, use PETG or coat the print with a sealant.

7. GoPro / Camera Mounts

Problem solved: Expensive name-brand mounts that cost $30+ for a piece of plastic.

Why it works: Action camera mounts are simple shapes. You can print a variety of angles and lengths for a fraction of the cost. Just make sure the tolerance is tight — a loose mount means a lost camera.

Filament: PETG (more impact-resistant than PLA).

Print time: 30 minutes to 2 hours.

8. Custom Key Covers

Problem solved: Identifying keys in the dark or in a hurry (house, office, mailbox, bike lock).

Why it works: Print small color-coded caps that snap onto key heads. Different shapes for different keys — no more guessing which key is which.

Filament: PLA (rigid, holds shape).

Print time: 10–15 minutes per cover.

9. Shower Caddy / Soap Dish

Problem solved: Soap that sits in a puddle and turns into mush.

Why it works: A printed soap dish with drainage slots keeps soap dry. Print it in PETG to survive humid bathroom conditions.

Filament: PETG (moisture-resistant).

Print time: 1–2 hours.

10. Custom Washer Fluid Funnel

Problem solved: Spilling washer fluid when refilling your car because the opening is in a cramped location.

Why it works: Print a custom-shaped funnel that fits your specific car's washer fluid reservoir. No more spills, no more wasted fluid.

Filament: PLA or PETG (both work; PETG is more durable).

Print time: 30–60 minutes.

11. Board Game Organizers

Problem solved: Cardboard inserts that fall apart after 10 games, pieces that migrate between compartments.

Why it works: Custom organizers fit the game box exactly. Cards stay sorted, meeples stay in their lane. Search for your specific game on Printables — someone has probably already designed it.

Filament: PLA (cheap, rigid).

Print time: 3–10 hours (multiple pieces).

12. Replacement Vacuum Cleaner Attachments

Problem solved: Lost or broken vacuum attachments that cost a fortune to replace.

Why it works: Measure the vacuum's tube diameter and print a custom attachment. Crevice tools, brush adapters, even custom shapes for pet hair — all possible.

Filament: PETG (flexible enough to not scratch surfaces).

Print time: 1–3 hours.

13. Customizable Coin Sorter

Problem solved: Loose change scattered in jars, taking up space and never organized.

Why it works: A printed coin sorter uses angled slots sized for each coin denomination. Drop in a handful of change, and it sorts automatically. It's oddly satisfying to watch.

Filament: PLA (rigid, smooth surfaces).

Print time: 4–8 hours.

14. Tablet/Book Reading Stand

Problem solved: Neck strain from looking down at a tablet or book.

Why it works: An adjustable stand holds your tablet or book at eye level. Many designs are collapsible for travel. Great for following recipes in the kitchen or reading in bed.

Filament: PLA (looks good) or PETG (more durable).

Print time: 3–6 hours.

15. Custom Jigs and Fixtures

Problem solved: Repetitive tasks that need precise positioning (cutting, drilling, gluing).

Why it works: A 3D printed jig holds parts exactly where they need to be. Woodworkers love these for repeatable cuts. Crafters use them for consistent glue-ups. If you do any kind of making, jigs will save you hours.

Filament: PETG (impact-resistant) or TPU (for clamping soft materials).

Print time: 1–4 hours.

How to Choose the Right Filament

Not all prints need the same material. Here's a quick guide:

Use Case Best Filament Why
Indoor decorative PLA Easy, cheap, looks good
Outdoor / humid PETG Moisture + UV resistant
Tools / jigs PETG or ABS Impact-resistant
Flexible clips TPU Bends without breaking

For a deeper dive on filament properties, see our PETG vs PLA comparison and TPU guide for beginners.

Conclusion

A 3D printer is only as useful as what you print with it. These 15 projects prove that 3D printing isn't just about making plastic tchotchkes — it's about solving real problems around your home, saving money on replacements, and customizing your space in ways store-bought products can't match.

Start with the quick wins (cable clips, key covers, drawer organizers) to build your skills. Then move to the more involved projects (tool racks, jigs, replacement parts) that really show off what 3D printing can do. Before long, you'll find yourself thinking "I could print that" every time you encounter a small problem around the house — and that's when you know the printer has paid for itself.
