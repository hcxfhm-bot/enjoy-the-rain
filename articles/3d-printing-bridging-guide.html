3D Printing Bridging: How to Print Over Gaps Without Supports

📅 May 25, 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🌉

What Is Bridging in 3D Printing?

Bridging is when your 3D printer creates a horizontal span between two points without any support underneath. Instead of printing on top of a solid layer or support material, the extruded filament stretches across an open gap, held in place only by its connection to the sides. Done well, bridges create clean horizontal surfaces without the hassle of support removal.

Every FDM printer can bridge to some degree — it's a fundamental capability of the technology. But there's a limit to how far filament can span before it sags, breaks, or creates a mess. Understanding bridging helps you design better models, orient prints smarter, and tune settings for cleaner overhangs.

The key difference between bridging and regular overhangs: bridges have anchor points on both sides, while overhangs extend outward from only one side. This makes bridges easier to print than extreme overhangs, but they still require proper technique.

How Bridging Works

When your printer creates a bridge, it's essentially stretching molten plastic across a gap. The plastic cools rapidly as it leaves the nozzle, solidifying enough to support itself. Success depends on several factors:

  • Cooling: Part cooling fan solidifies the filament quickly
  • Speed: Slower bridges give plastic time to cool
  • Extrusion: Slightly less material prevents sagging
  • Distance: Shorter bridges are easier
  • Material: Some filaments bridge better than others

Maximum Bridge Distance

How far can a bridge span before it fails? It depends on your printer, settings, and material, but here are realistic expectations:

Material Easy Bridge Challenging Bridge Support Recommended
PLA 0-30mm 30-60mm 60mm+
PETG 0-20mm 20-40mm 40mm+
TPU 0-10mm 10-20mm 20mm+
ABS 0-25mm 25-50mm 50mm+

PLA bridges best because it cools quickly and solidifies fast. PETG is stringier and tends to sag more. TPU bridges poorly due to its flexibility — expect sagging on anything beyond short spans.

Bridge Settings in Your Slicer

Modern slicers have dedicated bridge settings that optimize speed, cooling, and extrusion for bridging moves. Here's how to configure them:

Bridge Speed

Bridges need to be printed slower than normal layers to allow the filament to cool before sagging. However, there's a balance — too slow and the filament stretches and thins out.

Recommended bridge speeds:

  • PLA: 15-25 mm/s (slower for long bridges)
  • PETG: 20-30 mm/s
  • TPU: 10-15 mm/s

In Cura, this is under Speed → Bridge Speed. In PrusaSlicer, look under Print Settings → Speed → Bridges.

Bridge Flow Rate

Extruding slightly less material on bridges helps prevent sagging. The filament stretches slightly as it spans the gap, and full flow rate can result in excess material drooping down.

Recommended flow rates:

  • Short bridges (under 20mm): 95-100%
  • Medium bridges (20-40mm): 90-95%
  • Long bridges (40mm+): 85-90%

Cooling

Part cooling is critical for bridges. The fan should be at 100% during bridge moves to solidify the filament as quickly as possible. Most slicers automatically increase cooling for bridges.

If your bridges are sagging, check that your cooling fan is working properly and set to 100% for bridges.

Bridge Skin Support

Some slicers offer "bridge skin support" or similar features that print extra perimeters under bridge areas to provide more anchor points. This can help with challenging bridges but may leave visible patterns on the bottom surface.

Tips for Better Bridging

Beyond slicer settings, several techniques improve bridge quality:

1. Orient Your Model Strategically

The most effective way to handle bridges is to orient your model so bridges are minimized or eliminated. Rotate your model in the slicer to see if a different orientation reduces bridge length or changes bridge direction.

2. Use Bridge Patterns

When a bridge spans multiple parallel gaps (like the top of a hollow box), the printer can lay down lines that follow the shortest distance across each gap. This is more reliable than trying to fill a large area all at once.

3. Reduce Layer Height

Thinner layers are easier to bridge because there's less material to sag. If you're struggling with a specific bridge, try reducing layer height from 0.2mm to 0.16mm or even 0.12mm.

4. Check Your Temperature

Lower temperatures mean faster solidification. If bridges are sagging, try reducing nozzle temperature by 5-10°C. Just be careful not to go so low that you get under-extrusion.

5. Improve Part Cooling

Strong part cooling is essential for bridging. If your printer has a weak or poorly directed fan, consider upgrading to a dual-fan setup or a high-flow duct design. Better cooling dramatically improves bridge performance.

6. Use Multiple Perimeters

For large bridges, printing multiple perimeter lines first gives the infill more to anchor to. Set your slicer to print 3-4 wall perimeters before starting infill.

When to Use Supports Instead

Sometimes bridging just isn't the answer. Use supports when:

  • Bridge length exceeds 50mm: Even with perfect settings, long bridges will sag or fail.
  • Surface quality matters: Bridges always have some imperfection on the bottom surface.
  • Material bridges poorly: TPU and some exotic filaments need support for almost any bridge.
  • Model has complex geometry: Irregular bridges (not straight lines) are much harder to print.

For guidance on support settings, see our article on 3D print support settings.

Testing Your Bridge Capabilities

To find your printer's bridge limits, print a bridge test model. These are widely available on Thingiverse and Printables. A good test includes bridges of varying lengths, typically from 10mm up to 100mm.

How to Read Bridge Test Results

  • Clean bottom surface: Perfect bridge
  • Minor sagging: Acceptable, could improve with tuning
  • Significant droop: Reduce speed or increase cooling
  • Failed bridge: Reduce length, add support, or adjust settings

Common Bridge Problems and Fixes

Problem Likely Cause Solution
Sagging bridge Too fast, low cooling Slow down, fan to 100%
Gaps in bridge Flow too low Increase bridge flow to 100%
Bridge breaks mid-span Too long, poor adhesion Add support or reorient
Stringy mess PETG/TPU without tuning Tune retraction, add support
Uneven surface Inconsistent cooling Check fan alignment

Bridging vs Overhangs

While related, bridging and overhangs are different challenges:

  • Bridges span between two anchor points horizontally
  • Overhangs extend outward from a single anchor point

Bridges are generally easier because both ends are supported. Overhangs become problematic above 45° because each layer extends beyond the one below it. For more on handling steep angles, see our guide on printing overhangs without supports.

Conclusion

Bridging is a valuable skill that lets you print horizontal spans without the waste and effort of support material. With proper settings — slow speed, high cooling, and appropriate flow — most printers can handle bridges up to 30-50mm in PLA. For longer spans or demanding materials, supports remain the reliable choice.

Test your printer's bridge capabilities, tune your slicer settings, and consider model orientation when bridges appear in your prints. With practice, you'll know exactly when to rely on bridging and when to add supports.
