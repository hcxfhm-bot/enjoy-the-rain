Under-Extrusion in 3D Printing: Causes and Fixes for Beginners

📅 May 9, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
⬇️

What Is Under-Extrusion?

Under-extrusion happens when your 3D printer doesn't push enough filament through the nozzle to fill the space it's supposed to. Instead of solid walls, you get gaps, missing sections, and weak layer bonding. The print looks incomplete — thin walls, holes in solid layers, and top surfaces that don't close properly.

This is one of the most common problems for beginners, and it has many possible causes. The good news is that most are easy to diagnose and fix once you understand what's happening. Under-extrusion is always a supply problem — something is preventing enough filament from reaching the nozzle at the right time.

This guide covers the six most common causes, starting with the easiest to check and fix.

Signs of Under-Extrusion

Learn to recognize under-extrusion early:

  • Gaps between lines: Infill and walls don't touch each other
  • Thin, weak walls: Walls that should be solid feel fragile
  • Holes in top layers: Top surfaces have gaps instead of being smooth
  • Missing layers: Sections where no filament was deposited at all
  • Poor layer adhesion: Layers separate easily when you stress the part
  • Clicking extruder: The feeder gear skips back, making a clicking sound

If you see these symptoms, your printer isn't extruding enough plastic. Time to find out why.

Cause 1: Nozzle Clog or Partial Blockage

The most common cause of under-extrusion is a clogged nozzle. Dust, debris, or burnt filament accumulates inside the nozzle opening, restricting flow. The printer tries to push filament through, but the blockage limits how much can pass.

How to check:

  • Heat the nozzle to printing temperature and manually extrude filament. Does it come out smoothly in a straight line, or does it curl and sputter?
  • Look at the filament coming out of the nozzle. Is the diameter consistent, or does it vary?
  • Try a cold pull (heat to 200°C, insert filament, cool to 90°C, pull out quickly) to see if debris comes out with it.

How to fix:

  • Use a needle or nozzle cleaning wire to clear the opening while hot
  • Perform a cold pull with nylon filament to clean the inside
  • If the clog persists, replace the nozzle (they're cheap — keep spares)

Prevention: Use filament from a clean source, and avoid leaving the nozzle hot without filament for extended periods.

Cause 2: Nozzle Temperature Too Low

Filament needs to be hot enough to flow properly. If your nozzle temperature is set too low, the plastic stays viscous and doesn't flow fast enough to keep up with the print speed. The result: gaps and under-extrusion, especially during faster sections.

Recommended temperatures:

  • PLA: 190–220°C (start at 200°C, increase if under-extruding)
  • PETG: 230–250°C
  • TPU: 210–240°C
  • ABS: 230–260°C

How to fix:

  • Increase nozzle temperature by 5–10°C and test again
  • Print a temperature tower to find the optimal range for your specific filament
  • Check the filament manufacturer's recommendation — different brands need different temperatures

For detailed temperature settings by material, see our PLA Filament Temperature Guide.

Cause 3: Incorrect Flow Rate (Extrusion Multiplier)

Your slicer has a flow rate setting (also called extrusion multiplier) that determines how much filament gets pushed. If this is set below 100%, the printer deliberately under-extrudes. This can be useful for fine-tuning, but if set incorrectly, it causes problems.

Default values:

  • Most filaments: 95–100%
  • PLA: Usually 100%
  • PETG: 95–100% (some benefit from slight over-extrusion)
  • TPU: 100–105%

How to fix:

  • Check your slicer's flow rate setting — make sure it's at 100% or slightly above
  • Calibrate your extruder steps-per-mm (see Cause 5) for accurate flow
  • Don't compensate for other problems with flow rate — fix the root cause instead

Cause 4: Wet Filament

Filament absorbs moisture from the air, especially PETG, TPU, and nylon. When wet filament is heated, the water turns to steam, creating bubbles and reducing the amount of plastic that gets deposited. You'll hear popping sounds and see rough surfaces.

Symptoms of wet filament:

  • Popping or crackling sounds during extrusion
  • Steam or bubbles visible in the extruded filament
  • Stringing that gets worse over time
  • Poor surface finish and weak parts

How to fix:

  • Dry your filament using an oven, dehydrator, or filament dryer
  • Store filament in sealed bags with desiccant packets
  • See our guide on how to dry 3D printer filament for detailed instructions

Wet filament is one of the most overlooked causes of print problems. If nothing else explains your under-extrusion, check your filament.

Cause 5: Extruder Calibration (E-Steps)

Your extruder is calibrated to push a specific amount of filament per rotation. If the steps-per-mm (E-steps) value is wrong, the printer won't push the amount it thinks it's pushing. A misconfigured extruder can cause chronic under-extrusion across all your prints.

How to calibrate:

  1. Mark your filament 120mm from the extruder entrance
  2. In your printer's control panel, extrude 100mm of filament
  3. Measure how much filament was actually extruded (120 - remaining distance)
  4. If less than 100mm was extruded, increase E-steps proportionally
  5. Repeat until exactly 100mm is extruded when 100mm is commanded

Example: If you commanded 100mm but only 95mm came out, your E-steps need to increase by 100/95 = 5.3%

This is especially important after changing extruders or if you're using a printer that wasn't calibrated at the factory.

Cause 6: Bowden Tube Issues

For Bowden setups (where the extruder is separate from the hot end), the PTFE tube guides filament to the nozzle. If this tube is damaged, too long, or not seated properly, it adds resistance that causes under-extrusion.

Common Bowden problems:

  • Gap at the nozzle: The tube doesn't seat flush against the nozzle, creating a pocket where filament accumulates
  • Kinked tube: Sharp bends add friction
  • Worn tube: Inner surface becomes rough over time, increasing drag
  • Too long: Extra tube length means more friction

How to fix:

  • Cut the tube at a perfect 90° angle and reseat it firmly against the nozzle
  • Replace tubes that are more than a year old or show signs of wear
  • Route the tube with gentle curves, not sharp bends
  • Use high-quality Capricorn tubing for lower friction

Quick Diagnosis Table

Symptom Likely Cause Quick Fix
Filament curls from nozzle Nozzle clog Clean or replace nozzle
Popping sounds Wet filament Dry the filament
Clicking extruder Clog or temperature too low Check nozzle + increase temp
Consistent under-extrusion E-steps wrong Calibrate extruder
Worse at high speeds Temperature too low Increase nozzle temp

Conclusion

Under-extrusion has many causes, but the diagnosis process is systematic. Start with the easiest: check for nozzle clogs and increase temperature if needed. Then move to flow rate settings, filament condition, and extruder calibration. For Bowden printers, inspect the PTFE tube.

Most under-extrusion problems are solved by one of these six fixes. Work through them in order, and you'll be back to solid, strong prints quickly. The key is not to compensate with slicer settings — find and fix the root cause.
