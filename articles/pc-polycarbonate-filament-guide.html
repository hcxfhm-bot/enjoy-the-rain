Polycarbonate (PC) Filament Guide: The Strongest Printing Material

📅 June 1, 2026⏱️ 7 min read✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
💪

Polycarbonate (PC) is one of the strongest printable filaments available. It's heat-resistant, impact-resistant, and nearly indestructible — but it's also one of the most challenging materials to print successfully. This guide covers everything you need to know to get great PC prints.

Why Print with Polycarbonate?

  • Impact resistance — 10x stronger than PLA, won't shatter or crack
  • Heat resistance — Glass transition temp of 147°C (vs 60°C for PLA)
  • Optical clarity — Can be printed transparent with the right settings
  • Chemical resistance — Resists oils, alcohols, and many solvents

Required Equipment

PC is NOT beginner-friendly. You need:

  • An all-metal hotend capable of 300°C+
  • An enclosed printer with chamber temp of 60°C+
  • A heated bed that reaches 110-130°C
  • A PEI or Garolite build plate (PC won't stick to bare glass)

Optimized Print Settings

SettingValue
Nozzle Temperature270-310 °C
Bed Temperature110-130 °C
Chamber Temperature60-80 °C
Print Speed30-50 mm/s
Layer Height0.16-0.20 mm
Part Cooling Fan0-10% (minimize cooling)
Retraction1-2 mm (direct) / 4-6 mm (Bowden)

Common Problems and Solutions

  • Warping — The #1 issue. Use a brim (10+ lines), slow first layer (15 mm/s), and maximize chamber temp
  • Layer delamination — Caused by too-fast cooling. Keep the chamber hot and avoid opening the enclosure
  • Bed adhesion failure — Clean PEI plate with isopropyl alcohol, use a glue stick, and set bed to 120°C+
