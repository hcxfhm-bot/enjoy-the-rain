Home Assistant Automations for Beginners: 10 Easy Ways to Automate Your Home

📅 June 7, 2026 ⏱️ 12 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🏠

You installed Home Assistant. Your devices are connected. Now what? The real magic of Home Assistant isn't just controlling things from an app — it's making your home think for itself. That's what automations do.

In this guide, I'll walk you through 10 practical automations that actual beginners can set up today. No programming degree required — just a working Home Assistant instance and about 30 minutes.

If you haven't set up Home Assistant yet, start with our Getting Started with Home Assistant on Raspberry Pi guide first.

How Home Assistant Automations Work

Every automation in Home Assistant follows a simple three-part structure:

  • Trigger — What starts the automation (time, device state, sun position, etc.)
  • Condition (optional) — Rules that must be true for the automation to run
  • Action — What happens when the automation runs

Think of it like a sentence: "When [trigger], if [condition], then [action]."

Example: "When the sun sets, if someone is home, then turn on the living room lights."

You can create automations two ways: through the visual UI editor (Settings → Automations → Create Automation) or by writing YAML directly. Both produce the same result. The UI is great for beginners; YAML is faster once you're comfortable.

Automation #1: Lights On at Sunset

This is the "Hello World" of Home Assistant automations. It turns on your lights when the sun goes down, so you never come home to a dark house.

UI Setup

  1. Go to Settings → Automations & Scenes → Create Automation
  2. Click "Create new automation"
  3. Trigger: Sun → Sunset (offset: -00:30:00 to turn on 30 minutes before sunset)
  4. Action: Call service → light.turn_on, select your living room light
  5. Save and name it "Lights On at Sunset"

YAML Version

alias: "Lights On at Sunset"
trigger:
  - platform: sun
    event: sunset
    offset: "-00:30:00"
action:
  - service: light.turn_on
    target:
      entity_id: light.living_room

Automation #2: Lights Off When Everyone Leaves

No more "Did I leave the lights on?" anxiety. This automation detects when the last person leaves home and turns off all lights.

How It Works

It uses the zone integration and your phone's GPS (via the Home Assistant companion app) to track presence. When all tracked people leave the "home" zone, the automation fires.

alias: "All Lights Off When Away"
trigger:
  - platform: zone
    entity_id: person.you
    zone: zone.home
    event: leave
condition:
  - condition: zone
    entity_id: person.you
    zone: zone.home
  - condition: template
    value_template: "{{ state_attr('zone.home', 'persons') | length == 0 }}"
action:
  - service: light.turn_off
    target:
      entity_id: all

Pro tip: Add a 5-minute delay before turning off lights so you don't kill the lights if you're just taking out the trash.

Automation #3: Morning Routine

Wake up like you live in the future. This automation gradually turns on lights and starts your coffee maker at your chosen time — but only on weekdays.

alias: "Morning Routine"
trigger:
  - platform: time
    at: "06:30:00"
condition:
  - condition: time
    weekday:
      - mon
      - tue
      - wed
      - thu
      - fri
  - condition: state
    entity_id: person.you
    state: "home"
action:
  - service: light.turn_on
    target:
      entity_id: light.bedroom
    data:
      brightness: 10
      transition: 300
  - delay:
      minutes: 5
  - service: light.turn_on
    target:
      entity_id: light.kitchen
    data:
      brightness: 80
  - service: switch.turn_on
    target:
      entity_id: switch.coffee_maker

The transition: 300 makes the bedroom light fade in over 5 minutes — much gentler than a sudden flashlight at 6:30 AM.

Automation #4: Motion-Activated Night Light

Perfect for hallways and bathrooms. Turns on a dim light when motion is detected at night, then turns it off after no motion for 2 minutes.

alias: "Night Hallway Light"
trigger:
  - platform: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.hallway_motion
    to: "on"
condition:
  - condition: time
    after: "22:00:00"
    before: "06:00:00"
action:
  - service: light.turn_on
    target:
      entity_id: light.hallway
    data:
      brightness: 10
      kelvin: 2200
  - wait_for_trigger:
      - platform: state
        entity_id: binary_sensor.hallway_motion
        to: "off"
        for:
          minutes: 2
  - service: light.turn_off
    target:
      entity_id: light.hallway

The kelvin: 2200 gives a warm amber tone that won't blind you at 3 AM. If you want to build custom sensor housings for your motion sensors, check out our guide on 3D printing custom smart home device cases.

Automation #5: Temperature-Based Fan Control

Let your home manage its own comfort. When the temperature rises above your threshold, the fan turns on. When it drops, the fan turns off.

alias: "Auto Fan Control"
trigger:
  - platform: numeric_state
    entity_id: sensor.living_room_temperature
    above: 26
    id: "hot"
  - platform: numeric_state
    entity_id: sensor.living_room_temperature
    below: 24
    id: "cool"
action:
  - choose:
      - conditions:
          - condition: trigger
            id: "hot"
        sequence:
          - service: fan.turn_on
            target:
              entity_id: fan.living_room
      - conditions:
          - condition: trigger
            id: "cool"
        sequence:
          - service: fan.turn_off
            target:
              entity_id: fan.living_room

The 2-degree gap between on (26°C) and off (24°C) prevents the fan from rapidly cycling on and off — a technique called hysteresis.

Automation #6: Door Open Notification

Get a phone notification when a door or window is left open too long. Great for security and for not wasting AC.

alias: "Front Door Left Open Alert"
trigger:
  - platform: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.front_door
    to: "on"
    for:
      minutes: 10
action:
  - service: notify.mobile_app_your_phone
    data:
      title: "⚠️ Front Door Open"
      message: "The front door has been open for 10 minutes."

This requires a door sensor. You can use a cheap ESP32-based sensor running ESPHome or Tasmota — we cover that in our ESP32 smart home guide.

Automation #7: Movie Mode

One tap dims the lights, turns on the TV, and closes the blinds. This is the automation that makes guests say "Whoa."

alias: "Movie Mode"
trigger:
  - platform: state
    entity_id: input_boolean.movie_mode
    to: "on"
action:
  - service: light.turn_on
    target:
      entity_id: light.living_room
    data:
      brightness: 15
      kelvin: 2200
  - service: cover.close
    target:
      entity_id: cover.living_room_blinds
  - service: media_player.turn_on
    target:
      entity_id: media_player.living_room_tv
  - service: input_boolean.turn_off
    target:
      entity_id: input_boolean.movie_mode

The input_boolean acts as a virtual switch you can put on your dashboard. Tap it, and everything configures itself. The last action resets the switch so it's ready for next time.

Automation #8: Guest WiFi Password Reminder

When a guest arrives (detected via their phone connecting to your network or entering the home zone), automatically show the WiFi password on a dashboard or send it as a notification.

alias: "Guest Welcome"
trigger:
  - platform: state
    entity_id: person.guest
    to: "home"
action:
  - service: notify.mobile_app_your_phone
    data:
      title: "🎉 Guest Arrived"
      message: "Guest is home! WiFi: MyNetwork / Password: MyPass123"

Automation #9: Plant Watering Reminder

Using a soil moisture sensor (you can build one with an ESP32), get a reminder when your plants need water.

alias: "Plant Needs Water"
trigger:
  - platform: numeric_state
    entity_id: sensor.plant_soil_moisture
    below: 30
action:
  - service: notify.mobile_app_your_phone
    data:
      title: "🌱 Thirsty Plant"
      message: "Soil moisture is {{ states('sensor.plant_soil_moisture') }}%. Time to water!"

You can 3D print a waterproof enclosure for your soil sensor — see our smart home case printing guide for ideas.

Automation #10: Goodnight Routine

The ultimate "I'm going to bed" automation. One action that secures your home for the night.

alias: "Goodnight"
trigger:
  - platform: state
    entity_id: input_boolean.goodnight
    to: "on"
action:
  - service: light.turn_off
    target:
      entity_id: all
  - service: lock.lock
    target:
      entity_id: lock.front_door
  - service: cover.close
    target:
      entity_id: all
  - service: switch.turn_off
    target:
      entity_id:
        - switch.coffee_maker
        - switch.living_room_fan
  - service: notify.mobile_app_your_phone
    data:
      title: "🌙 Goodnight"
      message: "Everything is locked up. Sleep well!"
  - service: input_boolean.turn_off
    target:
      entity_id: input_boolean.goodnight

Using Blueprints for Faster Setup

If writing YAML feels like too much work, Blueprints are pre-made automations you can import and customize. Think of them as templates shared by the community.

How to Use Blueprints

  1. Go to Settings → Automations & Scenes → Blueprints
  2. Click "Import Blueprint"
  3. Paste the URL of a blueprint from the Home Assistant Community
  4. Create an automation from the blueprint and fill in your device names

Popular blueprints include: Z-Wave button controllers, presence-based lighting, and thermostat scheduling. The community has hundreds available for free.

Debugging Automations That Don't Work

Automations sometimes fail silently. Here's how to figure out why:

  1. Check the trace: Go to the automation → three dots → Traces. This shows you exactly what happened at each step.
  2. Test manually: Use the "Run" button on the automation to fire it immediately, bypassing the trigger.
  3. Check entity states: Go to Developer Tools → States to verify your sensors report the values you expect.
  4. Watch the log: Settings → System → Logs will show errors from your automations.
  5. Use conditions sparingly at first. Get the trigger+action working, then add conditions.

The most common beginner mistake? Using the wrong entity ID. Always copy entity IDs from Developer Tools → States, not from memory.

Tips for Building Better Automations

  • Start simple — Get the basic trigger→action working before adding conditions and delays
  • Name everything clearly — "Lights On" is bad; "Sunset Living Room Lights On" is good
  • Add descriptions — Future you will thank you when debugging at midnight
  • Use delays and wait — Not every action should happen at the same time
  • Test in stages — Don't build a 15-step automation and hope it all works
  • Keep a backup — Before editing a working automation, make a note of what you changed

For more advanced automation patterns (like using templates and variables), the Home Assistant documentation is your best friend. And if you want to expand your smart home with custom sensors and switches, our ESP32 smart home projects guide shows you how to build your own devices for under $5 each.

What You'll Need

To get started with these automations, here's what you need:

  • Home Assistant running on a Raspberry Pi or dedicated device (setup guide here)
  • Home Assistant Companion App on your phone (for presence detection and notifications)
  • Smart lights — Wi-Fi, Zigbee, or Z-Wave bulbs or switches
  • Sensors — Motion, door, temperature (ESP32-based DIY sensors work great)
  • A multimeter — for checking wiring on your DIY sensor builds (our multimeter guide)

Key Takeaways

  • Every automation = trigger + condition (optional) + action
  • Start with the UI editor, learn YAML when you're ready
  • Blueprints let you skip the hard work — use community templates
  • Use Traces to debug automations that don't behave as expected
  • Build automations in stages — test each part before adding complexity
  • The best automation is one you forget about because it just works

The beauty of Home Assistant is that you're never locked into someone else's idea of what your smart home should do. These 10 automations are just the beginning — once you understand the trigger/condition/action pattern, you can automate literally anything in your home.

Start with one or two that solve real problems in your daily routine. Once you see how they work, you'll find yourself automating everything. It's addictive — in the best way.
