3D Print First Layer Not Sticking? 7 Fixes That Actually Work

📅 May 8, 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🔧

Why the First Layer Matters Most

Nothing is more frustrating than watching your 3D printer lay down a perfect first few lines, only to see them curl up and detach halfway through. When your first layer doesn't stick, the entire print is doomed before it really begins. This is one of the most common problems for beginners, and it almost always has a fixable cause.

The first layer is the foundation for everything that follows. If it doesn't bond to the build plate properly, you'll get warping, shifted layers, or a pile of spaghetti instead of the part you intended. The good news? First layer adhesion problems are almost always solvable with the right adjustments. This guide walks through the seven most effective fixes, starting with the most common culprits.

Fix 1: Check Your Bed Leveling

Improper bed leveling is the #1 cause of first layer problems. If your nozzle is too far from the bed, the filament won't squish into the surface — it'll just sit on top and peel away. If it's too close, you'll get clogged nozzles and ugly ridges.

The paper test: Most printers recommend sliding a piece of standard printer paper (0.1mm thick) between the nozzle and bed. You should feel slight resistance when pulling the paper, but it shouldn't tear or get stuck. Do this at all four corners and the center of your bed.

Signs your nozzle is too far:

  • Lines are round instead of flat
  • Gaps between adjacent lines
  • Filament comes off easily when you touch it

Signs your nozzle is too close:

  • Filament spreads too wide, creating ridges
  • Nozzle drags through previous lines
  • Extruder clicking or grinding sounds

If your printer has automatic bed leveling (like the Bambu Lab A1 Mini), run the calibration sequence again. Even auto-leveling systems need occasional recalibration, especially after moving the printer or changing nozzles.

Fix 2: Clean Your Build Plate

You'd be surprised how often a "sticking problem" is actually a "dirty plate problem." Oils from your fingers, dust, and filament residue accumulate on the build surface over time, creating an invisible barrier that prevents adhesion.

Quick fix: Wipe your build plate with isopropyl alcohol (70% or higher) and a lint-free cloth. Do this before every print if you're having issues. For PEI sheets, a light scrub with dish soap and warm water works even better — just make sure the plate is completely dry before printing.

What to avoid:

  • Don't touch the print surface with bare hands — handle edges only
  • Avoid paper towels that leave lint behind
  • Don't use glass cleaner or other household chemicals

For textured PEI or powder-coated plates, a clean surface is usually all you need for excellent PLA and PETG adhesion. For smooth surfaces, you may need additional aids (see Fix 4).

Fix 3: Adjust Your First Layer Temperature

Temperature affects how well filament flows and bonds. If your bed temperature is too low, the plastic won't adhere properly. If it's too high, you might get "elephant's foot" — a widened first layer that looks squashed.

Recommended bed temperatures for first layer:

  • PLA: 55–65°C (60°C is a safe starting point)
  • PETG: 70–80°C
  • ABS: 90–110°C (with enclosure)
  • TPU: 50–60°C

The first layer benefits from slightly higher temperatures than the rest of the print. Many slicers have a "first layer bed temperature" setting separate from subsequent layers — try increasing this by 5°C.

Also check your nozzle temperature. A slightly hotter first layer (5–10°C above normal) helps the filament flow and bond better. Just don't go too high, or you'll get stringing and oozing.

Fix 4: Use Adhesion Aids

Sometimes a clean, level bed isn't enough — especially with materials that naturally resist adhesion (like PETG on smooth surfaces) or prints with small contact areas. Adhesion aids create a bonding layer between your filament and the build plate.

Common options:

  • Glue stick: Cheap, effective, works on almost any surface. Apply a thin, even layer. Let it dry before printing. Clean with water afterward.
  • Hair spray: Works well for PLA. Apply a light mist to a warm bed, let it get tacky before starting. Avoid brands with heavy fragrances or additives.
  • Blue painter's tape: Classic solution for PLA on bare aluminum or glass. The texture provides grip. Replace when it starts peeling.
  • PEI sheet: The gold standard for most materials. Textured PEI holds PLA and PETG without any additional aids.
  • Magigoo or specialized adhesives: Designed specifically for 3D printing. More expensive but reliable across many materials.

Start with the simplest option (glue stick) and work up if needed. For most users, a textured PEI sheet eliminates the need for any adhesives entirely.

Fix 5: Slow Down Your First Layer

Printing the first layer too fast is a common beginner mistake. When the nozzle moves quickly, it doesn't give the filament enough time to bond with the bed. The plastic gets dragged along instead of staying put.

Recommended first layer speed:

  • PLA: 15–25 mm/s (about 50% of normal print speed)
  • PETG: 15–20 mm/s
  • TPU: 10–15 mm/s

Most slicers have a dedicated "first layer speed" setting. Set this to 20 mm/s or lower when you're having adhesion problems. You can speed up after the first few layers are down.

Also check your "initial layer flow rate" — setting this to 100–105% can help squish the filament into the bed for better contact.

Fix 6: Check for Drafts and Temperature Changes

If your first layer sticks initially but lifts during the print, temperature fluctuations might be the culprit. A draft from an air conditioner, open window, or fan can cool the bed unevenly, causing the edges to curl.

Solutions:

  • Move your printer away from vents, windows, and doors
  • Use an enclosure for ABS, ASA, and other warp-prone materials
  • Avoid printing in rooms with significant temperature swings
  • For PLA, a simple cardboard windbreak can help

This is especially important if you're experiencing warping with PETG or ABS — these materials shrink more as they cool, making them sensitive to temperature changes.

Fix 7: Add a Brim or Raft

When all else fails, give your print a larger footprint. Brims and rafts extend the contact area, making it harder for parts to peel off the bed.

Brim vs. Raft:

  • Brim: A flat ring of filament around your print's base. Adds surface area without much waste. Easy to remove. Best for parts with small footprints or sharp corners.
  • Raft: A thick base printed underneath your part. Provides excellent adhesion but uses more material and leaves a rougher bottom surface. Reserve for difficult materials or problematic geometries.

In most slicers, you can enable brims with one click. A 5–8mm brim is usually enough. For rafts, use them sparingly — they're overkill for most PLA prints but can save the day with ABS or nylon.

Quick Troubleshooting Checklist

Before your next print, run through this list:

  • ✅ Bed leveled with paper test (slight resistance)
  • ✅ Build plate cleaned with isopropyl alcohol
  • ✅ First layer bed temp 5–10°C higher than normal
  • ✅ First layer speed set to 20 mm/s or slower
  • ✅ No drafts hitting the printer
  • ✅ Adhesion aid applied if needed (glue stick, tape, PEI)
  • ✅ Brim enabled for small-footprint prints

Run through these seven fixes in order. In 90% of cases, the solution is in the first three: level the bed, clean it, and adjust temperature. The remaining fixes handle edge cases and difficult materials.

Conclusion

First layer adhesion problems are frustrating but solvable. Start with bed leveling and plate cleanliness — these two factors account for most failures. Adjust temperature and speed if the basics don't work. Add adhesion aids or brims for challenging prints.

Once you dial in your first layer, you'll know it: the lines will be flat and slightly squished, they'll connect to each other without gaps, and the print will stay put when you gently tug at the edges. Master the first layer, and your print success rate will skyrocket.
