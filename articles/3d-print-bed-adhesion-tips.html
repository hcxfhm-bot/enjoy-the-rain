3D Print Bed Adhesion: Glue Stick, Tape, Hairspray & More Tips

📅 May 24, 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🔧

Nothing is more frustrating than watching your print peel off the bed 30 minutes in. Bed adhesion is the foundation of every successful 3D print — if the first layer doesn't stick, nothing else matters. This guide covers every adhesion method from glue sticks to PEI sheets, when to use each, and which filaments they work best with.

Why Prints Don't Stick

Before fixing adhesion, understand what causes it:

  • Uneven bed: If the nozzle is too far in one corner, the filament won't squish into the surface. Level your bed first — this solves 50% of adhesion issues.
  • Dirty bed: Finger oils and dust create a barrier. Clean with isopropyl alcohol before every print.
  • Wrong bed temperature: PLA needs 55–60°C, PETG 70–80°C, ABS 100–110°C. Too cold = no stick. Too hot = elephant foot.
  • Material mismatch: Some filaments (nylon, PETG) need specific surfaces or adhesives.

Method 1: PEI Sheet (Best Overall)

A PEI (polyetherimide) sheet is the gold standard for bed adhesion. It's a smooth, slightly textured metal surface that grips most filaments without any extra adhesives.

  • PLA: Sticks great at 55–60°C. Releases on its own when the bed cools below 40°C.
  • PETG: Sticks too well — can rip chunks out of the PEI surface. Apply a thin glue stick layer as a release agent.
  • ABS/ASA: Sticks well at 100–110°C on PEI.
  • Nylon: Needs glue stick on PEI for reliable adhesion.

Tip: Textured PEI gives a nice matte bottom finish. Smooth PEI gives a glossy finish. Both grip equally well.

Method 2: Glue Stick (Most Versatile)

A basic PVA glue stick (Elmer's, UHU, or generic) is the most popular adhesion aid. It works as both a sticking agent and a release agent — sounds contradictory, but it's true.

  • For PLA: Optional. PEI alone usually works. But glue stick helps if your bed is worn.
  • For PETG: Use as a release agent. A thin layer prevents PETG from bonding permanently to PEI.
  • For ABS: Helps on glass and PEI surfaces.
  • For Nylon: Almost required. Nylon barely sticks without it.

How to apply: Draw a thin, even layer while the bed is cold. Let it dry for 30 seconds. Reapply every 3–5 prints. Wash off with warm water when buildup gets thick.

Method 3: Painter's Tape (Budget Classic)

Blue painter's tape (3M ScotchBlue 2090) was the go-to before PEI sheets became common. It still works well, especially on glass beds.

  • For PLA: Very reliable on unheated or lightly heated beds (50°C). The texture grips PLA well.
  • For PETG: Works, but PETG can pull the tape off the glass if adhesion is too strong.
  • Not for ABS/Nylon: These need heated beds that exceed tape's adhesive tolerance.

Tip: Replace tape when it tears or gets glossy. Worn tape loses its grip. Overlap strips slightly to avoid gaps where filament can slip under.

Method 4: Hairspray (Quick & Easy)

Aquaji-style hairspray (cheap, extra-hold, unscented) is a fast adhesion booster. Spray it on a warm bed, wait 30 seconds, and print.

  • For PLA: Works on glass and PEI. Light coat is enough.
  • For PETG: Can help, but glue stick is more predictable as a release agent.
  • For ABS: Common method on glass beds before PEI became standard.

Downsides: Overspray gets everywhere. Builds up over time and needs cleaning with alcohol. Not as precise as glue stick for tricky spots.

Method 5: Dedicated Adhesives (Magigoo, 3DLac)

These are commercial adhesives designed specifically for 3D printing:

  • Magigoo: Comes in different formulas for different filaments (PLA, PETG, PolyPro for nylon/PP). Best consistency and easiest to apply.
  • 3DLac: Spray adhesive popular in Europe. Works similar to hairspray but with better formulation.
  • Dimafix: Pen-style applicator. Good for spot application on tricky corners.

Worth it? If you print daily or use tricky materials like Nylon, the $10–$15 investment saves frustration. For occasional PLA printing, glue stick is fine.

Method 6: Garolite (G10) for Nylon

Garolite (also called G10 or FR4) is a fiberglass-epoxy laminate that nylon bonds to extremely well. It's cheap ($10–$15 for a sheet) and reusable. Heat the bed to 70°C and nylon sticks without any adhesives.

This is a niche option — only worth it if you print a lot of nylon. For everything else, PEI + glue stick covers your needs.

Pro Tips for Bulletproof Adhesion

  • Always use a brim for parts with small footprints. A 5mm brim adds enormous holding power.
  • Clean the bed between prints. IPA (70%+) on a paper towel removes oils and old adhesive.
  • Slow down the first layer. 15–20 mm/s for the first layer gives filament time to bond.
  • Use ABL — auto bed leveling compensates for minor unevenness.
  • Check first layer height. You should see a slight squish — the line should be flat, not round. Too round = nozzle too high. Too thin = nozzle too low.

Conclusion

Start with a clean PEI sheet and proper bed leveling — that solves most adhesion problems. Add glue stick when printing PETG (as a release agent) or nylon (as a sticking agent). Use painter's tape if you're on a glass bed without PEI. And if your first layer still isn't sticking, re-level the bed before trying anything else. Most adhesion "problems" are really leveling problems in disguise.
