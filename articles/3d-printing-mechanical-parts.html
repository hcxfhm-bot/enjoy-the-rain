3D Printing Mechanical Parts: Gears, Pulleys & Brackets

📅 June 1, 2026⏱️ 7 min read✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
⚙️

Printing decorative models is fun, but printing functional mechanical parts is where 3D printing truly shines. A well-designed and properly printed gear or bracket can replace machined parts at a fraction of the cost. Here's how to do it right.

Material Selection for Mechanical Parts

MaterialBest ForStrength Notes
PETGGeneral mechanical partsGood impact resistance, easy to print
NylonGears, bearings, wear surfacesSelf-lubricating, very durable
PCHigh-temp, high-load partsStrongest common FDM material
Carbon Fiber PETG/NylonStiff brackets, jigsRigid but brittle — no flex

Gear Design Tips

  • Module size: Minimum module 1.5 for FDM (smaller teeth don't print well)
  • Pressure angle: 20° standard (easier to print than 14.5°)
  • Clearance: Add 0.2mm backlash between meshing gears
  • Orientation: Print gears flat (teeth parallel to bed) for maximum tooth strength
  • Hub: Add a hub with a set screw hole for shaft attachment

Bracket Design Tips

  • Fillet all internal corners — Sharp corners are stress concentrators
  • Use 4+ perimeters for wall strength
  • 50%+ infill with Gyroid pattern for omnidirectional strength
  • Use heat-set inserts rather than printed threads for bolt holes
