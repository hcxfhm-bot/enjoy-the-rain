How to Level a 3D Printer Bed: Paper Method, Knobs & Tips

📅 May 21, 2026 ⏱️ 10 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
📏

Bed leveling is the #1 skill every 3D printing beginner must master. A poorly leveled bed causes first layer adhesion failures, nozzle gouging, and wasted filament. In this guide, you'll learn the foolproof "paper method" for manual bed leveling, how to adjust your bed knobs correctly, and pro tips to keep your bed level for weeks at a time.

What Is Bed Leveling (And Why Is It So Important)?

Your 3D printer's nozzle must maintain a consistent distance from the build surface—usually 0.1mm to 0.2mm (about the thickness of a sheet of paper). Bed leveling adjusts the four corners of your print bed so the entire surface is coplanar with the nozzle's movement plane.

Even a 0.05mm height difference across the bed causes:

  • First layer not sticking (nozzle too far)
  • Nozzle dragging / gouging (nozzle too close)
  • Uneven layer lines (visible defects on vertical walls)
  • Failed prints (warping, detachment after 10-30 layers)

If you're struggling with first layer calibration, bed leveling is the first thing to check.

Pro Tip: Use a feeler gauge (0.1mm) instead of paper. Printer paper thickness varies by brand and humidity, leading to inconsistent leveling.

Step-by-Step: The Paper Method (Manual Bed Leveling)

This method works on any 3D printer—Ender 3, Prusa MK4, AnyCubic, Bambu Lab, you name it. It takes 5-10 minutes once you get the hang of it.

Step 1: Preheat Your Bed (Critical!)

Set your bed temperature to your typical printing temperature:

  • PLA: 60°C
  • PETG: 75°C
  • ABS: 100°C

Why? Aluminum and glass beds expand when heated. Leveling a cold bed gives false results—your perfectly leveled bed will be 0.1-0.2mm off once it heats up.

Step 2: Home All Axes

Use your printer's LCD menu: Prepare → Auto Home (or send G28 via G-code). This moves the nozzle to the starting position (X=0, Y=0, Z=0).

Step 3: Move Nozzle to the First Corner

Navigate to the front-left corner using your LCD's move controls. Typical coordinates:

  • Ender 3 / CR-10: X=30, Y=30
  • Prusa MK4: X=25, Y=25
  • Bambu Lab P1P: X=20, Y=20

Adjust these numbers based on your bed size and probe offset. You want the nozzle ~30mm from each edge.

Step 4: Adjust the Bed Knob (Feel for Resistance)

Slide a sheet of printer paper between the nozzle and the bed. Then:

  1. If the paper slides freely with zero resistance: The bed is too low. Tighten the bed knob (turn clockwise) by ¼ turn.
  2. If the paper won't move at all: The bed is too high. Loosen the bed knob (turn counterclockwise) by ¼ turn.
  3. If the paper slides with slight resistance (you can feel it dragging): Perfect! Move to the next corner.

Key Test: You should be able to pull the paper out, but it should feel like it's grazing the nozzle tip. Too easy = too far. Too hard = too close.

Step 5: Repeat for ALL Four Corners (Then Do It Again!)

Beds flex. Adjusting the front-left knob changes the pressure on the entire bed, affecting the other three corners. Here's the correct sequence:

  1. Front-Left → adjust
  2. Front-Right → adjust
  3. Back-Right → adjust
  4. Back-Left → adjust
  5. Repeat the entire sequence 2 more times.

By the third pass, the bed will be stable and level.

Step 6: Check the Center (Optional but Recommended)

Move the nozzle to the center of the bed (X=110, Y=110 for a 220x220mm bed). The paper should have the same resistance as the corners.

If the center is too high: Your bed is concave (warped upward in the middle). You'll need manual mesh bed leveling to compensate.

If the center is too low: Your bed is convex (warped downward in the middle). Again, mesh leveling is the fix.

Step 7: Do a Test Print

Print a single-layer square (20mm x 20mm) in each corner and the center. The lines should be slightly squished (flat on top, not round). If any spot looks like a thin round string, re-level that corner.

How to Adjust Bed Knobs Correctly (Avoid This Common Mistake!)

Most beginners spin bed knobs randomly and wonder why nothing changes. Here's the right way:

Clockwise = Tighten = Bed Moves UP

Imagine the knob is a screw. Turning it clockwise lifts that corner of the bed (shortens the spring).

Counter-Clockwise = Loosen = Bed Moves DOWN

Turning counterclockwise lowers the bed corner (lengthens the spring).

Don't Fully Compress the Springs!

If your bed springs are squished completely flat, you have zero adjustment range. The fix:

  1. Loosen all four knobs until the springs are slightly extended.
  2. Re-level from scratch.
  3. If your springs are too weak (bed vibrates loose), upgrade to yellow, high-tension springs ($10 on Amazon).

Bed Leveling vs Bed Tramming (What's the Difference?)

You'll hear both terms. Here's the distinction:

  • Bed Tramming: Adjusting the bed so it's parallel to the X-axis gantry. This is what you do with the knobs.
  • Bed Leveling: The broader process of ensuring the nozzle's Z-height is correct across the entire bed. Includes tramming + Z-offset adjustment.

In practice, everyone says "bed leveling" even when they mean "tramming." Use whichever term—we'll know what you mean.

5 Pro Tips for Keeping Your Bed Level Longer

1. Upgrade to Silicone Bed Mounts

Replace your bed springs with silicone spacers (Creality sells a kit for $15). Silicone doesn't vibrate loose like springs. Your bed stays level for months.

2. Use a Feeler Gauge (More Consistent Than Paper)

Printer paper thickness varies from 0.07mm to 0.11mm depending on brand and humidity. A $10 feeler gauge gives you a precise 0.1mm reference every time.

3. Tighten Your Bed Knobs (Then Back Off ¼ Turn)

After leveling, tighten each knob by hand until snug, then back off ¼ turn. This "preloads" the spring so vibrations don't loosen it.

4. Add a BLTouch or CR Touch (Auto Bed Leveling)

If you hate manual leveling, install an auto bed leveling sensor. A $35 BLTouch probes the bed automatically before every print.

5. Check Bed Level Before Every Long Print

Before a 10-hour print, run a quick 2-minute bed level check. It's better than waking up to a spaghetti mess.

Troubleshooting: "I Leveled My Bed, But Prints Still Fail!"

Problem 1: First Layer Is Perfect in Corners, Bad in Center

Cause: Warped bed (concave or convex).

Fix: Use manual mesh bed leveling (Marlin firmware) or upgrade to a glass bed (flatter than aluminum).

Problem 2: Bed Won't Stay Level (Needs Re-Leveling Every Print)

Cause: Weak springs, vibrations loosening knobs.

Fix: Upgrade to yellow high-tension springs ($10) or silicone bed mounts ($15). Add nylon lock nuts to the bed knobs (prevents vibration loosening).

Problem 3: Nozzle Gouges the Bed (Too Close)

Cause: Z-offset too low, or bed leveled incorrectly.

Fix: Raise the Z-offset by 0.05mm (M851 Z-1.95 instead of M851 Z-2.0). Re-level with slightly thicker paper (2 sheets instead of 1).

Problem 4: First Layer Won't Stick (Nozzle Too Far)

Cause: Z-offset too high, or bed too far from nozzle.

Fix: Lower Z-offset by 0.05mm. Re-level with a single sheet of thinner paper (or a feeler gauge).

When to Upgrade to Auto Bed Leveling (ABL)

Manual bed leveling is a rite of passage, but it gets old fast. Consider upgrading to ABL if:

  • You level your bed more than once a week
  • Your bed is warped and mesh leveling isn't enough
  • You want to start prints and walk away (no pre-print leveling checks)

Best ABL Sensors in 2026:

  • BLTouch V3.1 ($40) — Gold standard, works on any printer
  • Creality CR Touch ($30) — Cheaper BLTouch clone, works great on Creality printers
  • Euclid Probe ($60) — Premium, deployable probe for Voron/CoreXY builds

Conclusion: Practice Makes Perfect

Bed leveling feels impossible the first time. By the 10th time, it's second nature. Stick with it—every 3D printing expert started exactly where you are now.

Quick Recap:

  1. Preheat your bed to printing temperature
  2. Use the paper method (0.1mm feeler gauge is even better)
  3. Adjust all four corners, then repeat 2 more times
  4. Do a test print (single-layer squares)
  5. Upgrade to yellow springs or silicone mounts to make it last
