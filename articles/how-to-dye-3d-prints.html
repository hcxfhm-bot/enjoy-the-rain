How to Dye 3D Prints: Color Techniques for PLA and Nylon

📅 June 1, 2026⏱️ 6 min read✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🌈

Dyeing 3D prints is an alternative to painting that produces deep, uniform color that penetrates the surface rather than sitting on top. It won't chip, scratch off, or show brush marks. But it only works well with certain materials.

Which Materials Can Be Dyed?

MaterialDye ResultBest Dye Type
Nylon⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ExcellentAcid dye, RIT DyeMore
PLA⭐⭐⭐ Good (light colors only)RIT All-Purpose, disperse dye
PETG⭐⭐ FairRIT DyeMore (requires high temp)
Resin⭐⭐ FairAlcohol-based dye
ABS⭐ Poor (warp risk)Not recommended

Dyeing Nylon (Best Results)

  1. Bring a pot of water to 82°C (180°F)
  2. Add RIT DyeMore (synthetic fiber dye) — 1 bottle per 3 gallons
  3. Add a tablespoon of white vinegar (helps dye bond)
  4. Submerge the print and stir for 30-60 minutes
  5. Rinse in warm water until water runs clear
  6. Let dry completely

Dyeing PLA

PLA dyeing is trickier because PLA starts to soften at 60°C. Keep the dye bath below 55°C and extend the soaking time to 2-4 hours. Light colors (yellow, pink, light blue) work best. Dark colors may come out uneven.

For other coloring methods, see our painting guide and filament brands guide.
