Common Resin Print Failures and How to Fix Them

📅 May 26, 2026 ⏱️ 10 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🔧

Resin 3D printing rewards patience and attention to detail, but when things go wrong, they can go very wrong. A failed print wastes resin, time, and can be frustrating for beginners.

The good news? Most resin print failures follow predictable patterns and have straightforward solutions. This guide covers the most common issues you'll encounter and how to fix them.

Understanding Resin Print Failures

Before diving into specific problems, it helps to understand the three main categories of failure:

  • Adhesion failures: The print doesn't stick to the build plate or supports
  • Structural failures: The print separates, warps, or collapses during printing
  • Quality failures: The print adheres but has surface or detail problems

Each failure type has different causes and solutions, so let's tackle them one by one.

1. Print Not Adhering to Build Plate

This is the most common failure, especially for beginners. Your print fails to stick and often detaches partway through, potentially damaging your FEP film.

Causes and Solutions

Incorrect Bottom Exposure Time

Problem: The initial layers don't get enough UV exposure to bond properly.

Solution: Increase bottom exposure time in your slicer. Start with 30-60 seconds for standard resin and adjust based on results. If prints are still not adhering, increase in 10-second increments.

Build Plate Not Level

Problem: Uneven first layer means parts of the model aren't making proper contact.

Solution: Re-level your build plate. Most printers have automated or semi-automated leveling processes—use them. If manual, follow your manufacturer's guide precisely.

Contaminated FEP Film

Problem: Old resin or debris on the FEP film prevents clean release.

Solution: Clean the FEP film with isopropyl alcohol and a soft cloth. Replace if scratched or heavily clouded.

Wrong Bottom Layer Count

Problem: Not enough bottom layers to establish good adhesion.

Solution: Increase bottom layer count to 4-6 layers for most prints.

2. Supports Not Adhering to Model

Your print sticks to the build plate, but the model separates from the supports, leaving you with a ghostly hollow shell.

Causes and Solutions

Insufficient Support Contact Points

Problem: Supports don't have enough surface area touching the model.

Solution: Use auto-generation tools with appropriate density settings. For detailed prints, add manual supports to critical areas. Ensure supports have contact points of at least 2-3mm diameter.

Support Tip Too Small

Problem: Point supports sink into the model during printing.

Solution: Use heavier support tips (0.8-1.0mm) especially for flexible or tough resins. Consider switching to "sacrificial" bases that get removed during cleanup.

Incorrect Support Exposure

Problem: Support tips cure before touching the model, preventing fusion.

Solution: Enable "separate support exposure" in your slicer and set support tip exposure to 0 (no pre-cure) or use washable supports designed for this purpose.

3. Layer Delamination

Your print looks fine during printing, but later you notice layers separating—like a half-melted sandwich.

Causes and Solutions

Low Cure Power (Underexposure)

Problem: Each layer doesn't fully cure, creating a weak bond with the next.

Solution: Increase normal exposure time by 2-4 seconds. Print an exposure test cube to find optimal settings for your specific resin and printer.

Releasing Force Too Strong

Problem: The peeling force during the build plate's upward movement separates weakly-cured layers.

Solution: Use a slower lift speed for the first few layers, or switch to a flexible FEP film system if your printer supports it.

Temperature Issues

Problem: Printing in a cold environment prevents proper curing.

Solution: Maintain room temperature between 68-77°F (20-25°C). Colder environments slow or prevent complete curing.

4. Elephant Foot

The bottom of your print flares outward, creating a rounded, elephant-like foot. This is common on resin prints and especially noticeable on models that need to fit together.

Causes and Solutions

Bottom Layers Overexposed

Problem: Too much UV exposure on bottom layers causes them to swell slightly before curing.

Solution: Reduce bottom exposure time. If your slicer supports it, use a dedicated "bottom" exposure that's slightly lower than standard.

Insufficient Bottom Layer Lift Height

Problem: The build plate doesn't lift high enough before the peel, squishing the first layers.

Solution: Increase bottom layer lift height slightly. The build plate should lift 6-8mm minimum to allow proper release.

Post-Processing: Sand the Foot

Quick Fix: For tolerance-critical prints, sand the foot carefully after curing. Start with 400 grit and work up to 800 grit.

5. Incomplete Details

Fine details, sharp edges, or small text don't print properly—they're either missing or melted-looking.

Causes and Solutions

Underexposure

Problem: Not enough UV to fully cure fine details.

Solution: Increase exposure time. Fine details need slightly more exposure than standard layers to ensure complete curing of small cross-sections.

Resolution Mismatch

Problem: Details are smaller than your printer's pixel size.

Solution: Check your slicer's "minimum detail size" setting and ensure your model details are above this threshold. For extremely fine details, consider a higher-resolution printer.

Poor Support Placement

Problem: Fine details break during separation because they lack support.

Solution: Add manual supports specifically for delicate areas. Position supports to hold thin sections without obscuring the detail itself.

6. Surface Scarring and Lines

Your prints have visible lines, scars, or inconsistent surfaces that shouldn't be there.

Causes and Solutions

Dirty or Scratched FEP Film

Problem: Debris or scratches on the FEP cause blemishes in every print.

Solution: Inspect your FEP film under good lighting. Clean with IPA or replace if damaged.

Resin Flow Issues

Problem: Resin doesn't flow properly to fill gaps between layers.

Solution: Increase bottom lift and retract speeds slightly. Ensure your vat isn't nearly empty—low resin levels can cause incomplete fills.

Contaminated Resin

Problem: Old resin or resin mixed with debris from failed prints.

Solution: Strain your resin through a fine mesh filter before use. Replace resin that shows signs of degradation (thickening, color change, poor curing).

7. Resin Puddling and Incomplete Release

Resin collects in certain areas and doesn't fully release from the FEP, causing deformation or failed prints.

Causes and Solutions

High Viscosity Resin

Problem: Thick resin doesn't flow fast enough during printing.

Solution: Warm the resin slightly (not above 35°C/95°F) to reduce viscosity. Alternatively, switch to a resin formulated for easier flow.

Insufficient Release Film Lubrication

Problem: The FEP film becomes sticky with repeated use.

Solution: Apply a thin layer of FEP release spray or silicone oil between prints. Some users swear by a thin application of petroleum jelly.

8. Supports Fused to Model

You can't remove supports without taking chunks out of your print.

Causes and Solutions

Overexposed Supports

Problem: Supports cure so hard they bond permanently to the model.

Solution: Reduce support tip exposure time. In Chitubox and Lychee, look for "light off delay" or "gradient settings" for supports.

Supports in Inaccessible Areas

Problem: You can't reach supports to remove them without damaging the print.

Solution: Reorient your model in the slicer to place supports in accessible areas. Consider a rotatable hinge system if your printer supports one.

General Troubleshooting Tips

Keep a Print Log

Record your settings for every print—resin type, exposure times, temperature, and orientation. When something works, you'll know why.

Test Regularly

Print an exposure test chart monthly to verify your settings haven't drifted. Resin ages, LCDs degrade, and temperatures change—all affecting optimal settings.

Work Clean

Most failures trace back to contamination: dirty FEP, old resin, temperature fluctuations. A clean workspace prevents most problems.

Conclusion

Resin printing failures are frustrating but usually solvable. Keep detailed notes, test regularly, and don't be afraid to experiment with settings. Each printer and resin combination is slightly different.
