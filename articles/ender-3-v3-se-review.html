Ender 3 V3 SE Review: Still the Best Beginner Printer in 2026?

📅 May 3, 2026 ⏱️ 5 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
⭐

The Creality Ender 3 series has sold over 5 million units. That's not a typo — it's genuinely the most popular beginner 3D printer line in the world. The V3 SE, released in late 2024 and still the current model in 2026, is Creality's answer to one question: "How do we make the Ender 3 so easy that total beginners never get frustrated?"

After weeks of printing with it — PLA benchies, PETG functional parts, TPU phone cases — here's what you actually get for your $149.

Unboxing & Assembly: 25 Minutes From Box to Print

Creality ships the V3 SE as a semi-assembled kit. The base frame, gantry, and heated bed come pre-assembled. You attach the gantry to the base (4 screws), connect the display cable (1 plug), mount the spool holder, and you're done. No soldering, no crimping connectors, no reading a 40-page manual.

The included instructions are a single large-format poster with clear illustrations. Took me exactly 22 minutes from opening the box to powering on. If you've never touched a 3D printer before, budget 30-40 minutes and you'll still have it built.

What's In the Box

  • Pre-assembled base frame with Y-axis rail, heated bed, and power supply
  • Pre-assembled XZ gantry with direct drive extruder and CR-Touch bed sensor
  • Creality-branded LCD control knob (rotary encoder, not touchscreen)
  • 200g sample PLA spool (enough for maybe 2 benchies — buy a real spool)
  • Full set of hex keys, wrenches, side cutters, and spare nozzle

Auto Bed Leveling: The Feature That Changes Everything

The original Ender 3 required manually turning four knobs and sliding a piece of paper under the nozzle — and beginners got this wrong constantly. The V3 SE includes Creality's CR-Touch sensor, which probes a 4x4 grid of 16 points across the bed before every print.

Here's the thing: it actually works. I ran 11 consecutive prints without touching a single screw. The mesh compensation adjusts the Z-axis in real time during the first layer. The one caveat — you might need to fine-tune the Z-offset (distance between nozzle and bed) once. Out of the box, mine was -1.47mm, which worked perfectly for PLA.

If your first layer isn't sticking, don't immediately blame the sensor — check our bed adhesion guide first. Most issues come down to a dirty bed (wipe it with isopropyl alcohol) or wrong temperatures.

Print Quality: What $149 Gets You in 2026

PLA (Standard Test Prints)

At 0.2mm layer height with standard 50mm/s speed, PLA prints look excellent. Sharp corners, clean vertical walls, no ghosting or ringing at default speeds. Benchy prints come out with readable text on the bottom and minimal stringing. Overture PLA at 200°C nozzle / 60°C bed produced the best results in my testing.

PETG (Surprisingly Capable)

This is where the V3 SE's direct drive extruder pays off. PETG at 240°C / 80°C bed printed cleanly with just a little extra stringing. You won't get Prusa MK4-level perfection, but functional mechanical parts — brackets, mounts, clips — came out strong and dimensionally accurate within 0.15mm.

TPU (95A Shore Hardness)

Flexible filaments worked on the first try with slow speeds (20mm/s) and 220°C. The direct drive path means the filament doesn't buckle inside a Bowden tube. Printed a flexible phone case that fit perfectly. If you're curious about flexible materials, our filament guide explains TPU settings in full detail.

The V3 SE isn't just a minor tweak — it fixes every pain point beginners had with the V2. The jump from Bowden to direct drive alone makes it a fundamentally different machine.

The Downsides (Let's Be Honest)

No printer at this price is perfect. Here's what you need to know before buying:

  • No WiFi. You transfer files via SD card. That's fine for beginners, but some $150 machines (looking at you, Neptune 4) include network printing. Creality sells a WiFi box separately for $25.
  • Manual filament loading. You run the "load filament" menu item and push the filament by hand until it oozes. It's not automatic like a Bambu Lab.
  • Fan noise. At full speed, the cooling fan hits about 48 decibels. Not unbearable, but you won't put this in your bedroom overnight.
  • Sample filament is tiny. The included 200g spool prints about 2-3 small objects. Order a real 1kg PLA spool at the same time you buy the printer.
  • No enclosure. Printing ABS requires a DIY enclosure (cardboard box works in a pinch), but for PLA and PETG, you don't need one.

Who Should Buy the Ender 3 V3 SE

Buy it if: You're a complete beginner who wants a proven, reliable printer under $150. You don't mind SD card transfers. You want access to the largest 3D printing community on the internet for help and mods.

Skip it if: You want the absolute fastest prints and can spend $199 on the Bambu Lab A1 Mini. You need WiFi printing built-in (the Neptune 4 does this at $165). You only want to print ABS or ASA (you need an enclosed printer at this point).

Final Verdict: 9/10

At $149, the Ender 3 V3 SE is the safest recommendation for a first 3D printer in 2026. It's not the fastest, not the quietest, and not the most automated — but it prints beautifully, auto-levels perfectly, and is backed by the largest community in 3D printing. Creality finally made an Ender 3 you can unbox and trust.
