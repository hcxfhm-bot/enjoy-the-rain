3D Printing vs CNC Machining: Which Is Right for Your Project?

📅 June 1, 2026⏱️ 7 min read✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🔩

3D printing (additive) and CNC machining (subtractive) are fundamentally different approaches to making parts. Understanding when to use each can save you time, money, and frustration.

Head-to-Head Comparison

Factor3D Printing (FDM)CNC Machining
MaterialThermoplastics onlyMetals, wood, plastics, composites
ComplexityInternal cavities, organic shapesLimited by tool access
Precision±0.2mm±0.01mm
Surface FinishLayer linesSmooth, machined finish
StrengthAnisotropic (weak between layers)Isotropic (full material strength)
Setup Cost$0Toolpath programming time
Cost per Part$0.50-20$10-200+

When to 3D Print

  • Parts with internal cavities or channels
  • Organic, curved, or complex geometries
  • Low volume (1-50 parts)
  • Rapid prototyping and iteration
  • Lightweight, non-critical applications

When to CNC

  • Metal parts required
  • Tight tolerances needed (±0.01mm)
  • High strength in all directions
  • Smooth surface finish required
  • Production of 50+ identical parts
