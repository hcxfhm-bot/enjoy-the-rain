7 Most Common 3D Printing Failures and How to Fix Them

📅 May 19, 2026 ⏱️ 10 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🔧

Every 3D printer owner faces failures. Even after 10 years of printing, you'll still occasionally peel a spaghetti monster off your build plate. The difference between a beginner and an expert isn't avoiding failures — it's knowing how to diagnose and fix them fast.

This guide covers the 7 failures that account for 90% of wasted filament. For each one, you'll get the cause, the fix, and — most importantly — how to prevent it next time.

1. First Layer Adhesion Failure

Symptoms: Print won't stick, corners lift, entire first layer peels up, or print shifts mid-layer.

Why it happens: Your nozzle is too far from the bed, the bed is unlevel, or your bed surface is contaminated with oil or dust.

The fix:

  1. Level your bed properly. Use a piece of paper — you want slight resistance when pulling it between nozzle and bed.
  2. Clean your bed. Wipe with isopropyl alcohol (IPA) and a lint-free cloth. No fingerprints = better adhesion.
  3. Adjust Z-offset. If your first layer looks like a series of round dots instead of a flat squished line, you're too far. If it's so squished you can't see the gap between lines, you're too close.
  4. Use a brim. A 5-10mm brim adds surface area and prevents warping on tricky prints.

Prevention: Run a full calibration routine monthly. Your bed level changes with temperature and use.

2. Stringing (Oozing)

Symptoms: Thin "hairs" of filament connecting different parts of your print. Looks like a spider web on your model.

Why it happens: While the print head moves between sections, molten plastic drips from the nozzle because retraction isn't pulling filament back far enough or fast enough.

The fix:

  1. Increase retraction distance. Start at 5mm for Bowden setups, 2mm for direct drive. Increase in 0.5mm increments.
  2. Increase retraction speed. Faster retraction = less time for oozing. Try 40-60mm/s.
  3. Enable Z-hop. This lifts the nozzle slightly during travel moves, preventing it from dragging through stringy plastic.
  4. Lower print temperature. Hotter plastic = runnier plastic. Drop your temp by 5°C and retest.

Prevention: Different filaments need different retraction settings. PLA is easy; TPU is a nightmare. Create a test profile for each spool.

3. Warping and Curling

Symptoms: Corners lift up, print detaches from bed, or the entire model curves like a potato chip.

Why it happens: Plastic shrinks as it cools. If the bottom layers cool faster than the top, they contract and pull the print off the bed. ABS is notorious for this; PLA rarely warps.

The fix:

  1. Use an enclosure. Keeps ambient temperature stable and prevents drafts.
  2. Turn off part cooling for first layers. Let the bottom layers cool slowly.
  3. Use a heated chamber. Some printers (like Bambu Lab) have this built-in.
  4. Switch to PLA. If you're printing in ABS and fighting warping, ask yourself why. PLA warps 10x less than ABS.

Prevention: Use a brim (again) and make sure your bed is hot enough. 60°C for PLA, 80-100°C for ABS.

4. Layer Shift

Symptoms: Layers are offset from each other, creating a "staircase" effect. Print looks like it shifted to the left or right halfway through.

Why it happens: The print head or bed hit an obstacle, the belt is loose, or a stepper motor skipped steps due to excessive friction or speed.

The fix:

  1. Check for obstructions. Did a stray piece of plastic block the print head? Remove it and restart.
  2. Tighten your belts. They should make a "twang" sound when plucked, like a guitar string. Too loose = layer shift. Too tight = motor strain.
  3. Lower acceleration and jerk settings. Your printer might be moving faster than the motors can handle.
  4. Check pulley grub screws. If the pulley is slipping on the motor shaft, you'll get layer shift. Tighten the set screw.

Prevention: Do a monthly maintenance check. Loose belts are the #1 cause of layer shift.

5. Under-Extrusion

Symptoms: Gaps between layers, thin walls, or the printer "clicking" as the extruder gear slips on the filament.

Why it happens: The extruder can't push enough filament. This could be a clogged nozzle, tangled filament, or incorrect extrusion multiplier.

The fix:

  1. Check for tangles. Make sure your filament unspools smoothly. A single tangle can cause under-extrusion halfway through a print.
  2. Cold pull or atomic pull. Heat your nozzle to 220°C, push filament through, then quickly yank it out when it cools to 90°C. This pulls clogs out with it.
  3. Calibrate your E-steps. Your printer thinks it's pushing 100mm of filament, but it's actually only pushing 90mm. Here's how to calibrate.
  4. Increase flow rate. In your slicer, bump flow from 100% to 105% and see if layers fill in better.

Prevention: Store filament in a dry box. Wet filament bubbles and pops, causing under-extrusion and surface defects.

6. Over-Extrusion

Symptoms: Blobby dimensions, rounded corners instead of sharp edges, or plastic squishing out between layers.

Why it happens: Opposite of under-extrusion — your printer is pushing too much plastic.

The fix:

  1. Calibrate E-steps. Same as above, but in reverse. Make sure your printer pushes exactly as much plastic as it thinks it is.
  2. Lower flow rate. Drop from 100% to 95% in your slicer.
  3. Check nozzle size. If your slicer thinks you have a 0.4mm nozzle but you're actually using 0.6mm, you'll over-extrude.

Prevention: Print a calibration cube and measure it with calipers. If X/Y dimensions are too big, you're over-extruding.

7. Elephant Foot (First Layer Squish)

Symptoms: The bottom layer is wider than the rest of the print, creating a "flared" look like an elephant's foot.

Why it happens: The first layer is squished too hard into the bed to ensure adhesion. This makes the perimeter wider than intended.

The fix:

  1. Enable "Elephant Foot Compensation" in your slicer. Cura, PrusaSlicer, and Bambu Studio all have this. It shrinks the first layer's outline slightly to compensate.
  2. Raise your Z-offset slightly. Less squish = less flare.
  3. Use a chamfer on the model. If you're designing your own parts, add a 45° chamfer to the bottom edge. This hides elephant foot completely.

Prevention: Don't go overboard on first layer squish. You want adhesion, not a pancake.

Troubleshooting Flowchart

When a print fails, don't just restart and hope. Follow this decision tree:

  1. Did the first layer stick? No → Fix bed leveling and Z-offset.
  2. Are there strings? Yes → Adjust retraction settings.
  3. Are layers shifting? Yes → Check belts and pulleys.
  4. Are there gaps in walls? Yes → Calibrate E-steps and check for clogs.
  5. Is the print warping? Yes → Use an enclosure or switch to PLA.

If none of these fix it, check our full troubleshooting guide or ask the community. Every failure is a lesson — document what you tried and what worked.

Final Thoughts

3D printing is 20% printing and 80% troubleshooting. The more failures you work through, the better you'll understand how your machine behaves. Keep a notebook (or a maintenance checklist) and record what works.

And remember: every failed print is just a $2 lesson in what not to do next time.

Want fewer failures? Start with a reliable budget printer and dial in your PLA settings before moving to trickier materials like PETG or ABS.
