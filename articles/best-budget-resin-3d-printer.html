Best Budget Resin 3D Printers Under $200 in 2026: Top Affordable Picks

📅 May 29, 2026 ⏱️ 9 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🏷️

Resin 3D printing has traditionally been the expensive cousin of FDM printing, with quality machines often costing $500 or more. But in 2026, the landscape has shifted dramatically. You can now get a capable resin printer for under $200, and the results are genuinely impressive — whether you're into miniatures, dental models, or highly detailed prototypes.

The key question isn't whether you can spend less than $200 on a resin printer — you can — it's whether that cheap printer will actually deliver a satisfying experience. We've done the research and tested the field to bring you this roundup of the best budget resin 3D printers under $200.

What to Expect From a Budget Resin Printer

Let's be upfront: printers in this price range come with trade-offs. Most use smaller build volumes (typically 120×80×150mm or smaller), mono LCD screens with moderate lifespan, and basic firmware. But here's the good news — the print quality itself is often indistinguishable from printers costing twice as much.

You'll still get the legendary smooth surface finish and intricate detail that resin is known for. The main differences are convenience features and long-term durability of components like the LCD and UV LED array.

Our Top Picks for 2026

1. ELEGOO Mars 3 SE — Best Overall

At around $180, the ELEGOO Mars 3 SE hits a sweet spot that most competitors can't match. It features a 4K mono LCD, a solid build volume of 143×89×175mm, and ELEGOO's refined Mars platform that's been iterated over years. The auto-resin detection, build plate leveling system, and WiFi connectivity make it surprisingly beginner-friendly.

Print quality is excellent — details pop, the surface finish is smooth right off the build plate, and layer lines are minimal even at the default 50-micron layer height. If you're spending $200, this is the one we'd recommend to most people. Learn more about ELEGOO's beginner-friendly approach in our resin printer guide.

2. Creality Halot Mage SE — Best Value for Features

Creality has made huge strides with their Halot series, and the Mage SE at approximately $170 is a feature-packed option. It boasts a 5.96-inch 4K mono LCD, built-in air purifier, and Creality's cloud slicing via WiFi. The 168×120×160mm build volume is one of the roomiest in this price bracket.

The leveling process is semi-automatic and straightforward. The main drawback is firmware can be slightly finicky with third-party resins, so stick with Creality's own resin profiles initially.

3. Anycubic Photon M3 — Best Build Volume Under $200

If you need size more than anything else, the Anycubic Photon M3 delivers a 163×102×180mm build volume — the largest of any printer on this list. Priced around $190, it uses a 7K monochrome LCD (7024×4080 resolution) that rivals printers costing twice as much.

Anycubic's Photon Workshop slicer is polished and easy to use, which matters a lot when you're just starting out. The machine is slightly larger and heavier than the Mars 3 SE, so make sure you have the desk space.

4. ELEGOO Saturn Nano — Most Compact

Not everyone needs a large build volume. The Saturn Nano (around $160) packs a 87×56×150mm chamber into an incredibly compact form factor. It's perfect for miniatures painters, jewelry makers, or anyone who wants a desktop-friendly machine that doesn't dominate their workspace.

Resolution is outstanding thanks to its 4K LCD, and the smaller build area means faster exposure times. Think of it as the "portable studio" option — powerful results in a small footprint.

Key Features to Prioritize at This Price

Mono LCD vs. RGB LCD

Every printer under $200 in 2026 uses a monochrome LCD. That's actually great news — mono screens last 2–3× longer than RGB screens (2000+ hours vs. 500–800 hours), produce sharper prints, and are significantly faster per layer. You don't need to compromise on this.

Build Volume

Bigger isn't always better. A larger volume means more resin used per print and longer exposure times. For miniatures and jewelry, a smaller printer like the Saturn Nano is actually more efficient. For larger models or dental/engineering applications, go with the Photon M3.

Slicer Compatibility

All three brands (ELEGOO, Creality, Anycubic) provide their own slicers, and they're all free. CHITUBOX and Lychee Slicer also work with these machines and offer more advanced hollowing and anti-aliasing options once you're ready to experiment.

Running Costs to Factor In

The printer itself is just the beginning. Budget an additional $40–60 for your first liter of resin, safety gear (nitrile gloves, a respirator mask rated for organic vapors, and isopropyl alcohol for washing), and a proper wash-and-cure station if your kit doesn't include one.

Monthly resin costs depend on how much you print, but expect to spend $20–40 on consumables as a casual hobbyist. The UV LED arrays and LCD screens are the parts most likely to need replacement over time — factor this into your long-term budget.

Our Verdict

If you want the best all-around experience under $200, the ELEGOO Mars 3 SE is the clear winner. It's reliable, well-supported, produces excellent prints, and has a healthy ecosystem of accessories and third-party resins.

If you specifically need a larger build volume, the Anycubic Photon M3 is the standout choice. For miniatures and tabletop gamers, the Saturn Nano's compact size and great resolution make it a natural fit.

The days of needing a $500+ resin printer to get professional-quality results are over. With any of the options on this list, you're getting into resin printing at a genuinely accessible price point — and the prints will speak for themselves.
