How Much Does 3D Printing Really Cost?

📅 May 14, 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
💰

The Real Cost of 3D Printing

Everyone knows a 3D printer costs money to buy. But what about the ongoing costs — the filament, electricity, replacement parts, and your time? If you're deciding whether to get into 3D printing, or trying to justify the purchase to someone who shares your budget, you need the full picture.

This guide breaks down every cost category with real numbers. No guesses, no marketing fluff — just the actual costs based on typical home printing scenarios.

Upfront Cost: The Printer

Your biggest one-time expense. The good news: entry-level printers have never been cheaper or more capable.

Category Price Range Example Who It's For
Budget $100–200 Ender 3 V3 SE First-timers, tinkerers
Mid-range $200–500 Bambu Lab A1 Mini Reliable daily printing
Premium $500–1500+ Prusa MK4, Bambu X1C Serious makers, small business

For recommendations, see our guide to the best budget 3D printers under $200.

Consumable Cost: Filament

This is your recurring expense. Filament is sold by the spool (typically 1kg) and the cost per print depends on how much material the model uses.

Filament Prices per kg (2026)

  • PLA: $15–25/kg (basic colors) | $20–35/kg (premium/specialty)
  • PETG: $18–30/kg
  • TPU: $25–40/kg
  • ABS: $15–25/kg

For brand recommendations, see our best filament brands guide.

Cost per Print

Most hobby prints use 20–200 grams of filament. At $20/kg for PLA:

  • Small print (20g): $0.40
  • Medium print (80g): $1.60
  • Large print (200g): $4.00
  • Full spool project (1kg): $20.00

That's remarkably cheap. Most prints cost less than a cup of coffee. Even a large cosplay helmet at 300g runs about $6 in filament.

Electricity Cost

3D printers run for hours, sometimes days. But the power draw is modest — most FDM printers use 50–200 watts depending on what they're doing (heating uses more, cruising uses less).

Real-World Calculation

Average power consumption for a typical FDM printer: ~100W average

  • 1-hour print: 0.1 kWh → ~$0.01–0.02
  • 8-hour print: 0.8 kWh → ~$0.08–0.15
  • 24-hour print: 2.4 kWh → ~$0.25–0.45

At the US average of $0.12–0.16/kWh, electricity is a negligible cost. Even printing 8 hours a day, 30 days a month adds up to about $3–5/month. Don't let electricity cost scare you away from 3D printing.

Maintenance & Replacement Parts

Printers wear out. Parts need replacing. Here's what to budget annually:

Item Cost Replace Frequency Annual Budget
Nozzle (brass) $2–5 each Every 3–6 months $5–10
PTFE tube $3–8 Every 6–12 months $5–8
Build plate (PEI) $10–25 Every 12–18 months $10–15
Belts $5–15 Every 12–18 months $8–12
Lubricant, cleaning $5–10 Ongoing $5–10

Total annual maintenance budget: $30–55 for a hobby printer. See our maintenance checklist for a complete schedule.

Hidden Costs to Consider

Failed Prints

Beginners waste more filament on failed prints than successful ones. Budget an extra 20–30% for the first few months. After you dial in your printer, waste drops to 5–10%.

Tools and Accessories

You'll need a few things that aren't included with the printer:

  • Spatula/scraper: $5–10 (for removing prints from the bed)
  • Flush cutters: $5–10 (for removing supports)
  • Calipers: $10–20 (for measuring)
  • SD card: $5–10 (if not included)
  • Glue stick / hairspray: $3–5 (bed adhesion aids)

One-time setup cost: ~$30–55

Time

Your time has value. Learning to use a 3D printer takes 10–20 hours. Designing custom models takes time proportional to complexity. Setting up and monitoring prints is time you're not doing something else. For hobbyists, this is part of the fun. For people considering 3D printing for business, factor your hourly rate.

Total Cost of Ownership: First Year

Let's add it all up for a typical first year of 3D printing:

Category Budget Setup Mid-Range Setup
Printer $150 $350
Filament (5 spools/year) $100 $125
Electricity $30 $40
Maintenance & parts $30 $45
Tools & accessories $35 $35
TOTAL (Year 1) ~$345 ~$595
Per year after Year 1 ~$160 ~$210

When Does 3D Printing Save Money?

The printer pays for itself when you'd otherwise buy the things you print. A few examples:

  • Replacement dishwasher clips (8x): Store price $80+ vs. print cost $2 → saves $78
  • Custom desk organizer: Store price $25–40 vs. print cost $3 → saves $22–37
  • GoPro mount set: Store price $30–50 vs. print cost $2 → saves $28–48
  • Board game organizer: Store price $20–35 vs. print cost $4 → saves $16–31

For more money-saving ideas, see our list of 15 actually useful things to 3D print.

Cost per Hour of Printing

For those tracking costs precisely (small business, Etsy shops, etc.):

  • Filament: ~$2.00/hour (at 100g/hour average, $20/kg PLA)
  • Electricity: ~$0.01–0.02/hour
  • Maintenance amortized: ~$0.05/hour
  • Total: ~$2.05–2.10/hour of active printing

Compare this to commercial 3D printing services, which charge $0.10–0.50 per cubic centimeter (often $20–100+ for a single print). A $150 printer breaks even after just 3–5 prints that you'd otherwise outsource.

Conclusion

3D printing is surprisingly affordable once you look past the initial purchase price. The ongoing cost of filament ($0.40–4.00 per print) and electricity (negligible) makes it one of the cheapest hobbies per-hour of engagement. And if you use it to make things you'd otherwise buy, it can pay for itself within the first year.

The real question isn't "can I afford a 3D printer?" — it's "can I afford not to have one?" Once you start solving problems and creating custom items for pennies, you'll wonder how you lived without it.
