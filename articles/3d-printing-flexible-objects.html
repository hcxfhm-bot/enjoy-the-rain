3D Printing Flexible Objects: Hinges, Springs & Living Hinges

📅 June 1, 2026⏱️ 7 min read✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🪃

One of the coolest things about 3D printing is creating objects that flex — even using rigid filament. Living hinges, flex links, and printed springs open up possibilities that injection molding can't match. Here's how to design and print flexible mechanisms.

What Is a Living Hinge?

A living hinge is a thin, flexible section printed as part of a solid object. It bends because the material is thin enough to flex without breaking. Common in printed boxes, clips, and folding mechanisms.

Design Guidelines

FeatureRecommendedNotes
Hinge thickness0.4-0.8 mmToo thick = won't flex, too thin = breaks
Layer orientationParallel to hingeLayers bend, they don't separate
MaterialPETG preferredMore flexible than PLA, more durable
Wall count1 perimeterMultiple perimeters make the hinge too stiff
Lifespan100-1000 cyclesDepends on thickness, material, and bend angle

Flexible Mechanism Types

  • Living hinge — Thin flex line connecting two rigid parts
  • Flex links — Chain of thin sections allowing multi-axis movement
  • Printed springs — Helical or leaf springs printed in one piece
  • Clips and latches — Snap-fit mechanisms with flex tabs
