3D Printing Retraction Settings: How to Eliminate Stringing

📅 May 25, 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🔧

What Is Retraction and Why It Matters

Retraction is one of the most critical settings in 3D printing, yet many beginners overlook it until they encounter stringing — those thin, cobweb-like plastic threads that appear between parts of a print. Understanding and tuning retraction settings can transform your print quality from messy to clean with just a few adjustments.

When your 3D printer moves from one point to another without extruding (called a travel move), molten plastic tends to ooze out of the nozzle due to gravity and pressure. Retraction pulls the filament back before the travel move, relieving pressure and preventing ooze. This simple action is the primary defense against stringing.

Without proper retraction, even the best budget 3D printers will produce stringy, messy prints. The good news? Retraction is entirely tunable in your slicer software, and finding the right settings is a straightforward process.

The Two Main Retraction Settings

Retraction comes down to two primary values: distance and speed. Getting both right is essential for clean prints.

Retraction Distance

This determines how far the filament gets pulled back, measured in millimeters. Too little distance leaves pressure in the nozzle, allowing ooze. Too much distance can cause clogs, filament grinding, and under-extrusion when printing resumes.

Recommended starting points:

  • Direct drive extruders: 0.5–2.0mm (start at 1.0mm)
  • Bowden extruders: 4.0–6.0mm (start at 5.0mm)

The difference comes down to filament path length. In a Bowden setup, filament travels through a PTFE tube that can be 400mm+ long. When retraction happens, the filament needs to be pulled back far enough to relieve pressure at the nozzle, accounting for the "spring" effect of the long tube. Direct drive systems have the extruder right on the print head, so much less retraction is needed.

Retraction Speed

How fast the filament gets pulled back matters just as much as how far. Too slow, and ooze happens during the retraction itself. Too fast, and you risk grinding the filament or causing the extruder to slip.

Recommended retraction speeds:

  • PLA: 40–60 mm/s (45 mm/s is ideal)
  • PETG: 35–50 mm/s (slower due to stickiness)
  • TPU: 20–35 mm/s (gentle handling required)

Retraction Settings by Filament Type

Different materials behave differently when molten, so retraction needs to be adjusted per filament. Here's what works for common materials:

PLA

PLA is the easiest filament to tune for retraction. It has relatively low viscosity when melted and solidifies quickly, making it naturally resistant to stringing. Standard retraction settings work well: 5–6mm distance for Bowden, 1mm for direct drive, at 45 mm/s speed.

PETG

PETG is stickier and more prone to stringing than PLA. It stays molten longer and has higher viscosity. You'll need slightly more retraction distance (6mm Bowden, 1.5mm direct drive) and slower speeds (40 mm/s). Even with perfect settings, PETG may leave some fine strings — this is normal and can be cleaned up with a heat gun or lighter.

TPU

TPU flexible filament requires careful retraction tuning. Being flexible, it can compress and stretch in the extruder, making retraction less effective. Use minimal retraction (2–4mm for Bowden, 0.5–1mm for direct drive) at slow speeds (25 mm/s). Many TPU prints work best with retraction disabled entirely, accepting some stringing in exchange for reliability.

Nylon and ABS

These engineering materials have their own quirks. Nylon is very stringy and benefits from high retraction distances (6–7mm Bowden) combined with drying the filament thoroughly. ABS stringing is less of an issue than layer adhesion, but similar settings to PETG work well.

How to Tune Retraction Settings

Finding the perfect retraction settings requires testing. The most efficient method is using a stringing test model — a series of towers or pillars with gaps between them. This maximizes travel moves and makes stringing obvious.

Step-by-Step Tuning Process

  1. Start with defaults: Use the recommended settings above for your extruder type and filament.
  2. Print a stringing test: Many are available on Thingiverse and Printables. Look for "retraction test" or "stringing test."
  3. Evaluate the results: Look for strings between towers. If strings are present, retraction needs adjustment.
  4. Adjust distance first: Increase by 0.5mm and print again. Continue until stringing stops or under-extrusion appears.
  5. Then adjust speed: If stringing persists, try increasing speed by 5 mm/s increments. If you hear grinding or see dust around the extruder gear, slow down.
  6. Fine-tune temperature: Lower temperatures reduce ooze. Try dropping by 5°C if stringing continues.

Additional Settings That Help

Retraction works best when combined with other travel settings. In Cura and other slicers, these additional options can further reduce stringing:

Combing Mode

Combing routes travel moves over already-printed surfaces when possible, so any ooze lands on the model instead of in open air. Set Combing Mode to "All" for maximum stringing reduction.

Wipe Before Retraction

After retracting, the nozzle wipes across the last printed area, cleaning off any remaining ooze. A wipe distance of 2–4mm works well. This is especially helpful for PETG.

Travel Speed

Faster travel moves mean less time for ooze. Increasing travel speed from 100 to 150 mm/s can noticeably reduce stringing. Just be careful not to go so fast that you cause layer shifts.

Conclusion

Retraction settings are fundamental to achieving clean, string-free 3D prints. The key is understanding your extruder type (Bowden vs. direct drive), your filament material, and how distance and speed interact. Start with the recommended values in this guide, then fine-tune using a stringing test model. With proper retraction dialed in, you'll see a dramatic improvement in print quality — no more wasting time removing cobwebs from your models.
