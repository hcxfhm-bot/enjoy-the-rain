Cura vs PrusaSlicer vs Bambu Studio: Which Slicer Is Best?

📅 May 20, 2026 ⏱️ 10 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
💻

Your slicer turns 3D models into G-code — the instructions your printer follows. The right slicer can cut print times by 30%, improve surface quality, and reduce failed prints. The wrong one just causes headaches.

In 2026, three slicers dominate: Cura, PrusaSlicer, and Bambu Studio. Here's how they compare and which one you should use.

Quick Comparison

Cura (by UltiMaker — free)
Most popular slicer. Massive community, thousands of plugins, works with every printer. The default for Ender 3, CR-10, Anycubic, and most budget printers.

PrusaSlicer (by Prusa — free, open source)
Clean interface, reliable G-code, excellent support for Prusa printers. Growing support for third-party printers via community profiles.

Bambu Studio (by Bambu Lab — free)
Based on PrusaSlicer but optimized for Bambu Lab printers. The easiest slicer for beginners — presets just work. Limited support for non-Bambu printers.

Feature-by-Feature Breakdown

Ease of Use

Bambu Studio wins. Open it, load your model, hit print. The auto-detection figures out the best settings for your specific printer and filament. There's almost nothing to configure for standard prints.

Cura is good but can be overwhelming — hundreds of settings, some of which conflict with each other. PrusaSlicer sits in the middle: cleaner than Cura, more configurable than Bambu Studio.

Print Quality

Tie between all three — with properly tuned settings, all three produce identical output. The differences show up in how easy it is to get good results:

  • Bambu Studio: Good results with zero tuning (if you have a Bambu printer)
  • PrusaSlicer: Good results with minimal tuning (well-designed presets)
  • Cura: Good results after you've learned which settings matter (steeper learning curve)

Speed (Slicing, Not Printing)

PrusaSlicer is fastest at generating G-code, often 2-3x faster than Cura on complex models. Bambu Studio is similarly fast (it's based on PrusaSlicer). Cura has improved in recent versions but still lags behind.

In practice, this only matters for very complex models (100K+ triangles). For most prints, all three slice in under 30 seconds.

Customization and Plugins

Cura wins by a mile. The Cura Marketplace has hundreds of plugins: tree supports, custom G-code scripts, calibration tools, and more. If you want to tweak every aspect of your print, Cura has the deepest settings.

PrusaSlicer has some customization but it's more limited. Bambu Studio is the most locked down — designed to "just work" rather than be endlessly configurable.

Multi-Material and Special Features

Cura supports the widest range of multi-material setups, including MMU (Prusa), AMS (Bambu), and generic dual-extruder setups.

Bambu Studio integrates seamlessly with the AMS (Automatic Material System) for 4-color printing on Bambu Lab printers. If you have an AMS, Bambu Studio is mandatory.

PrusaSlicer has good multi-material support for MMU2/MMU3 but limited support for non-Prusa setups.

Which Slicer Should You Use?

Use Bambu Studio if:

  • You own a Bambu Lab printer
  • You want the simplest experience possible
  • You have an AMS for multi-color printing

Use Cura if:

  • You have an Ender 3, CR-10, Anycubic, or other budget printer
  • You want maximum customization and plugin support
  • You like to experiment with advanced features (adaptive layers, variable infill, experimental supports)
  • You print with many different filament types

Use PrusaSlicer if:

  • You own a Prusa printer (MK3S+, MK4, Mini+)
  • You want a clean, reliable slicer without Cura's complexity
  • You value open-source software

Key Settings to Know (Regardless of Slicer)

No matter which slicer you choose, these settings matter most:

  • Layer height: 0.2mm for standard, 0.12mm for quality, 0.3mm for speed
  • Infill density: 15-20% for most prints. 100% only for functional parts that bear loads
  • Wall line count: 3 walls is stronger than 2 and looks better. Worth the extra 2 minutes
  • Print temperature: Follow your filament manufacturer's recommendation
  • Support settings: See our complete support settings guide
  • Retraction: Start with your slicer defaults, then adjust if you see stringing

Lesser-Known but Good Alternatives

OrcaSlicer: A fork of Bambu Studio that supports more non-Bambu printers. If you like Bambu Studio's interface but don't have a Bambu printer, try this. Actively maintained and gaining popularity.

Simplify3D ($150): Commercial slicer with powerful multi-part layout and support generation. Was the gold standard before free slicers caught up. Only worth it for professional users with specific needs.

SuperSlicer: A fork of PrusaSlicer with extra features (organic supports, pressure advance, arch). For advanced users who find PrusaSlicer too basic.

Final Thoughts

There's no "best" slicer — only the best slicer for your printer and experience level. If you have a Bambu Lab printer, Bambu Studio is the obvious choice. If you have anything else, Cura or PrusaSlicer will serve you well.

The most important thing isn't which slicer you use — it's that you understand your settings and calibrate your printer. A skilled user with Cura will always outperform a beginner with the "smartest" slicer.

Start with your printer manufacturer's recommended slicer, learn the basics, then explore if you outgrow it. Most users never need to switch.
