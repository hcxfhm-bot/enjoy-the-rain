Best 3D Printer Settings for Every Filament: PLA, PETG & TPU

📅 May 19, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
📊

Copying someone else's slicer profile is like using their glasses — it might help, but it's probably not exactly right for your machine, your filament, and your goal. This guide gives you the starting profiles that work on most modern printers, then explains why each setting matters so you can tune from a solid baseline instead of starting blind.

The Universal Starting Point

Before diving into per-filament settings, every profile should start here:

  • Layer height: 0.2mm (the sweet spot of speed vs quality)
  • Wall count: 3 perimeters minimum for structural parts
  • Infill: 15-20% for functional parts, 5-10% for decorative
  • Wall ordering: Outside to Inside (most compatible)
  • Retraction: Enabled, distance 4-8mm (tune per printer)

If your first layer is off, nothing else matters. Read our calibration guide before you start tweaking these settings.

PLA Settings — The Easiest Filament to Print

PLA is forgiving and fast. It's the filament you learn on, and the one you'll return to for 80% of your prints.

Setting Recommended Notes
Nozzle Temperature 195 – 215°C Start at 205°C, adjust
Bed Temperature 55 – 65°C PEI sheet or glass works great
Print Speed 50 – 80 mm/s Slow = better quality on fine details
Cooling Fan 100% from layer 2 Essential for bridging and small parts
Flow Rate 95 – 100% Run a calibration cube to check
Retraction Distance 4 – 6mm (direct drive) Bowden: 5-8mm

PLA tips: If you see elephant's foot (top of walls wider than bottom), lower bed temp by 5°C. If corners are lifting (warping), lower bed temp but increase part cooling. For transparent PLA prints, reduce layer height to 0.12mm and increase nozzle temp to 215-220°C.

PETG Settings — The Balanced Engineering Filament

PETG bridges PLA and ABS — it's strong, slightly flexible, and food-safe with some caveats. But it's also more prone to stringing and needs different cooling settings than PLA.

Setting Recommended Notes
Nozzle Temperature 235 – 250°C Higher than PLA — PETG needs heat
Bed Temperature 70 – 80°C PETG sticks harder than PLA — PEI is ideal
Print Speed 40 – 60 mm/s Too fast = poor layer adhesion
Cooling Fan 30 – 50% from layer 3 Too much cooling = delamination
Flow Rate 95 – 98% PETG over-extrusion is very visible
Retraction Distance 3 – 5mm Too much = hot-end clogs

PETG tips: If PETG sticks too hard to the bed, reduce bed temp to 60°C next time. Use a draft shield or brim for large prints — PETG shrinks slightly and corners can lift. PETG absorbs moisture fast; dry your filament before printing if you hear hissing or pop sounds.

TPU Settings — Printing Flexible Filaments

TPU (Thermoplastic Polyurethane) is durable, flexible, and resistant to chemicals and impacts. But it's also the most demanding filament to print well. A direct drive extruder is strongly recommended.

Setting Recommended Notes
Nozzle Temperature 220 – 245°C Softer TPU (95A) = lower temp
Bed Temperature 45 – 60°C Lower bed = less warping on TPU
Print Speed 15 – 30 mm/s Slow. Very slow. No shortcuts here.
Cooling Fan 50 – 80% Helps set layers before next pass
Flow Rate 100 – 105% TPU compacts under pressure
Retraction Distance 1 – 2mm or OFF Retraction often causes clogs in TPU

TPU tips: If you're using a Bowden extruder and prints are failing, there's no setting fix — upgrade to direct drive. Store TPU in an airtight bag with descant. For very hard TPU (85D shore), you can push speed to 40mm/s. Our full filament guide covers TPU in more detail.

Quick Settings Cheat Sheet

Bookmark this comparison:

  • PLA → 205°C nozzle, 60°C bed, 60mm/s, full fan — fast, easy, forgiving
  • PETG → 245°C nozzle, 75°C bed, 50mm/s, 40% fan — strong, slightly tricky
  • TPU → 235°C nozzle, 50°C bed, 20mm/s, 60% fan, no retraction — slow but rewarding

The most common mistake: printing PETG with PLA settings. If your PETG prints are stringing and delaminating, your nozzle temp is probably too low. Bump it up 10°C and try again. And always — always — dry your filament before printing PETG and TPU. Moisture is the silent killer of print quality.
