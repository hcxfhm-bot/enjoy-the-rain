3D Print Warping: Causes and 5 Simple Solutions

📅 May 8, 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🌡️

What Is Warping?

Warping is when your 3D print lifts off the build plate, usually at the corners. Instead of a flat base, you get a curved, lifted edge that ruins dimensional accuracy and often causes layer separation higher up. In severe cases, the entire print detaches mid-print, resulting in a failed job and wasted filament.

The cause is always thermal. As filament cools, it shrinks. Different materials shrink at different rates, and if the shrinkage happens unevenly — faster at the edges than the center — the part curls upward. This internal stress pulls the print off the bed, starting at the corners where the surface area is smallest.

Understanding why warping happens is the first step to preventing it. Then you can apply the right solution for your specific material and printer setup.

Why Different Materials Warp Differently

Not all filaments are equal when it comes to warping. The shrinkage rate depends on the material's thermal properties and how much it contracts when cooling from extrusion temperature to room temperature.

Warping risk by material:

  • PLA: Low risk. Shrinks about 0.2–0.3%. Warping is rare unless the bed is too cold or there are severe drafts.
  • PETG: Medium risk. Shrinks about 0.3–0.5%. Corners can lift on larger prints, especially without a heated bed.
  • ABS: High risk. Shrinks 0.6–0.8%. Warping is almost guaranteed without an enclosure and controlled cooling.
  • Nylon: Very high risk. Shrinks 1–2%. Requires enclosed, heated chamber for any print larger than a few centimeters.
  • TPU: Low risk. Flexible nature reduces internal stress, so warping is uncommon.

If you're struggling with PETG warping or ABS lifting, you're fighting physics. The good news is that the solutions below work for all materials — they're just more critical for the high-shrinkage ones.

Solution 1: Improve Bed Adhesion

Strong adhesion is your first defense against warping. The better your print sticks to the bed, the harder it is for thermal stress to pull it off. This is especially important at the corners where warping starts.

Adhesion improvements:

  • Clean your bed: Oils and dust reduce adhesion. Wipe with isopropyl alcohol before every print.
  • Use a PEI sheet: Textured PEI holds PLA and PETG without additional aids. For ABS, a PEI sheet heated to 100°C+ works well.
  • Apply adhesion aids: Glue stick, hairspray, or Magigoo create a bonding layer. For ABS, ABS slurry (ABS dissolved in acetone) is extremely effective.
  • Add a brim: A 5–10mm brim increases the surface area at the edges, distributing stress over a larger area. This is one of the simplest and most effective anti-warping techniques.

For more details on getting your first layer to stick, see our guide on first layer adhesion problems.

Solution 2: Optimize Bed Temperature

A heated bed keeps the bottom of your print warm, reducing the temperature difference between the printed layers and the new ones being deposited. This minimizes the thermal stress that causes warping.

Recommended bed temperatures:

  • PLA: 50–60°C (50°C minimum, 60°C for larger prints)
  • PETG: 70–85°C (higher for better adhesion)
  • ABS: 100–110°C (as hot as your bed can go)
  • Nylon: 80–100°C

Don't turn the bed off after the first layer. Keep it at temperature for the entire print. Some slicers have a "cool down" feature after a certain height — disable this for warp-prone materials.

Solution 3: Use an Enclosure

An enclosure is essential for ABS and nylon, and helpful for PETG. By trapping heat around the print, an enclosure keeps the ambient temperature warm (30–50°C for ABS), slowing the cooling process and reducing thermal stress.

Enclosure options:

  • Built-in enclosure: Many printers (like Bambu Lab or Prusa MINI+) come enclosed. If you print ABS regularly, this is the best option.
  • DIY enclosure: A cardboard box, Lack table enclosure, or DIY acrylic box works for occasional ABS prints. Just make sure ventilation is adequate — ABS fumes aren't healthy.
  • Printed parts: For bed slingers like Ender 3, you can print side panels and a top cover to create a partial enclosure.

For PLA, an enclosure is usually unnecessary and can even cause problems if the electronics overheat. Reserve enclosures for materials that need them.

Solution 4: Control Cooling

Part cooling fans accelerate the solidification of filament, which increases thermal stress. For warp-prone materials, reducing or disabling part cooling can help.

Cooling recommendations:

  • PLA: 50–100% cooling. Warping is rarely a fan issue.
  • PETG: 30–50% cooling. Reduce for large flat prints.
  • ABS: 0% cooling. Never use the part cooling fan with ABS unless printing bridges or overhangs.
  • Nylon: 0–20% cooling. Minimal or no fan.

In Cura, you can set "Regular Fan Speed" to 0 for ABS and increase "Initial Fan Speed" delay so the fan doesn't kick in until higher layers. This gives the first layers time to bond before any cooling stress is introduced.

Solution 5: Design for Less Warping

Sometimes the problem is the model itself. Certain shapes are more prone to warping: large flat surfaces, sharp corners, and thin sections cool unevenly and pull off the bed.

Design adjustments:

  • Add rounded corners: Sharp 90° corners concentrate stress. A 2–5mm radius reduces lifting significantly.
  • Use a draft angle: Tilt vertical walls 1–2° inward. This increases the base area and reduces peel forces.
  • Add mouse ears: Small circular pads at corners (printed as part of the first layer) increase adhesion exactly where warping starts.
  • Orient the model: Printing a flat surface vertically instead of horizontally eliminates the warping-prone area on the bed.
  • Split large models: If you're printing a 300mm flat plate, consider printing it in sections and assembling later.

These design choices are especially valuable when printing functional parts where dimensional accuracy matters. A few minutes in CAD can save hours of failed prints.

Quick Reference: Warping Solutions by Material

PLA 55–60°C 50–100% Not needed Bed adhesion
PETG 75–85°C 30–50% Helpful Bed temp + brim
ABS 100–110°C 0% Required Enclosure + ABS slurry
Nylon 80–100°C 0–20% Required Heated chamber
TPU 50–60°C 50–100% Not needed Rarely warps

Conclusion

Warping is a thermal problem with thermal solutions. The faster your print cools, the more it shrinks, and the more likely corners are to lift. Fight this with heat: a warm bed, an enclosure for ABS and nylon, and reduced part cooling. Back it up with strong bed adhesion — a PEI sheet, adhesion aids, and a brim for large prints.

For PLA users, warping should be rare. If you're seeing it, check bed level and temperature first. For PETG and ABS, expect to use more of these techniques together. An Ender 3 V3 SE with a PEI sheet and brim can print PETG with minimal warping. ABS requires an enclosure — there's no workaround for physics.

Apply the right combination of these five solutions for your material, and warping becomes a solved problem rather than a recurring frustration.
