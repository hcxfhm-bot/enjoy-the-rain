3D Print Ironing: What It Is & Best Settings for Smooth Tops

📅 May 16, 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🎨

If you've ever been annoyed by the visible print lines on the top of a 3D print — those tiny ridges where each perimeter and infill line ends — ironing is the fix. It's a slicer feature that "irons" the top layers by moving the nozzle over them with the extruder off, squishing the plastic flat and making the surface feel almost injection-molded.

I've spent weeks tuning ironing settings across PLA, PETG, and TPU. Here's exactly what ironing does, the best settings for each filament type in 2026, and when you should not use it.

What Is Ironing, Exactly?

Ironing (sometimes called "top layer ironing") is a post-processing G-code trick. When the printer finishes the top solid layers of a print, instead of just stopping, it makes additional passes over the top surface with the filament retracted (no new plastic coming out). The hot nozzle tip presses down slightly on the already-printed plastic, smoothing out the ridges.

It's called "ironing" because it works exactly like an iron for clothes — heat + pressure + movement = smooth surface.

Which Slicers Support Ironing?

All major slicers support ironing in 2026:

  • Cura: "Ironing" checkbox in the "Shell" settings.
  • OrcaSlicer / Bambu Studio: "Ironing" in the "Quality" settings panel.
  • PrusaSlicer: "Ironing" in the "Infill" tab (yes, it's oddly placed).

Best Ironing Settings for PLA (Cura / OrcaSlicer)

PLA irons beautifully. It has a relatively low melting point and flows smoothly when warm. Here are my tested settings:

Setting Value
Ironing enabled Yes
Ironing only top layers Yes
Ironing flow 10-15%
Ironing speed 40-60 mm/s
Ironing line spacing 0.1-0.2mm
Ironing nozzle temp correction 0 to -5°C

Ironing flow: This is the most important setting. At 0% flow, no new plastic is extruded (pure ironing). At 10-15%, a tiny amount of plastic is extruded to fill in small gaps. Go above 20% and you'll get blobs on the surface.

Best Ironing Settings for PETG

PETG is trickier. It's more viscous than PLA and tends to string or leave "cobweb" marks if you iron it too hot. The key difference: lower your nozzle temperature by 5-10°C during ironing, or the nozzle will drag strings across the surface.

  • Ironing flow: 5-10% (less than PLA)
  • Ironing speed: 30-50 mm/s (slower than PLA)
  • Nozzle temp correction: -10°C
  • Ironing line spacing: 0.15-0.25mm (wider than PLA)

Even with tuned settings, PETG ironing won't be as glass-smooth as PLA. If top surface quality is critical, consider switching to PLA for that print.

When NOT to Use Ironing

Ironing isn't free — it adds 10-30% more print time, and it's not always worth it. Skip ironing when:

  • Functional parts — If the top surface isn't visible (e.g., a bracket, a box bottom), ironing wastes time.
  • Very small top surfaces — Ironing needs room to accelerate and decelerate. On a 10mm × 10mm top, ironing can make things worse, not better.
  • TPU / flexible filaments — TPU irons poorly. The nozzle drags and deforms the soft top layer. Don't bother.
  • Speeds matter more than quality — If you're iterating fast on a design, skip ironing to cut print time.

Common Ironing Problems & Fixes

Problem: Nozzle is scraping the top layer (rough, messy surface)
Fix: Increase the Z-hop setting during ironing, or reduce ironing flow to 5-10%. The nozzle should just touch the surface, not press into it.

Problem: Blobs or stringing on the top surface
Fix: Reduce ironing flow to 5-10%. Also check that retraction is working properly — stringing during ironing usually means the retraction settings for the rest of the print are insufficient.

Problem: Ironing takes forever (2x+ the print time)
Fix: Increase ironing line spacing to 0.3-0.4mm. The surface will be slightly less smooth but print time drops significantly. Also increase ironing speed to 60-80mm/s.

Problem: Ironing only partially covers the top
Fix: Enable "Ironing only topmost layer" — some slicers default to ironing all solid top layers (which is usually unnecessary). Also check "Ironing line width" — set it to 0.4mm (same as your nozzle).

Does Ironing Wear Nozzles Faster?

A little. The nozzle is dragging across hardened plastic, which causes minor abrasion. With a brass 0.4mm nozzle, I've noticed maybe 5-10% more wear after 5kg of ironed prints vs. non-ironed prints. It's not dramatic, but if you iron every print, a hardened steel nozzle will last longer.

Verdict: Is Ironing Worth Enabling?

For PLA display pieces, cosplay props, and anything where the top surface will be seen — absolutely yes. The difference between "printed look" and "almost molded" is genuinely impressive. For functional parts, skip it and save the time.

To enable it, open your slicer, search for "ironing," and use the PLA settings table above as your starting point. Print a small coaster or name tag as a test, then tweak from there.
