ASA Filament Guide: The Weather-Resistant Alternative to ABS

📅 May 25, 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
☀️

When it comes to 3D printing parts that need to survive outdoors, PLA just won't cut it — it degrades under UV light and becomes brittle. ABS is stronger but still yellows and cracks in sunlight. That's where ASA filament comes in. ASA is the outdoor-loving cousin of ABS, designed specifically to withstand UV radiation, rain, and extreme temperatures without degrading.

If you've been avoiding ASA because you've heard it's difficult to print, this guide will change your mind. With the right settings and environment, ASA is entirely manageable — and the results are worth it. Parts printed with ASA can sit in direct sunlight for years without fading or cracking. That makes it ideal for drone frames, automotive trim, garden fixtures, and outdoor signage.

What Is ASA Filament?

ASA (Acrylonitrile Styrene Acrylate) is a thermoplastic engineered as a UV-resistant replacement for ABS. It shares ABS's excellent mechanical properties — strong, durable, and heat-resistant — but adds a critical advantage: outstanding weatherability.

ASA was developed by BASF in the 1970s specifically for outdoor applications. It resists:

  • UV degradation — won't yellow, crack, or become brittle in sunlight
  • Rain and moisture — excellent water resistance
  • Temperature extremes — performs from -20°C to +80°C
  • Chemical exposure — resists oils, greases, and mild chemicals

ASA Printing Settings — Complete Reference

ASA prints at higher temperatures than PLA but lower than some ABS formulations. Here's what you need:

Temperature Settings

  • Nozzle Temperature: 240–260°C (start at 250°C)
  • Bed Temperature: 90–110°C (textured PEI works great)
  • Chamber Temperature: Ideally 40–50°C+ (heated enclosure helps enormously)

Print Speed & Layer Height

  • Print Speed: 40–60 mm/s
  • Layer Height: 0.2 mm standard, 0.12 mm for high detail
  • Wall Thickness: Minimum 3 perimeters for functional strength

Cooling & Flow

  • Part Cooling Fan: Off or very low (10–20%) for first layers, then 30–50% max
  • Flow Rate: 100–105%

Critical tip: Excessive cooling is the #1 cause of ASA delamination. Keep fans low and maintain a warm build chamber. If you see prints splitting between layers, your fan is too high or your chamber is too cold. Learn more about this in our article on warping causes and solutions.

Best Practices for ASA Printing

Use a Heated Enclosure

ASA loves warmth. An enclosure keeps the ambient temperature stable, dramatically reducing warping and cracking. Even a simple cardboard box over your printer works wonders. For a guide to building one, see our article on 3D printer enclosures.

Bed Adhesion Is Everything

ASA needs a reliable first layer. Options include:

  • Textured PEI sheet — best adhesion, easiest release after cooling
  • PEI smooth sheet — requires glue stick or hairspray
  • Buildtak — works well with moderate adhesion promoter

Allow the bed to cool fully (below 60°C) before removing your print. ASA contracts slightly as it cools, which helps the print release naturally from PEI.

Manage Warping

Warping in ASA is less severe than ABS but still a real risk on large prints. Combat it with:

  • Brim instead of rafts (2–4 mm brim width)
  • Slow down the first layer to 20–25 mm/s
  • Keep your enclosure closed during printing
  • Use a flat, level bed surface

ASA Post-Processing

Sanding & Painting

ASA sands beautifully — use 220, 400, then 600 grit sandpaper for a smooth finish. Unlike ABS, ASA doesn't craze when painted with acetone. You can use:

  • Acrylic paints — works directly on sanded ASA
  • Spray paint — use plastic primer first for best adhesion
  • Primer — fills fine scratches before painting

Gluing ASA Parts

For multi-part prints, ASA can be bonded with:

  • Cyanoacrylate (CA glue) — quick and strong for small parts
  • Plastic cement (Weld-Pilot) — chemically welds ASA parts together
  • Epoxy — best for load-bearing joints

Common ASA Problems & Fixes

Delamination Between Layers

Cause: Too much cooling or too-low chamber temperature.
Fix: Reduce part cooling fan to 30% or lower. Use an enclosure to keep the chamber warm. Increase bed temperature to 100°C.

Warping and Lifting from Bed

Cause: Poor bed adhesion, drafty environment.
Fix: Apply adhesion promoter or use a textured PEI sheet. Add a brim. Keep printer away from open windows and drafts. Check our bed adhesion guide for more.

Stringing

Cause: Temperature too high or retraction settings wrong for ASA.
Fix: Lower nozzle temp by 5°C increments. Increase retraction distance by 1mm. Reduce print speed for stringy materials.

What to Print with ASA

ASA's UV resistance makes it the go-to filament for:

  • Drone frames and propellers — light enough to fly, tough enough to crash
  • Car interior/exterior trim — tolerates heat and UV in a parked car
  • Garden fixtures — plant stakes, signage, irrigation components
  • Outdoor enclosures — camera housings, sensor mounts
  • Prototyping — durable functional parts that need to survive outdoors

Final Thoughts

ASA isn't as easy as PLA, but it's far more approachable than ABS once you have the right setup. A heated bed, an enclosure, and moderate fan settings will get you great results consistently. The payoff — parts that look and function perfectly outdoors for years — is absolutely worth the extra effort.

Start with a small print to dial in your settings, then scale up. And remember: keep that enclosure closed, your fans low, and your bed hot. Do that, and ASA will reward you with the most durable prints you've ever made. ☀️
