Metal 3D Printing for Beginners: Processes, Costs & Applications

📅 June 1, 2026⏱️ 7 min read✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🔩

Metal 3D printing has dropped dramatically in price and is now accessible to small businesses and serious hobbyists. Here's what you need to know to get started.

Metal Printing Processes

ProcessHow It WorksCostResolution
SLM/DMLSLaser melts metal powder$100K+ machine0.05mm
Binder JettingBinder + powder, sintered later$30K+ machine0.1mm
Metal FDMMetal-filled filament, sintered$5K+ (printer + kiln)0.2mm
Cold SprayHigh-velocity metal particles$500K+0.5mm

Metal FDM: The Most Accessible Option

Metal FDM uses filament loaded with 80-90% metal powder (stainless steel, copper, Inconel). You print like normal FDM, then sinter the part in a kiln at 1300°C+. The binder burns off and the metal particles fuse, shrinking the part ~20%.

Suppliers

  • Desktop Metal — Studio System 2 ($5K)
  • BASF Ultrafuse — Filament only, use your own printer + kiln ($200/kg filament)
  • Markforged — Metal X system ($100K+)

Shrinkage Is the Hard Part

All sintered metal parts shrink 15-25%. Your CAD model must be scaled up to compensate, and the shrinkage isn't always uniform. This is the biggest engineering challenge in metal FDM.
