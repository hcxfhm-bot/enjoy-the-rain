Best 3D Printer Filament Brands in 2026 (Tested & Ranked)

📅 May 7, 2026 ⏱️ 9 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🧵

Why Filament Brand Actually Matters

Cheap filament isn't always a bargain. A $15 spool that jams your nozzle, prints with inconsistent diameter, or arrives soaking wet costs you more in failed prints and wasted time than a $25 spool that prints perfectly every time. After testing 15+ brands across thousands of print hours, we've found clear winners — and brands to avoid.

What makes a filament brand "good"? Consistent diameter (1.75mm ±0.02mm), clean winding on the spool, dry packaging with desiccant, accurate color matching between batches, and predictable printing settings that don't require constant tuning. The brands below deliver on these criteria.

Best Overall PLA: Polymaker

Polymaker has been the benchmark for quality PLA for years, and that hasn't changed in 2026. Their PolyLite PLA line offers excellent dimensional accuracy (±0.02mm), consistent color, and reliable printing across the full temperature range. Every spool comes vacuum-sealed with desiccant — a small detail that saves you from moisture headaches.

What we like:

  • Extremely consistent diameter — no nozzle jams from thick spots
  • Wide color selection with accurate batch-to-batch matching
  • Packaging that actually keeps filament dry
  • Good documentation with recommended settings that actually work

Price: ~$20–25/kg for standard colors. Worth it for important prints.

Best for: Functional parts, prototypes, anything where print quality matters more than cost.

Best Budget PLA: Elegoo & Sunlu (Tie)

If you're printing a lot of test parts, draft models, or just learning and don't want to burn through premium filament, Elegoo and Sunlu both offer solid PLA at budget prices. Neither matches Polymaker's consistency, but for the price (~$12–15/kg), they're impressively close.

Elegoo PLA+ is slightly tougher than standard PLA and prints clean at 200–210°C. The spools are well-wound and packaging includes desiccant. Color selection is limited but the basics (white, black, gray, red, blue) are reliable.

Sunlu PLA offers even more colors and frequently runs multi-spool bundles that bring the per-kg price under $12. Quality varies slightly between batches, but we've had fewer than 5% problematic spools across 20+ orders.

Best for: Learning, test prints, large low-stakes prints, anyone printing high volume on a budget.

Best PETG: Overture

PETG is trickier than PLA — it's more sensitive to moisture, stringier, and requires careful temperature tuning. Overture's PETG strikes the best balance of printability, strength, and price we've found. It's less stringy than most competitors and adheres well to PEI beds at 70–75°C.

Why Overture wins:

  • Consistent diameter with minimal spooling issues
  • Less stringing than Prusament or Hatchbox PETG in our tests
  • Good layer adhesion and impact resistance
  • Clear spool lets you see how much filament remains
  • Vacuum-sealed with desiccant — arrives dry

Price: ~$18–22/kg. Slightly more than budget brands, but the reduction in failed prints makes it cheaper in practice.

Settings: 240°C nozzle, 70°C bed, 40–50mm/s print speed. Add drying time if it's been open more than a few days.

Best TPU: Sainsmart & Polymaker PolyFlex

Flexible filament is the hardest to print well. Bed adhesion, retraction, and feeding all require careful tuning. Sainsmart TPU has been our go-to for years — it's soft (95A shore hardness), prints reliably on direct-drive extruders, and doesn't cost a fortune.

Sainsmart TPU (~$20/kg) works well at 220–230°C with a 50°C bed. It's available in basic colors and prints smoothly once you dial in retraction (we use 1mm at 35mm/s on a direct drive).

Polymaker PolyFlex (~$28/kg) is the premium option. Better batch consistency, cleaner packaging, and slightly easier printing. If you're doing production work with flexible parts, the extra cost pays off in reliability.

Important: TPU requires a direct-drive extruder for best results. Bowden setups can print it, but you'll fight under-extrusion and jams. See our TPU guide for beginners for full settings.

Best Specialty Materials: MatterHackers

When you need something beyond PLA/PETG/TPU — carbon fiber nylon, wood-filled PLA, metal-filled filaments, or high-temp materials like PEEK — MatterHackers is the most reliable source we've found. Their Build series covers a wide range of engineering materials with consistent quality.

Standouts:

  • PRO Series PETG — higher clarity and strength than standard PETG
  • Build Series Nylon — prints cleaner than most nylon filaments
  • Quantum PLA — dimensional accuracy rivaling Polymaker at a lower price

MatterHackers also sells budget printers and their customer service is among the best in the industry — they've replaced problematic spools without hassle in our experience.

Best for Color Variety: Hatchbox

If you want options, Hatchbox delivers. They offer 40+ PLA colors, 20+ PETG colors, and specialty lines like wood PLA and metallic finishes. Quality is good — not Polymaker-level, but consistent enough for most work. Prices sit in the middle tier (~$18–22/kg).

What we like:

  • Huge color selection — if you need a specific shade, they probably have it
  • Metallic and wood-filled options that print well
  • Good spool design with clear labeling

What we don't: Packaging is hit-or-miss. Some spools arrive vacuum-sealed, others in simple plastic bags with no desiccant. Check moisture before printing.

Brands to Avoid (Or Approach with Caution)

Not all budget filament is worth the savings. We've had consistent problems with:

  • No-name Amazon brands — wildly inconsistent diameter, poor winding, wet on arrival
  • Extremely cheap spools (<$10/kg) — often recycled or rejected material from other manufacturers
  • Brands with no website or documentation — if you can't find recommended settings, that's a red flag

The filament market is flooded with rebranded bulk material. When a brand has no identity, no support, and no documentation, you're gambling on every spool. Stick with established names.

How We Tested

Every brand on this list has been through our standard test suite:

  • 3 benchmark prints — a calibration cube, a stringing tower, and a 3-hour functional part
  • Diameter check — measured at 10 points across the spool with digital calipers
  • Moisture test — printed straight from packaging, then after 24 hours exposed to ambient humidity
  • Color consistency — compared new spools against previous orders of the same color
  • Failure tracking — noted any jams, tangles, or unprintable sections

We don't accept free filament from manufacturers. Every spool tested was purchased at retail price, same as you'd buy.

Conclusion

For most users, Polymaker PolyLite PLA is the best all-around choice. It's consistent, well-packaged, and prints beautifully. If you're on a tight budget, Elegoo or Sunlu will serve you well for everyday printing. For PETG, Overture is our clear recommendation. And if you need specialty materials, MatterHackers has the selection and quality to justify the price.

One final tip: once you find a brand and color that works for your printer and settings, stick with it. Consistency beats experimentation when you're trying to produce reliable parts. Save the experimentation for new projects, not your filament supply.
