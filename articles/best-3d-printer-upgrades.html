Best 3D Printer Upgrades: 7 Mods That Actually Improve Print Quality

📅 May 19, 2026 ⏱️ 9 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🔧

You just bought a budget 3D printer and you're already eyeing upgrades. The forums are full of people telling you to install dual Z-axis rods, BLTouch, direct drive, and a MicroSwiss hotend — all before you've printed your first Benchy.

Slow down. Most upgrades are a waste of money. This guide covers the 7 upgrades that actually improve print quality, reliability, or speed — and which ones to skip.

1. Bed Surface Upgrade (PEI Sheet) — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Must-Have

Cost: $15-30
Improves: First layer adhesion, part removal, print success rate

If your printer came with a glass bed or (worse) a bare aluminum bed with tape, upgrade to a PEI sheet immediately. PEI (polyetherimide) is a miracle material that parts stick to when hot and pop off cleanly when cool.

Why it's worth it: You'll stop fighting with first layer adhesion. No more hairspray, glue stick, or blue tape. Just wipe with isopropyl alcohol and print.

Best for: Ender 3, Anycubic i3 Mega, Prusa MK3/S/ Mini — any printer with a flat bed.

2. Yellow Bed Springs — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Must-Have

Cost: $8-12
Improves: Bed leveling stability

Stock bed springs are soft and compress over time. Your carefully leveled bed will be unlevel again after 3 prints. Upgrade to stiffer yellow springs (or silicone spacers) and your bed stays level for weeks.

Why it's worth it: Less time leveling = more time printing. Also eliminates first layer failures caused by a shifting bed.

Installation: Unscrew old springs, replace with yellow ones. Tighten knobs firmly. Done in 10 minutes.

3. Dual Z-Axis Rod (Ender 3 Only) — ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Recommended

Cost: $35-50
Improves: Z-axis stability, reduces X-gantry sag

The Ender 3 has a single Z-axis rod on the left side. Over time, the right side of the X-gantry sags, causing layer shift and poor alignment on tall prints.

Why it's worth it: If you're printing tall parts (>100mm) and seeing Z-wobble or misaligned layers, this fixes it. Also lets you print faster without the gantry vibrating.

Skip if: You mostly print flat, wide objects. The stock single Z-rod is fine for prints under 80mm tall.

4. Direct Drive Extruder — ⭐⭐⭐ Nice-to-Have

Cost: $40-80 (or $15 for a printed adapter)
Improves: Retraction performance, TPU printing, multi-material

Bowden setups (extruder far from hotend) have long retraction distances and struggle with flexible filaments like TPU. Direct drive moves the extruder right above the hotend.

Why it's worth it: Retraction goes from 5-7mm (Bowden) to 0.5-1mm (direct). Stringing almost disappears. TPU prints reliably. Color changes are faster.

Skip if: You only print PLA and don't care about TPU. Bowden is fine for 90% of prints.

5. Auto Bed Leveling (BLTouch / CR Touch) — ⭐⭐⭐ Nice-to-Have

Cost: $35-50
Improves: First layer consistency across the bed

A BLTouch or CR Touch is a sensor that probes the bed at multiple points and compensates for an unlevel bed in software. No more manual knobs!

Why it's worth it: If your bed is warped (most are), manual leveling can't fix it. ABL maps the warp and adjusts Z-height in real-time. First layers become idiot-proof.

Skip if: You have a Bambu Lab or Prusa MK4 with built-in auto-leveling. You already have this.

Installation note: Requires firmware update. If you're scared of compiling Marlin, buy a printer that already has ABL.

6. All-Metal Hotend (MicroSwiss or E3D V6) — ⭐⭐ Optional

Cost: $60-90
Improves: Print temperature capability, retraction performance

Stock PTFE-lined hotends max out at ~240°C (PLA/PETG only). An all-metal hotend lets you print ABS, Nylon, Polycarbonate, and other high-temp materials.

Why it's worth it: If you're ready to move beyond PLA and PETG, you need higher temperatures. All-metal also improves retraction because there's no soft PTFE tube to compress.

Skip if: You're happy with PLA and PETG. Don't spend $80 to print materials you'll never use.

7. Raspberry Pi + OctoPrint — ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Recommended

Cost: $50-80 (Raspberry Pi 4B + webcam)
Improves: Remote monitoring, time-lapses, print cancellation

OctoPrint lets you control your printer from a browser, watch a webcam feed, and cancel failed prints remotely. No more sneaking back to the printer to check if it failed.

Why it's worth it: The time-lapses alone are worth it. Also, you can start/stop prints remotely, monitor temperatures, and get push notifications when a print finishes (or fails).

Alternative: Bambu Lab printers have this built-in with their app. For other printers, OctoPrint is the way.

Upgrades to SKIP (Save Your Money)

❌ Dual Extruder (for most people)

Dual extruders let you print two colors or materials, but they're a calibration nightmare. Wait until you've mastered single-color printing. Even then, consider painting your filament instead.

❌ Linear Rails

V-slot wheels are fine. Linear rails are quieter and smoother, but the print quality improvement is marginal. Spend this money on better filament instead.

❌ Enclosure (unless printing ABS)

An enclosure helps with warping and keeps drafts away, but it's overkill for PLA/PETG. If you must have one, build it from IKEA Lack tables and acrylic panels for $30, not $200.

The "Upgrade Trap" (Don't Fall For It)

There's a syndrome in 3D printing called "upgrading instead of printing." You spend more time installing mods and tuning firmware than actually printing. Don't be that person.

Rule of thumb: Only upgrade when your printer's current capability is limiting the prints you want to make. If you can't print TPU, get a direct drive. If your bed won't stay level, get better springs. But if your prints are coming out fine, leave it alone.

Recommended Upgrade Path (By Budget)

$20 budget: PEI sheet + yellow springs. The two upgrades that give the biggest bang for your buck.

$100 budget: Add BLTouch and Raspberry Pi with OctoPrint. Now you have auto-bed leveling and remote monitoring.

$200 budget: Full overhaul — PEI, springs, dual Z-axis, direct drive, and all-metal hotend. Your $200 Ender 3 now performs like a $600 printer.

Final Thoughts

The best upgrade you can make is learning how to calibrate your printer and dial in your settings. No hardware upgrade can fix bad slicer settings or poor filament choices.

Start with the PEI sheet and yellow springs. Those two alone will eliminate 80% of your print failures. Add more upgrades only when you hit a specific limitation that's preventing you from printing what you want.

Pro tip: Join a local 3D printing group and try before you buy. Someone will have the upgrade you're considering — see if it actually makes a difference before spending your money.

Now stop reading about upgrades and go print something!
