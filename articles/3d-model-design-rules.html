10 Design Rules for 3D Printing That Prevent Failed Prints

📅 June 1, 2026⏱️ 8 min read✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
📏

Most 3D printing failures happen before the printer even starts — they're baked into the design. Follow these 10 rules and you'll eliminate the vast majority of print failures.

Rule 1: Minimum Wall Thickness

1.2mm (3 perimeters with 0.4mm nozzle). Thinner walls may not slice correctly or will be too fragile.

Rule 2: 45° Overhang Rule

Any surface angled more than 45° from vertical needs supports. Design overhangs at 45° or less when possible.

Rule 3: Add Fillets to All Internal Corners

Sharp internal corners are stress concentrators and print weaker. A 0.5-1mm fillet significantly improves strength.

Rule 4: Tolerances for Fit

0.2mm clearance for snug fits, 0.4mm for loose fits, 0.6mm+ for sliding parts. Always test with a small sample before committing to a large print.

Rule 5: Chamfer the Bottom Edge

A 0.5mm chamfer on the bottom edge helps the first layer spread evenly and prevents elephant foot.

Rule 6: Avoid Flat Overhangs on Top

Bridge gaps with arcs or teardrop shapes instead of flat ceilings. Flat top surfaces spanning >10mm need support or will sag.

Rule 7: Orient for Strength

Layer lines are weak in tension perpendicular to them. Orient critical stress directions parallel to layer lines.

Rule 8: Use Teardrop Holes for Horizontal Bores

A vertical hole printed horizontally needs support inside. A teardrop shape (point at top) prints without support.

Rule 9: Text on Top Surface Only

Text on vertical walls looks terrible due to layer lines. Put text on horizontal (top) surfaces for best quality.

Rule 10: Think About Removal

Can you actually remove the supports? Can you reach internal surfaces for cleaning? If you can't, redesign for accessibility.
