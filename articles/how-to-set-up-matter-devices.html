How to Set Up Matter Devices with Home Assistant: Step-by-Step Guide

📅 July 23, 2026 ⏱️ 14 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🏠

You've just unboxed a shiny new Matter device — maybe a smart bulb, a contact sensor, or a Thread-enabled thermostat. The box promises "Works with Matter" and "Easy setup." But now you're staring at your Home Assistant dashboard, wondering how to actually get this thing connected.

You're not alone. Matter is the future of smart home connectivity, but "easy setup" is relative. In this guide, I'll walk you through exactly how to set up Matter devices with Home Assistant — whether they're Wi-Fi Matter devices, Thread-based sensors, or Matter over Thread border routers.

Understanding the Matter Setup Requirements

Before diving into the step-by-step process, let's clarify what you need. Setting up Matter devices with Home Assistant requires:

  • Home Assistant — Running on Raspberry Pi, mini PC, NAS, or Docker
  • Home Assistant 2023.11 or later — The Matter server integration is included by default
  • A Matter-compatible device — Wi-Fi or Thread-based Matter certified product
  • Commissioning codes — The QR code or numeric code that comes with your device
  • Optional: Thread border router — Required only for Thread-based Matter devices

If you haven't set up Home Assistant yet, check out our comprehensive Home Assistant Raspberry Pi setup guide for step-by-step instructions from scratch.

Prerequisites: Preparing Your Home Assistant

Let's make sure your Home Assistant is ready for Matter devices before attempting any setup.

Check Your Home Assistant Version

Home Assistant has included Matter support since version 2023.11. To check your version:

  1. Open Home Assistant in your browser
  2. Click on your profile icon (bottom left)
  3. Scroll down to see the Home Assistant version
  4. If you're below 2023.11, update through Settings → System → Updates

Verify the Matter Server Add-On

The Matter server runs as a Home Assistant add-on. To check if it's installed:

  1. Go to Settings → Add-ons
  2. Look for "Matter Server" in the add-on list
  3. If it's not installed, click Add Add-on Store and search for "Matter"
  4. Install the official "Matter Server" add-on by Home Assistant
  5. Start the add-on and enable auto-start

The Matter Server add-on handles all the complex pairing and communication protocols so you don't have to deal with them manually.

Method 1: Setting Up Wi-Fi Matter Devices

Wi-Fi Matter devices are the simplest to set up since they connect directly to your existing network without requiring Thread border routers. Most smart bulbs, plugs, and switches fall into this category.

Step 1: Put Your Device in Pairing Mode

Every Matter device has a specific way to enter pairing mode. Common methods include:

  • Factory reset — Usually power on/off 3-5 times, or hold a button for 10 seconds
  • LED indicators — Look for blinking patterns (often amber or blue)
  • Documentation — Check the manual for the specific pairing sequence

The device's LED will typically blink rapidly when it's in pairing mode and ready to be discovered.

Step 2: Add Matter Integration in Home Assistant

  1. Open Home Assistant and go to Settings → Devices & Services
  2. Click Add Integration
  3. Search for "Matter" and select it
  4. Click Submit to confirm the Matter server is running
  5. Home Assistant will scan your network for Matter devices in pairing mode

Step 3: Commission Your Device

If your device doesn't appear automatically, you'll need to commission it manually using the QR code:

  1. In the Matter integration, click Add Device
  2. A camera window will open — position the device's QR code in frame
  3. Home Assistant will read the code and begin commissioning
  4. Wait 30-60 seconds for the process to complete
  5. The device will appear in your device list with all its entities

If you're using a device without a camera-accessible QR code (some Thread devices), you can enter the numeric code manually. Look for a 11-21 digit number on the device or its packaging.

Step 4: Organize Your Device

After commissioning, assign your device to the appropriate area in Home Assistant:

  1. Click on your new device in the device list
  2. Click the Edit button (pencil icon)
  3. Select the room or area from the dropdown
  4. Give the device a friendly name
  5. Click Update

Method 2: Setting Up Thread-Based Matter Devices

Thread devices offer superior battery life and mesh networking, but they require a Thread border router to communicate with your Matter network. Here's how to set them up.

Understanding Thread Border Routers

A Thread border router bridges your Thread network to your Wi-Fi/Ethernet network. Without one, Thread devices can't communicate with Home Assistant. Compatible Thread border routers include:

  • Apple HomePod mini or HomePod — Most reliable Thread border router for Home Assistant users
  • Apple TV 4K — Also functions as a Thread border router
  • Google Nest Hub (2nd gen) — Thread support varies by firmware version
  • Amazon Echo (4th gen) — Thread border router capabilities expanding

If you don't have any Thread border routers, you'll need at least one to use Thread-based Matter devices. Apple devices tend to be the most reliable for Home Assistant integration.

Step 1: Set Up Your Thread Network

  1. Place your Thread border router device near your Home Assistant server
  2. Ensure your Thread device is powered on and in pairing mode
  3. Wait for the Thread border router to detect and commission the device
  4. The device should now appear in your Thread network

Step 2: Connect Thread to Home Assistant

Home Assistant needs to know about your Thread network to see Thread devices:

  1. Go to Settings → Devices & Services → Matter
  2. Click Configure on the Matter integration
  3. Enable Scan for nearby devices
  4. Home Assistant will discover your Thread border router and Thread devices
  5. Approved devices will be added to your Home Assistant device list

For a deeper understanding of how Thread works with Matter, read our Matter protocol explained guide which covers the technical details of Thread networking.

Method 3: Bridging Existing Devices to Matter

Don't have Matter-native devices? You can bridge your existing Zigbee, Z-Wave, and Wi-Fi devices to appear as Matter devices in Home Assistant.

Using Home Assistant's Built-in Bridges

Home Assistant's Matter integration automatically exposes compatible devices as Matter devices:

  1. Ensure your existing devices are already integrated in Home Assistant
  2. Go to Settings → Devices & Services → Matter
  3. Click Add via BLE or wait for devices to auto-discover
  4. Your existing entities will appear as Matter-compatible devices

Zigbee2MQTT Bridge

If you're using Zigbee2MQTT with Home Assistant, you can expose Zigbee devices as Matter devices:

  1. Install the Zigbee2MQTT Matter bridge add-on
  2. Configure which Zigbee devices to expose
  3. The bridge will create Matter-equivalent devices in Home Assistant

For detailed Zigbee setup instructions, see our Zigbee2MQTT setup guide which covers the complete installation process.

Verifying Your Matter Device Setup

After commissioning, verify everything is working correctly:

Check Device Status

In Home Assistant:

  • Navigate to Settings → Devices & Services
  • Click on your Matter device
  • Verify all entities (switches, sensors, etc.) are present
  • Check the "Last updated" timestamp to confirm connectivity

Test Device Control

Try controlling your device through Home Assistant:

  • Toggle a light on and off
  • Check sensor readings update in real-time
  • Test automations involving the Matter device
  • Verify the device responds within 1-2 seconds

Check Network Diagnostics

If you have issues, Home Assistant provides diagnostic information:

  1. Go to Settings → Devices & Services
  2. Click on the Matter integration
  3. Select 1 device (or however many you have)
  4. Click Diagnose for detailed connection information

Troubleshooting Common Matter Setup Issues

Even with a standard like Matter, things don't always go smoothly. Here are solutions to the most common problems.

Device Not Appearing in Pairing Mode

Problem: Your Matter device is in pairing mode but Home Assistant can't find it.

Solutions:

  • Move the device closer to your Home Assistant server or Thread border router
  • Ensure your Wi-Fi network isn't blocking device discovery (some routers have "IoT isolation")
  • Try factory resetting the device and starting the pairing process again
  • Check that the Matter Server add-on is running without errors
  • Restart Home Assistant and try again

Thread Device Keeps Going Offline

Problem: Thread sensor or device frequently disconnects.

Solutions:

  • Add more Thread border routers to extend mesh coverage
  • Move the device closer to an existing Thread router
  • Check for Wi-Fi interference — Thread operates on 2.4GHz and can be disrupted
  • Update Thread border router firmware to the latest version
  • Replace batteries if the device is battery-powered

Commissioning QR Code Not Scanning

Problem: Home Assistant can't read the Matter QR code.

Solutions:

  • Ensure adequate lighting — the camera needs to see the code clearly
  • Clean the camera lens on your device running Home Assistant
  • Use the manual numeric code entry instead of the QR code
  • Try a different browser if accessing Home Assistant via web interface
  • Some devices have the code printed twice — try the backup code

Device Added But Entities Missing

Problem: The Matter device shows in Home Assistant but has no controllable entities.

Solutions:

  • Wait 30-60 seconds after adding — entities sometimes appear with a delay
  • Check the device diagnostics for entity information
  • The device may require a firmware update before all entities appear
  • Remove and re-commission the device
  • Check if the device manufacturer has limited Matter feature support

Optimizing Your Matter Network

Once your Matter devices are set up, a few optimizations will make your smart home more reliable and responsive.

Organize Devices in Areas

Proper area assignment makes automations and dashboards much easier:

  • Group devices by room (Living Room, Bedroom, Kitchen)
  • Create logical groupings (all lighting, all climate, all security)
  • Use consistent naming conventions across devices

Configure Appropriate Update Intervals

Battery-powered devices benefit from longer update intervals to preserve battery life:

  • Temperature/humidity sensors — 30-60 seconds
  • Motion sensors — 10-30 seconds
  • Contact sensors — on-change only (most efficient)
  • Mains-powered devices — 5-15 seconds for responsive control

Create Helpful Automations

Now that your Matter devices are integrated, create automations to make them useful:

  • Turn on lights when motion is detected after sunset
  • Send notifications when doors/windows open
  • Adjust thermostat based on temperature sensors
  • Control multiple Matter devices with a single button press

For automation inspiration, see our guide on Home Assistant automations for beginners with 10 practical examples.

Using Matter Devices with Multiple Platforms

One of Matter's greatest strengths is cross-platform compatibility. Here's how to use your Matter devices across multiple ecosystems.

Simultaneous Home Assistant and Apple Home

If you have an Apple HomePod as your Thread border router:

  1. Commission the device to Apple Home first
  2. Open Home Assistant and add via the Matter integration
  3. Both platforms will recognize the same device
  4. Control from either Home Assistant or Apple Home app

For more on bridging Apple HomeKit with Home Assistant, check our guide on connecting HomeKit to Home Assistant.

Google Home and Home Assistant

Google Home can also access your Matter devices:

  1. Ensure your Google Home hub supports Matter
  2. Use the Google Home app to scan Matter QR codes
  3. Devices will be available in both Google Home and Home Assistant
  4. Voice control works through Google Assistant

Securing Your Matter Network

Matter includes robust security by default, but you should take additional precautions:

Network Segmentation

Consider placing smart home devices on a separate VLAN:

  • Isolates IoT devices from your main network
  • Protects computers and phones if an IoT device is compromised
  • Many routers support automatic IoT network creation

For detailed network setup, see our guide on smart home network configuration which covers VLANs, firewalls, and security best practices.

Keep Firmware Updated

Matter devices receive security updates through the standard protocol:

  • Check for firmware updates monthly
  • Enable automatic updates where available
  • Replace devices that no longer receive security patches
  • Monitor Home Assistant notifications for Matter update alerts

Expanding Your Matter Setup

Now that you have your first Matter device working, here are ideas for expanding your setup:

Add More Devices

Each new Matter device strengthens your network:

  • Smart bulbs — Start in one room, expand from there
  • Contact sensors — Doors, windows, cabinets
  • Motion sensors — Hallways, bathrooms, closets
  • Temperature sensors — Every room for zoned climate control
  • Smart locks — Front door, back door, garage

Consider Thread Devices for Battery-Powered Needs

If you need many battery-powered sensors, Thread-based Matter devices are ideal:

  • Battery life measured in years, not months
  • Mesh networking extends range without additional hubs
  • Fast response times even on battery power

Create a Comprehensive Dashboard

Use Home Assistant's dashboard capabilities to create a unified control center:

  • Overview of all Matter device statuses
  • Quick-toggle controls for frequently used devices
  • Historical charts for sensor data
  • Automation status and manual triggers

Conclusion

Setting up Matter devices with Home Assistant might seem daunting at first, but once you understand the process, adding new devices becomes second nature. The key points to remember are:

  • Wi-Fi Matter devices connect directly to your network and are commissioned through Home Assistant's Matter integration
  • Thread devices require a Thread border router (Apple HomePod recommended for Home Assistant users)
  • QR codes make commissioning fast, but numeric codes work when scanning fails
  • Organization is essential — proper naming and area assignment make automations much easier
  • Cross-platform compatibility is Matter's killer feature — use it to your advantage

With your Matter devices integrated into Home Assistant, you've built a foundation that will serve your smart home for years to come. Matter is designed to be future-proof, and devices you buy today will remain compatible with the ecosystem as it evolves.
