How to Calibrate E-Steps on Your 3D Printer (Step-by-Step)

📅 May 25, 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🔧

If your 3D prints are consistently underextruded — leaving gaps, weak top layers, or missing walls — then your extruder steps are almost certainly off. E-step calibration is the single most important adjustment you can make for consistent, high-quality prints, and it takes less than 15 minutes.

The good news? You don't need any special tools. Just a ruler, a marker, and a few terminal commands. Once you've calibrated your E-steps, every single print you make will benefit — better dimensional accuracy, stronger parts, and fewer print failures overall.

What Are E-Steps?

E-steps (extruder steps) define how many motor steps the extruder needs to push exactly 1mm of filament through your hotend. Your printer's firmware ships with a default E-step value, but these are rarely accurate for your specific setup.

Why does it matter? If your E-steps are set too low, you'll get under-extrusion — gaps in walls, stringy top layers, and fragile prints. Too high, and you get over-extrusion — blobs, oozing, and layer adhesion problems.

The factory E-step value assumes a certain gear ratio, hobbed bolt sharpness, and filament diameter. Your actual setup differs. That's why calibration is essential — especially after installing a new extruder, changing gears, or switching to a different filament brand.

Before You Start — What You Need

  • A ruler or caliper (caliper preferred)
  • A marker or piece of tape
  • Access to your printer's terminal (via OctoPrint, Pronterface, or LCD menu)
  • Your slicer's input shaft diameter setting (usually 1.75mm for most printers)

Step 1: Mark Your Filament

The classic marker method is quick and accurate enough for most setups:

  1. Heat your hotend to your filament's printing temperature (e.g., 200°C for PLA)
  2. Feed filament through until it extrudes cleanly
  3. Wait for the extruder motor to stop
  4. Mark the filament at the entrance to your extruder housing with a piece of tape or a marker line
  5. Mark a second line exactly 120mm further down the filament

Write the measurement clearly — 120mm is the standard test length because it multiplies small errors, making them easier to measure accurately.

Step 2: Send the E-Step Test Command

Now you'll tell your printer to extrude exactly 100mm of filament. How you do this depends on your setup:

Via OctoPrint or Pronterface (Terminal)

Connect to your printer and send this G-code command:

G91          ; Set relative positioning
G1 E100 F100 ; Extrude 100mm at 100mm/min

Wait for the extrusion to finish completely before measuring.

Via LCD Menu

On many printers (Ender 3, Prusa, etc.), you can find this in:

Prepare → Move Axis → Extruder → Extrude 100mm

Via Pronterface (Printrun)

Download Pronterface, connect your printer, and use the Extrude button — enter 100mm and press it once.

Step 3: Measure the Actual Extrusion

Once the extruder has finished moving, measure the distance between your two marks.

Ideally, the second mark should be exactly at the extruder entrance — meaning 120mm - 100mm = 20mm from where you started.

Record your measurement:

  • If 20mm of filament remains between your marks → E-steps are already correct!
  • If more than 20mm remains → under-extrusion — your E-steps need to increase
  • If less than 20mm remains → over-extrusion — your E-steps need to decrease

Step 4: Calculate Your New E-Step Value

Use this formula:

New E-steps = (Actual length / 100mm) × Current E-steps

Example:

  • Current E-steps: 93 (Ender 3 default)
  • You extruded 100mm but only 88mm came through (12mm between marks instead of 20mm)
  • New E-steps = (100 / 88) × 93 = 105.7

Another example:

  • Current E-steps: 93
  • You extruded 100mm and 23mm came through (meaning too much was extruded)
  • New E-steps = (100 / 103) × 93 = 90.3

Step 5: Update Your E-Steps

Once you have your new value, update your firmware. Choose the method that matches your printer:

Via G-Code (Temporary — resets on power off)

M92 E105.7   ; Set new E-steps (replace with your value)
M500         ; Save to EEPROM

Via LCD Menu

Control → Motion → Esteps → adjust the value → Store Settings

Via Marlin Firmware Configuration (Permanent)

Edit your Marlin Configuration.h file:

#define DEFAULT_AXIS_STEPS_PER_UNIT { 80, 80, 400, 105.7 }

Then recompile and flash your firmware. For guidance on updating firmware, see our firmware update guide.

Step 6: Verify Your Calibration

Repeat the mark-and-extrude test with your new E-steps. You should now measure exactly 20mm remaining between your marks (assuming 120mm start and 100mm extrusion).

If it's within 1mm, you're good. For perfect accuracy, repeat once more.

E-Steps vs Flow Rate — What's the Difference?

Many beginners confuse E-steps with flow rate. Here's the key difference:

E-StepsFlow Rate
What it controlsHow much filament the extruder physically pushesHow much filament the slicer asks for
When to adjustOnce (hardware calibration)Per-material (different filaments)
Set viaFirmware / E-step commandSlicer setting (%)
Example causeGear ratio change, new extruderFilament diameter variance

Calibrate E-steps first. Then adjust flow rate in your slicer for each new filament type. This is covered in detail in our best 3D printer settings guide.

When to Recalibrate E-Steps

You should recalibrate E-steps when:

  • You install a new extruder (Bowden or direct drive)
  • You change your hobbed gear or extruder assembly
  • You switch from PLA to a significantly different filament (e.g., to TPU or PETG)
  • Your prints suddenly start showing consistent under/over-extrusion
  • You upgrade to a different size nozzle (e.g., 0.6mm vs 0.4mm)

Troubleshooting Common E-Step Issues

Under-Extrusion Despite Correct E-Steps

If your E-steps are correct but you're still seeing gaps, the issue is likely:

  • Clogged nozzle — heat your hotend and do a cold pull. See our clogged nozzle guide
  • Filament slipping — check your extruder tension, tighten the gear screw
  • Insufficient retraction — see our retraction settings guide

E-Steps Value Is Way Off (e.g., 200+)

This usually means you've got a dual-extruder E-step setting (which uses a different gear ratio). Make sure you're using the correct E-step value for extruder 1, not extruder 2.

Can't Extrude at All

If your extruder isn't moving filament at all, check for:

  • Loose motor connector
  • Filament jammed in the hotend
  • Extruder gear not engaging (too loose tension)

The Full Calibration Workflow

E-step calibration is the foundation, but for truly great prints you'll want to run through the full calibration process:

  1. E-steps — extruder accuracy (this guide)
  2. Bed leveling — first layer adhesion (see bed leveling methods)
  3. Temperature tower — optimal nozzle temp for your filament
  4. Retraction settings — minimize stringing (see retraction guide)
  5. Flow rate — fine-tune extrusion amount per material

For a complete step-by-step walkthrough, read our article on how to calibrate your 3D printer.

Final Thoughts

E-step calibration is the foundation of every great 3D print. It's a 15-minute investment that pays dividends on every single print you'll ever make. Once your E-steps are dialed in, your slicer settings become meaningful — because the printer is actually extruding what the slicer tells it to.

Don't skip this step. And if you're running a Bowden setup, recalibrate whenever you change tubes or reroute your filament path. Your prints will thank you. 🔧
