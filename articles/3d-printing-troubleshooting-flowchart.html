3D Print Troubleshooting Flowchart: Diagnose Any Problem in 5 Steps

📅 June 1, 2026⏱️ 7 min read✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🔄

When something goes wrong with your print, it's tempting to start changing settings randomly. That usually makes things worse. Instead, follow this systematic diagnostic flowchart.

The 5-Step Diagnostic

Step 1: Is the first layer correct?

90% of print problems start at the first layer. If the first layer has issues, stop and fix it before worrying about anything else.

  • Gaps between lines → Z-offset too high
  • Rough/rippled surface → Z-offset too low
  • Not sticking → Clean bed, increase bed temp, add adhesive

Step 2: Is the extrusion consistent?

If the first layer is fine but upper layers have gaps or bulging:

  • Under-extrusion → Check E-steps, flow rate, partial clog
  • Over-extrusion → Reduce flow rate
  • Inconsistent → Wet filament, loose extruder gear, worn PTFE

Step 3: Are dimensions accurate?

Print a 20mm calibration cube and measure all three axes:

  • X/Y off → Check belt tension and steps/mm
  • Z off → Check Z steps/mm, lead screw pitch
  • Hole sizes wrong → Adjust horizontal expansion in slicer

Step 4: Is the surface quality acceptable?

  • Ringing/ghosting → Reduce acceleration or enable input shaping
  • Stringing → Increase retraction, reduce temperature
  • Z-banding → Check Z-axis hardware, PID tune hotend

Step 5: Are supports and overhangs clean?

  • Drooping overhangs → Reduce overhang speed, increase cooling
  • Supports hard to remove → Increase Z-gap, switch to tree supports
