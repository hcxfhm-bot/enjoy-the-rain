How to Flash Tasmota Firmware on Sonoff Devices: Complete Guide

📅 July 5, 2026 ⏱️ 14 min read ✍️ 3D Print Starter Team
🔧

If you own a Sonoff smart switch or outlet, you've probably noticed it only works with the eWeLink app — and that comes with cloud dependence, data privacy concerns, and limited customization. The good news: you can take full control by flashing Tasmota firmware on your Sonoff device. Once flashed, your device runs locally, responds faster, and integrates seamlessly with Home Assistant and other platforms without any cloud dependency.

In this guide, you'll learn everything you need: what Tasmota is, which Sonoff devices are supported, the flashing methods available, and step-by-step instructions for the most common Sonoff models including the Sonoff Basic, Sonoff Mini, Sonoff POW, and Sonoff 4CH Pro.

What Is Tasmota Firmware?

Tasmota is a free, open-source firmware for ESP8266 and ESP32-based smart devices. It replaces the stock firmware on Sonoff products and gives you:

  • Local control — no cloud, no internet required
  • Web interface — configure via any browser
  • MQTT support — integrates perfectly with Home Assistant and other DIY platforms
  • Energy monitoring — real-time power, voltage, and current readings on supported devices
  • Rule engine — automate behavior without external systems
  • Over-the-air updates — update via web UI or MQTT

Think of it as upgrading your Sonoff from a "dumb smart device" to a fully programmable, local-first smart home component. And unlike proprietary systems, Tasmota has an active community that continuously improves it.

Which Sonoff Devices Support Tasmota?

Almost every Sonoff device built on the ESP8266 chip can run Tasmota. Here's a quick compatibility matrix:

Sonoff Model Chip Energy Monitoring Flash Method
Sonoff Basic R1 / R2 ESP8266 No Header pins / OTA
Sonoff Mini ESP8266 No Switch legs / OTA
Sonoff POW R2 ESP8266 Yes Header pins / OTA
Sonoff Dual R2 ESP8266 No Header pins / OTA
Sonoff TH10/TH16 ESP8266 No (TH16: Yes) Header pins
Sonoff 4CH Pro R3 ESP8266 No Header pins
Sonoff S31 (US) ESP8266 Yes Header pins / OTA

The Sonoff Basic and Sonoff Mini are the most popular starting points — they're inexpensive, widely available, and straightforward to flash.

What You Need Before Flashing

Gather these tools before you begin:

  • A computer (Windows, Mac, or Linux)
  • USB to TTL Serial Adapter (3.3V FTDI recommended) — for header pin flashing
  • Jumper wires — if using serial adapter
  • A small flathead screwdriver — to open the Sonoff case
  • Tasmota firmware binary — download from tasmota.github.io/install
  • TasmoCompiler (optional) — web-based tool at app.compilestack.com for OTA flashing
  • Tasmota device firmware — the specific .bin file for your device model

Method 1: Flash via Serial Header (Recommended for Beginners)

Serial flashing gives you the most reliable results and works for all Sonoff models. Here's how:

Step 1: Open the Sonoff Device

Use a small flathead screwdriver to pry open the Sonoff case. Be gentle — the plastic clips can break. Once open, locate the four programming header pins on the PCB (usually labeled GND, TX, RX, 3V3).

Step 2: Wire the Serial Adapter

Connect your USB to TTL adapter to the Sonoff headers:

  • 3V3 → 3V3 (power — be careful, only power this AFTER all connections are made)
  • GND → GND
  • TX → RX
  • RX → TX

Step 3: Put the Device in Flash Mode

Before powering on, hold down the Sonoff button (or the onboard button). While holding it, plug the USB cable into your computer. The device should now be in flash mode — release the button after 2-3 seconds.

Step 4: Flash Using Tasmota Web Installer

The easiest way to flash Tasmota is through your browser using the official Tasmota Web Installer:

  1. Connect your serial adapter to your computer
  2. Open https://tasmota.github.io/install in Chrome or Edge
  3. Click Connect and select your serial port
  4. Choose your Sonoff model (or select tasmota.bin for generic installation)
  5. Click Install and wait for the upload to complete

If the web installer doesn't work, you can use esptool from the command line:

esptool.py --port COM3 erase_flash
esptool.py --port COM3 write_flash 0x00000 tasmota.bin

Replace COM3 with your actual serial port and tasmota.bin with your device-specific binary.

Method 2: Flash OTA (No Soldering Required)

Over-the-air flashing works for Sonoff devices that already have ESP8266-based firmware and have been previously flashed, or for devices running Sonoff's original eWeLink firmware with a workaround. This method is only viable if your device is already on a compatible intermediate firmware like Sonoff-Tasmota-compatible intermediate build or if you can use the Sonoff DIY mode.

For most people starting fresh, serial flashing is the recommended method. OTA is typically used for updating an already-flashed device.

Initial Tasmota Configuration

After flashing, your Sonoff creates a WiFi access point. Here's how to set it up:

  1. Power on the flashed Sonoff — it will broadcast a WiFi network named Tasmota_XXXXXX-XXXX
  2. Connect your phone/computer to that WiFi network
  3. Open a browser and go to http://192.168.4.1
  4. Enter your home WiFi credentials and click Save
  5. Wait for the device to connect to your network
  6. Find the device's IP address from your router's DHCP table and open it in a browser

On the Tasmota web interface, go to Configuration → Configure Module and select your Sonoff model from the dropdown. Then go to Configuration → Configure WiFi to confirm your settings.

Connecting Tasmota to Home Assistant

Once Tasmota is running, integrating with Home Assistant takes just a few steps via MQTT:

Step 1: Configure MQTT in Tasmota

In the Tasmota web interface, go to Configuration → Configure MQTT and enter:

  • Host: Your MQTT broker IP (e.g., your Home Assistant IP)
  • Port: 1883 (default)
  • Client: Sonoff (or leave auto)
  • Topic: sonoff-living-room (use a unique name per device)
  • User / Password: Your MQTT credentials

Step 2: Add to Home Assistant

Home Assistant will auto-detect your Tasmota device if MQTT discovery is enabled. In Tasmota, go to Configuration → Configure Other and check MQTT and enable discovery. Home Assistant will automatically add your Sonoff as a switch entity.

If auto-discovery doesn't work, you can manually add it to your configuration.yaml:

switch:
  - platform: mqtt
    name: "Living Room Light"
    command_topic: "cmnd/sonoff-living-room/power"
    state_topic: "stat/sonoff-living-room/POWER"
    qos: 1
    payload_on: "ON"
    payload_off: "OFF"
    retain: true

Tasmota Rule Examples

One of Tasmota's most powerful features is its built-in rule engine. You can program logic directly on the device without needing a home automation platform. Here are a few examples:

Auto-Off Timer

Automatically turn off a light 30 minutes after it's turned on:

Rule1 ON Power1#State=1 DO Backlog Delay 1800; Power1 0 ENDON

Power State Recovery

Have the device restore its last state after a power outage:

SetOptions 65 1

PIR Motion Sensor Integration

If you have a Sonoff with a connected PIR sensor, turn on a light automatically:

Rule2 ON Switch1#State=1 DO Power1 TOGGLE ENDON

To learn more about programming Tasmota devices with rules and logic, check out our full guide to Tasmota Rules and Automations.

Sonoff POW: Energy Monitoring

The Sonoff POW series is special because it includes a HLW8012 power monitoring chip that measures voltage, current, and power consumption. After flashing Tasmota, you get real-time energy data in the web interface.

To enable energy monitoring on the Sonoff POW, go to Configuration → Configure Module and select Sonoff POW R2 (or your specific model). The energy data is displayed in the main UI and can also be published via MQTT for logging in Home Assistant.

Combined with Tasmota Energy Monitoring configuration, you can track your appliances' power usage over time and set automation triggers based on wattage thresholds.

Troubleshooting Common Tasmota Flashing Issues

Device Not Entering Flash Mode

If the flashing fails, make sure you're holding the button before and during power-on. Some USB serial adapters don't provide enough power — try using a dedicated 3.3V power source on the 3V3 pin instead of drawing power from USB.

Wrong COM Port Selected

On Windows, check Device Manager to confirm the correct COM port. On Mac/Linux, the port will be something like /dev/ttyUSB0 or /dev/cu.usbserial-XXXX.

Device Won't Connect to WiFi After Flashing

If the Tasmota AP doesn't appear, the flash may have failed. Re-flash using the serial method. If the AP appears but it won't connect to your network, double-check your WiFi password and make sure your router supports 2.4GHz (Tasmota doesn't support 5GHz).

Serial Port Permission Denied (Linux/Mac)

Run: sudo chmod 666 /dev/ttyUSB0 (replace with your port). On Mac, you may need to install drivers for your USB serial adapter.

Tasmota vs ESPHome: Which Should You Choose?

If you're deciding between Tasmota and ESPHome for your Sonoff devices, here's a quick comparison:

  • Tasmota: Pre-compiled binaries, easy flashing, web UI, built-in MQTT, excellent rule engine — best for most users
  • ESPHome: YAML-based configuration, tighter Home Assistant integration, custom sensor support, compile-time optimization — better for advanced users who want maximum control

For most smart home enthusiasts, Tasmota is the easier starting point. If you outgrow it, you can always migrate to ESPHome later.

Safety Warning

⚠️ Important: Working with mains voltage (110V-240V) is dangerous. Always disconnect power before opening a Sonoff device or connecting the serial adapter. Never flash a Sonoff while it's plugged into mains voltage — always use the low-voltage serial connection for flashing. If you're not comfortable working with electricity, consult a licensed electrician.

Final Thoughts

Flashing Tasmota firmware on Sonoff devices is one of the best upgrades you can make for your smart home. It removes cloud dependencies, improves reliability, and gives you complete control over your devices. Whether you want to automate your light switches, monitor energy usage, or integrate everything into HomeKit via Home Assistant, Tasmota makes it possible.

The initial process takes about 15-20 minutes per device, and once it's done, you'll have a local-first smart home that works even when your internet is down. Start with a Sonoff Basic — they're inexpensive and forgiving — and work your way up to more complex devices like the Sonoff POW for energy monitoring.

Have questions or run into issues? The Tasmota community forums and Reddit r/homeassistant are excellent resources for troubleshooting.
