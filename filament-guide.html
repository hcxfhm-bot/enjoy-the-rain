The Complete 3D Printer Filament Guide for Beginners

📅 Updated April 2026 · ⏱️ 8 min read

Choosing the right filament is just as important as choosing the right printer. This guide covers everything you need to know about the 4 main FDM filament types.

FilamentDifficultyNozzle TempBed TempBest For

💡 Beginner Rule: Start with PLA. Master PLA, then move to PETG. Only venture into TPU and ABS when you have specific needs that require them.

PLA (Polylactic Acid) — The Beginner's Best Friend

PLA is by far the most popular 3D printing filament, and for good reason. It's made from renewable resources (corn starch) and is biodegradable.

Why PLA Is Perfect for Beginners

  • Prints at low temperatures (190-220°C)
  • Sticks to almost any bed surface
  • Minimal warping — great for large prints
  • No heated enclosure needed
  • Odorless and non-toxic
  • Available in every color imaginable

PLA Limitations

  • Not heat-resistant — deforms above 55°C
  • Brittle compared to PETG
  • Not UV-resistant for outdoor use

PLA Variants Worth Trying

  • Silk PLA — Shiny, metallic finish. Great for decorative prints
  • PLA+ / PLA Pro — Slightly stronger, better layer adhesion
  • Wood PLA — Contains wood fibers, looks and smells like wood
  • Marble PLA — Stone-like texture, hides layer lines beautifully

PETG — The Upgrade from PLA

PETG (Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol) is the next step up. Think of it as PLA's tougher, more durable cousin.

Why Upgrade to PETG?

  • Stronger and more flexible than PLA
  • Heat-resistant up to 75°C
  • Better for functional/mechanical parts
  • Good layer adhesion

PETG Challenges

  • Stringing — PETG loves to ooze. Dial in retraction settings
  • Bed adhesion — can stick too well to glass beds (use glue stick as release agent)
  • Absorbs moisture faster than PLA

TPU — Flexible Filament

TPU (Thermoplastic Polyurethane) is a rubber-like filament that can bend and flex. Great for phone cases, drone parts, and gaskets.

TPU Tips

  • Works best with direct drive extruders
  • Print slowly — 20-30mm/s max
  • Disable retraction to prevent jams
  • Not recommended as your first filament

ABS — The Professional Choice

ABS (Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene) is tough, heat-resistant, and used in many commercial products. But it's tricky for beginners.

ABS Challenges

  • Requires a heated enclosure (85°C+)
  • Strong fumes — needs ventilation
  • Significant warping without enclosure
  • Poor bed adhesion without proper prep
⚠️ Skip ABS as a beginner. By the time you need ABS, you'll know enough to handle it. PLA and PETG cover 90% of hobbyist needs.

How to Store Your Filament

Filament absorbs moisture from the air. Wet filament causes:

  • Popping / hissing sounds during printing
  • Stringing and blobs on your prints
  • Weak, brittle parts
  • Clogged nozzles
